# Every nontrivial suborbit of a quarter-successor pencil is large

At the first quarter-rate successor, let $R$ be a hypothetical degree-$D$ pencil with three complete fibers in $H=\mu_n$, where $D=268435454$ and $n=2^{30}$. In its geometric monodromy action on the $D$ sheets, every orbit of a sheet stabilizer has size one or at least **9,085,508**. There are at most two singleton orbits and at most 29 other orbits. This holds for the full pencil and, with the same subdegree floor, for every rational inner factor. ## The subgroup estimate on one component

Write $R=V\circ T$. A sheet-stabilizer orbit of size $a$ for $T$ corresponds to one irreducible component $X$ of $T(x)=T(y)$ over the algebraic closure. Use its smooth projective normalization. The degrees of $x$ and $y$ on $X$ are both $a$. Indeed both projections of the corresponding orbit of ordered pairs are onto a transitive set of the same size, so the fibers of either projection have the same size. Above each of the $3D$ subgroup points in the three full fibers, all $a$ choices of the second coordinate belong to that same full fiber. These points of the pair curve are smooth because both coordinates are unramified for $T$. Consequently $X$ has at least $3Da$ places with $x^n=y^n=1$.

A multiplicatively dependent component is the graph of a scaling or reciprocal preserving $T$. Its degree is one. All degree-one components come from deck transformations of $T$, and each preserves $R$. The earlier source-symmetry argument bounds their number by two including the diagonal. For a multiplicatively independent component let $S$ be the zeros and poles of $x,y$ and put $\chi=2g(X)-2+|S|$. Adjunction and the projection degrees give $\chi\le2a^2$. Corvaja–Zannier's Theorem 2 gives $3Da\le(108n^2a^4)^{1/3}$ because $12n^2a^2/p<1$. In particular $a>4$. [Source-symmetry and curve bounds](quarter-split-pencil-pullbacks.md#an-obstruction-for-inner-maps-without-symmetries)

Put $u=x^n$, $v=y^n$, and let $X'$ have function field $\bar{\mathbb F}_p(u,v)$. The extension $\bar{\mathbb F}_p(X)/\bar{\mathbb F}_p(X')$ is Galois. It is generated by the two radicals $x,y$ and all their conjugates are already in this field. Its group embeds in $H^2$ by the scaling action on the two coordinates. The kernel of the projection to the first scaling consists of maps $(x,y)\mapsto(x,\beta y)$. Each satisfies $T(\beta y)=T(x)=T(y)$, and therefore preserves $R$. By the source-symmetry bound this kernel has size at most two. The degree $c$ of the extension is at most $2n$. In particular the two heights on $X'$ are equal to $na/c\ge a/2>2$.

Apply Proposition 2 of Corvaja–Zannier on $X'$ with its parameters $h=8,k=2$. All objects are defined over some finite extension of the prime field. The height condition $10na/c<p$ holds and the exceptional alternative requires $na/c\le2$. The degree coefficient in equation (22) is $(8+3\cdot2)/(8\cdot2+8+2)=7/13$ and the Euler-characteristic coefficient is $25/2$. On pullback the gcd divisor scales in degree by $c$. The zeros and poles upstairs are exactly the inverse images of those downstairs, so logarithmic Riemann–Hurwitz gives $c\chi(X')\le\chi(X)$ for the corresponding punctured curves. Thus
\[
 3Da \le \frac{7}{13}na+\frac{25}{2}\chi.
\]
In particular $\chi\ge\lambda a$ where $\lambda=(78D-14n)/325=5905579876/325$. Together with $\chi\le2a^2$ this gives $a\ge\lceil\lambda/2\rceil=9085508$. Summing subdegrees gives the asserted rank bound. [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2, equation (22)](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/409)

These parameters maximize the resulting lower bound on $\chi/a$ for equal heights in Proposition 2. With positive integers $h,k$ put $N=hk+h+k$. The candidate bound is $2(3D-n(h+3k)/N)/(N-1)$. For $N\ge90$ it is at most $6D/89<\lambda$. For $N\le89$ each parameter is at most 44, and exact enumeration gives the unique maximum at $(8,2)$. A parameter zero provides no positive bound. ## Branch orders of the primitive factor and of the whole pencil

Let $R=V\circ W\circ U$ be the decomposition with $\deg U=h\le2$, $W$ the large primitive stage, $m=\deg W$ and $t=hm$. Let $Y$ be the ordered distinct-pair curve for $W$ and let $E=\sum_b(1-1/b)-2$ be the excess of its geometric inertia orders. The components above $Y$ in $U(x_1)=y_1,U(x_2)=y_2$ are pair components of $W\circ U$ with $U(x_1)\ne U(x_2)$. They are all multiplicatively independent. Their projection degrees sum to $h(m-1)$, and their total logarithmic Euler characteristic is at most $h^2(2g(Y)-2+4(m-1))$. Thus $2g(Y)-2\ge(m-1)(\lambda/h-4)$. Using the variance bound $(2g(Y)-2)(E+2)\le2(m-1)^2E$ gives
\[
  E \ge \frac{2(\lambda-4h)}{2(t+h)-\lambda}.
\]
The denominator is positive at all four possible prefix degrees. [Quadratic pullback and variance proofs](quarter-split-branch-orders.md) · [Lean elimination](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitSuborbits.lean)

For the full cover, let $F$ be the Galois closure of $R$ over its target function field $K$, let $L$ be the function field of the target of $W$ embedded in $F$, and let $M$ be the Galois closure of $W$ over $L$. Then $M\subseteq F$ since $F/L$ is normal. We have $[L:K]=\deg V=D/t$. All ramification is tame since the monodromy embeds in $S_D$ and $p>D$. Write $E_R$ for the analogous excess of $R$. Galois Riemann–Hurwitz on the two closures and ordinary Riemann–Hurwitz from $F$ to $M$ give
\[
 [F:K]E_R=2g(F)-2\ \ge [F:M]\,(2g(M)-2)=[F:M][M:L]E.
\]
Hence $E_R\ge E/(D/t)>0.070063$ in all four cases. In particular **the whole pencil** also excludes every $(2,3,b)$ signature with $b\le10$. The existence of pencils with the remaining signatures is open. The group and curve arguments above are written proofs.
