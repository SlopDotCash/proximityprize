# Proximity Prize thresholds and proof status

Lean proves an exact finite algebraic formula for **both operational thresholds** on every supplied smooth Reed–Solomon code at the four prize rates. The formulas use ranks of linear systems on the evaluation points to find the first failing integer error count. They can be prohibitively expensive to evaluate. Several production inputs have explicit numerical answers, while others still have only certified numerical bounds. [Universal theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean) · [Numerical integration assessment](integration-assessment-2026-11-21.md)

The [human-review manuscript](support-rank-thresholds.tex) proves the two rank criteria, works through four-rate examples at lengths 32, 128 and $2^{30}$, and compares the boundaries with Johnson. It also describes the exact rate-$1/16$ result at the second designated prime and the half-rate ceiling at the first. Build the PDF with `tectonic docs/research/proximity-gap/support-rank-thresholds.tex` from the repository root.

## Statement and endpoints

`ProximityPrize.RankReview.correctForEveryDomain` proves `FullPrizeReview.correctForDomain` for any finite field of size $q$, any supplied smooth domain of length $n>0$, any nonnegative tolerance $ε$, every dimension $k$ realizing rate $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$ or $1/16$, and every positive list width $m$. For every real radius in $[0,1]$, it classifies affine-line MCA security and the worst interleaved closed-ball list size. The sponsor uses $2^{-128}$ as an example tolerance. The companion paper highlights $k\le 2^{40}$ and $q<2^{256}$. Its list challenge asks for the radius and explicitly permits inefficient decoding. [Lean specification](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/FullPrizeContract.lean) · [Sponsor statement](https://proximityprize.org/) · [Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi, §1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680)

The sponsor's literal wording asks for a **largest safe real radius**. Closed Hamming balls are constant between grid points. If the first failing error count is $e>0$, the safe radii are exactly $[0,e/n)$, with supremum $e/n$ and last safe grid count $e-1$. Failure at zero gives an empty safe set. If every count passes, radius one is the attained maximum. At positive dimension and width the radius-one list contains all $q^{km}$ words, so at $ε<1$ the literal list maximum never exists. The operational formula identifies the boundary in each case. [Discrete boundary and endpoint lemmas](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankBoundary.lean) · [MCA maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_MCAGoodRadiusMaximum.lean) · [List maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ListClosedForm.lean)

The capacity radius has $n-k$ errors. MCA is constant from $n-k-1$ errors through radius one, so every finite first failure lies strictly below capacity. At every positive width and tolerance $ε<1$, the list first failure lies exactly one step above capacity if $\binom nk\le\lfloor εq\rfloor$, and at or below capacity otherwise. These tests determine which side of capacity a boundary occupies. The distance of a below-capacity boundary from capacity can still require the rank test. [Capacity comparison theorems](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/CapacityBoundaryReview.lean)

At 128-bit tolerance with $q<2^{256}$, Lean also settles the literal MCA maximum separately at each dimension $0<k<n$. A greatest safe real radius exists exactly when $\binom{n}{k+1}\le\lfloor q/2^{128}\rfloor$, and its value is one. Below that budget the support-rank test gives the operational first failure. [Single-rate field criterion and attained maximum](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointAdjacentFieldReview.lean)

Under the same field-size and tolerance assumptions, the largest possible smooth length for simultaneous radius-one MCA safety and first list failure at minimum distance is **128** at rates $1/2,1/4,1/8$ and **256** at rate $1/16$. The BLS12-381 scalar field realizes every eligible dyadic length up to each ceiling, at every positive interleaving width. Above each ceiling, every such field and smooth domain already fails MCA at $n-k-1$ errors and exceeds the list budget at $n-k$ errors. Earlier failure is possible. [Sharp length ceilings and field realization](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointDyadicCeilings.lean)

## How the formulas determine the answer

At $e$ errors put $t=n-e$, $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ and $L=B+1$. Either challenge fails as soon as one of the following tests succeeds. The tests examine supports and finite-field linear equations. Their inputs contain the actual evaluation points. [Answer definitions and correctness](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean)

| Test | What certifies failure | Work for one error count |
|---|---|---|
| MCA | For each scalar, select exactly $s=\max(t,k+1)$ positions. Solve the folded-agreement equations together. Each scalar's joint-agreement equations must strictly reduce the dimension of that common solution space. | For $1\le B<q$, fix two scalar labels at zero and one. There are $\binom{q-2}{B-1}$ scalar sets and $\binom{n}{s}^L$ support assignments per set. For $B=0$ the sole scalar may be fixed at zero. For $B\ge q$ failure is impossible. |
| List | Choose one support of exactly $t$ sites for each of $L$ list members. Overlap equations force consistency with one center. An inclusion–exclusion sum of powers of $q$, using Vandermonde kernel dimensions and width $m$, is positive exactly when distinct compatible words exist. | $\binom{n}{t}^{L}$ ordered support choices. The general sum has $2^{\binom{L}{2}}$ terms per choice. |

The fixed-size MCA test preserves the original event for each received pair. A folded support on which the rows have no joint explanation contains a size-$(k+1)$ subset on which the second row already fails interpolation. Keeping that subset and padding inside the original support to $s$ preserves failure. An affine change of the scalar parameter preserves the folded-agreement and joint-agreement conditions and places two selected scalars at zero and one. Hence the MCA error at radius one already occurs at $\max(0,n-k-1)$ errors. [Fixed-size and normalized rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ExactSizeMcaSupport.lean)

Lagrange interpolation supplies explicit parity equations on each MCA support. For lists, whenever $\binom{L}{2}\le q$, only the base kernel and one comparison per pair are needed. This condition holds at $ε=2^{-128}$ throughout $q<2^{256}$, at every positive width. The number of support choices can still be enormous. The Lean functions `mcaBoundary` and `listBoundary` compute the first failure from explicit coordinates, field operations and an integer budget. MCA also takes an enumeration of the field. The theorem `correctAtPrizeTolerance` proves that passing the integer quotient of the field order by $2^{128}$ yields the stated canonical security conditions. A separate prime-field program implements the tests and compares them with direct exhaustive decoding on three length-three fixtures over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_5$, including width two, across all four error counts. [MCA rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportRank.lean) · [Explicit parity equations](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportParity.lean) · [List Vandermonde theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportRank.lean) · [One comparison per pair](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportForcedPair.lean) · [Small-field probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_supplied_domain_rank_thresholds.py)

The executable search stops at the first failing error count. For positive widths it switches to the single-pair list test whenever the field order covers the number of pairs. The Lean MCA evaluator generates the free scalar labels from the set with zero and one removed and produces supports directly at their required size. On the order-four subgroup of the field with five elements, a direct enumeration of all 390625 received pairs gives worst bad counts 1, 4, 4, 4 and 4 in order of increasing errors. The independent finite-field rank probe gives first failure at one error for budget three and at zero errors for budget zero. Every Gaussian block omits the interpolation anchors, whose residuals vanish. For each selected label, adding just the second ordinary row block detects joint agreement because its folded row is already present. [Finite-field probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_supplied_domain_rank_thresholds.py) [Gaussian evaluator](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportGaussian.lean) · [Executable checks](../../../ArkLib/Tests/ProximityPrize/GrandChallengeReview.lean)

The evaluator can certify both bounds from integer inputs alone in the large-field range. `RankExecutable.circuitsCovered n k B` certifies MCA safety through radius one if $\binom n{k+1}\le B$. With positive dimension and width and $B<q$, `RankExecutable.packingBoundary n k q m B` returns the first failing list count whenever $\lfloor\binom nk/(k+1)\rfloor\le B$. It returns $n-k+1$ if the full binomial fits the budget and $n-k$ otherwise. Both checks stop multiplying as soon as the relevant cap is reached. On the length-128, 246-bit example these checks compute both answers at rates $1/4$, $1/8$ and $1/16$ without enumerating any field elements. [Executable and correctness proofs](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankExecutable.lean)

For every code with two parity checks, the complete MCA curve has two values. If the length is $n=k+2$ and the field order is $q$, the worst probability equals $1/q$ below radius $1/n$ and $n/q$ from radius $1/n$ onward, at every positive interleaving width. Each circuit omits one site. The $n$ sums of those circuits differ by the omitted evaluation value, so the subset-sum witness attains the circuit count. This includes every length-four half-rate prize code. The certified evaluator returns the boundary immediately for these dimensions. It returns zero errors for budget zero, one error for budgets from one through $n-1$, and all-safe for budgets of at least $n$. [Two-parity-check theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/TwoParityMca.lean)

**A closed answer in sufficiently large fields.** Write $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ with $ε<1$. If $B\ge\max\{\binom{n}{k+1},\binom nk\}$, every MCA radius through one is safe and the first failing list count is $n-k+1$ for every positive width. This gives the value on the supplied code from $n,k,B$ alone. At 128-bit tolerance the single condition $q\ge2^{128}\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ is necessary and sufficient for this combination of answers at all eligible rates on a positive even length. At half rate, the worst list at $n-k$ errors has exactly the central binomial number of words, which gives necessity. For longer domains this condition can exceed the field-size range of the companion paper. [Joint theorem and adjacent list case](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointLargeFieldReview.lean) · [Exact field-size criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointLargeFieldCriterion.lean)

For these same codes with positive dimension and $0<B<q$, the first failing list count is **2** when $B<\binom n2$ and **3** otherwise. Before two errors the list has at most one word. At two errors its exact maximum is $\binom n2$, for every positive interleaving width. At budget zero both challenges already fail at zero errors. [Joint proof at 128-bit tolerance](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/TwoParityPrize.lean)

## Prize submission

The sponsor describes the statement as preliminary. Its guidelines call for acceptance at a reputable field-appropriate conference or journal, a publicly archived academic version, and an emailed PDF explaining the result and its relationship to prior work. AI-assisted work requires human verification and editing. The judges assess significance and eligibility. The repository records the formal result, while numerical production evaluations, peer review and prize adjudication have their own evidence requirements.
