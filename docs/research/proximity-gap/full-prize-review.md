# Proximity Prize thresholds and proof status

Lean proves an exact finite algebraic formula for **both operational thresholds** on every supplied smooth Reed–Solomon code at the four prize rates. The formulas use ranks of linear systems on the evaluation points to find the first failing integer error count. They can be prohibitively expensive to evaluate. Several production inputs have explicit numerical answers, while others still have only certified numerical bounds. [Universal theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean) · [Numerical integration assessment](integration-assessment-2026-11-21.md)

## Statement and endpoints

`ProximityPrize.RankReview.correctForEveryDomain` proves `FullPrizeReview.correctForDomain` for any finite field of size $q$, any supplied smooth domain of length $n>0$, any nonnegative tolerance $ε$, every dimension $k$ realizing rate $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$ or $1/16$, and every positive list width $m$. For every real radius in $[0,1]$, it classifies affine-line MCA security and the worst interleaved closed-ball list size. The sponsor uses $2^{-128}$ as an example tolerance. The companion paper highlights $k\le 2^{40}$ and $q<2^{256}$. Its list challenge asks for the radius and explicitly permits inefficient decoding. [Lean specification](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/FullPrizeContract.lean) · [Sponsor statement](https://proximityprize.org/) · [Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi, §1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680)

The sponsor's literal wording asks for a **largest safe real radius**. Closed Hamming balls are constant between grid points. If the first failing error count is $e>0$, the safe radii are exactly $[0,e/n)$, with supremum $e/n$ and last safe grid count $e-1$. Failure at zero gives an empty safe set. If every count passes, radius one is the attained maximum. At positive dimension and width the radius-one list contains all $q^{km}$ words, so at $ε<1$ the literal list maximum never exists. The operational formula identifies the boundary in each case. [Discrete boundary and endpoint lemmas](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankBoundary.lean) · [MCA maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_MCAGoodRadiusMaximum.lean) · [List maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ListClosedForm.lean)

## How the formulas determine the answer

At $e$ errors put $t=n-e$, $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ and $L=B+1$. Either challenge fails as soon as one of the following tests succeeds. The tests examine supports and finite-field linear equations. Their inputs contain the actual evaluation points. [Answer definitions and correctness](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean)

| Test | What certifies failure | Work for one error count |
|---|---|---|
| MCA | Choose $L$ different scalars and one support of at least $t$ sites for each. Solve all folded-agreement equations together. For each scalar, adding the joint-agreement equations for its support must strictly reduce the dimension. Lean proves that one received pair then witnesses all $L$ bad scalars. | $\binom{q}{L}$ scalar sets and up to $(\sum_{j=t}^n\binom{n}{j})^L$ support assignments per set. Each assignment uses a base dimension and $L$ comparisons. |
| List | Choose one support of exactly $t$ sites for each of $L$ list members. Overlap equations force consistency with one center. An inclusion–exclusion sum of powers of $q$, using Vandermonde kernel dimensions and width $m$, is positive exactly when distinct compatible words exist. | $\binom{n}{t}^{L}$ ordered support choices. The general sum has $2^{\binom{L}{2}}$ terms per choice. |

Lagrange interpolation supplies explicit parity equations on each MCA support. For lists, whenever $\binom{L}{2}\le q$, only the base kernel and one comparison per pair are needed. This condition holds at $ε=2^{-128}$ throughout $q<2^{256}$, at every positive width. The number of support choices can still be enormous. The Lean answer definitions are noncomputable specifications of finite rank formulas. A separate prime-field program implements the tests and compares them with direct exhaustive decoding on three length-three fixtures over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_5$, including width two, across all four error counts. [MCA rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportRank.lean) · [Explicit parity equations](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportParity.lean) · [List Vandermonde theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportRank.lean) · [One comparison per pair](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportForcedPair.lean) · [Small-field probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_supplied_domain_rank_thresholds.py)

## Numerical results at 128-bit security

Over the prime field of order $65\cdot2^{239}+1$, `RankKnownDomain.numericalBoundaries` proves that the universal formulas give the table's length-128 values at every positive width. The proof uses the independently established numerical thresholds and uniqueness of the operational boundary. For length $2^{30}$ over the **same field**, Lean certifies the safe and failing MCA radii shown. The first failure lies strictly above the safe radius and at or below the failing radius. **List budgets with a short numerical formula.** On every injective domain, for $1\le k\le n$ and positive width, the worst list at $n-k$ errors has exactly $C=\binom{n}{k}$ words. If $\lfloor C/(k+1)\rfloor\le B<C$ and $B<q$, the first failure is $n-k$. If $C\le B<q$, it is $n-k+1$. For other budgets the rank test still applies. Further results include first failures of 94 at length 128 and quarter rate over a degree-27 extension, and 268435457 at length $2^{30}$ and half rate on a field with budget two. At every length through $2^{128}$ the scalar list cutoff transfers to every positive width over every finite field. Other results explain why the field and particular domain matter. Prime-field sequences have different MCA behaviors on the two sides of the entropy threshold, and the critical exponent itself permits different outcomes. At $n=k+2e$, the unconditional extremum over domain and received pair is sharp, while attainment on a specified smooth domain requires further information about that domain. Character cancellation remains an open input to one route to shorter production bounds. ## Prize submission

The sponsor describes the statement as preliminary. Its guidelines call for acceptance at a reputable field-appropriate conference or journal, a publicly archived academic version, and an emailed PDF explaining the result and its relationship to prior work. AI-assisted work requires human verification and editing. The judges assess significance and eligibility. The repository records the formal result, while numerical production evaluations, peer review and prize adjudication have their own evidence requirements. [Submission guidelines and FAQ](https://proximityprize.org/)
