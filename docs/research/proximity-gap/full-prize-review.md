# Proximity Prize thresholds and proof status

Lean proves an exact finite algebraic formula for **both operational thresholds** on every supplied smooth Reed–Solomon code at the four prize rates. The formulas use ranks of linear systems on the evaluation points to find the first failing integer error count. They can be prohibitively expensive to evaluate. Several production inputs have explicit numerical answers, while others still have only certified numerical bounds. [Universal theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean) · [Numerical integration assessment](integration-assessment-2026-11-21.md)

The [human-review manuscript](support-rank-thresholds.tex) proves the two rank criteria, works through four-rate examples at lengths 32, 128 and $2^{30}$, and compares the boundaries with Johnson. It also describes the exact rate-$1/16$ result at the second designated prime and the half-rate ceiling at the first. Build the PDF with `tectonic docs/research/proximity-gap/support-rank-thresholds.tex` from the repository root.

The sponsor gives the field and smooth evaluation domain as inputs. The two designated length-$2^{30}$ primes below are repository benchmarks. The finite formula covers them, while their concrete threshold values measure how much the proof says about cryptographic parameters without running the full search.

At the two designated benchmark fields, the first prime has exact MCA first failures of **536,870,912** at rates $1/8$ and $1/16$. The second prime has an exact MCA first failure of **715,827,882** at rate $1/16$. The other **five MCA** and **eight list** integers remain open. Determining one requires either evaluating its rank criterion or proving safety through some $e-1$ and exhibiting failure at $e$. For example, the first prime's quarter-rate case already has an exact polynomial test for its first unresolved count. At both first-prime half- and quarter-rate candidates, a source Möbius map cannot carry one required fiber to the second and then to the third. Existence without this symmetry remains open. ## Statement and endpoints

`ProximityPrize.RankReview.correctForEveryDomain` proves `FullPrizeReview.correctForDomain` for any finite field of size $q$, any supplied smooth domain of length $n>0$, any nonnegative tolerance $ε$, every dimension $k$ realizing rate $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$ or $1/16$, and every positive list width $m$. For every real radius in $[0,1]$, it classifies affine-line MCA security and the worst interleaved closed-ball list size. The sponsor uses $2^{-128}$ as an example tolerance. The companion paper highlights $k\le 2^{40}$ and $q<2^{256}$. Its list challenge asks for the radius and explicitly permits inefficient decoding. [Lean specification](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/FullPrizeContract.lean) · [Sponsor statement](https://proximityprize.org/) · [Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi, §1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680)

The sponsor asks for a **largest safe real radius**, assuming the field is large enough for that maximum to exist. With closed Hamming balls, this existence assumption holds exactly when radius one is safe. The paper's Definition 4.3 writes the MCA support inequality for interior radii. Lean uses that same inequality at zero and one, as required by the challenge's closed interval. Closed Hamming balls are constant between grid points. If the first failing error count is $e>0$, the safe radii are exactly $[0,e/n)$, with supremum $e/n$ and last safe grid count $e-1$. Failure at zero gives an empty safe set. If every count passes, radius one is the attained maximum. At positive dimension and width the radius-one list contains all $q^{km}$ words, so at $ε<1$ the literal list maximum never exists. The operational formula identifies the boundary in each case. [Discrete boundary and endpoint lemmas](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankBoundary.lean) · [MCA maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_MCAGoodRadiusMaximum.lean) · [List maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ListClosedForm.lean)

The sponsor calls the relative minimum distance the **capacity radius**. Its error count is $n-k+1$. MCA is constant from $n-k-1$ errors through radius one, so every finite first failure lies at least two errors below capacity. At every positive width and tolerance $ε<1$, the list first failure equals capacity exactly when $\binom nk\le\lfloor εq\rfloor$. Otherwise it falls at least one error short. Determining how much farther below capacity an individual code lies can still require the rank test. [Companion paper, p. 5](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680) · [Capacity comparison theorems](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/CapacityBoundaryReview.lean)

At 128-bit tolerance with $q<2^{256}$, Lean also settles the literal MCA maximum separately at each dimension $0<k<n$. A greatest safe real radius exists exactly when $\binom{n}{k+1}\le\lfloor q/2^{128}\rfloor$, and its value is one. Below that budget the support-rank test gives the operational first failure. [Single-rate field criterion and attained maximum](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointAdjacentFieldReview.lean)

Under the same field-size and tolerance assumptions, the largest possible smooth length for simultaneous radius-one MCA safety and first list failure at minimum distance is **128** at rates $1/2,1/4,1/8$ and **256** at rate $1/16$. The BLS12-381 scalar field realizes every eligible dyadic length up to each ceiling, at every positive interleaving width. Above each ceiling, every such field and smooth domain already fails MCA at $n-k-1$ errors and exceeds the list budget at $n-k$ errors. Earlier failure is possible. [Sharp length ceilings and field realization](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointDyadicCeilings.lean)

For an arbitrary supplied tolerance $ε$, write $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ and $N=\binom{n}{k+1}$. When $\binom{B+1}{2}\le q$ (in particular, when $B^2<q$), MCA is safe at the sponsor's capacity radius exactly when $N\le B$. In that case every radius through one is safe. Otherwise a first failure occurs at or before $n-k-1$ errors and there is no largest safe real radius. Without any restriction on the field or tolerance, radius-one safety still requires $Nq\le B(q+N-1)$. [Arbitrary-tolerance circuit criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ToleranceCircuitCriterion.lean)

## How the formulas determine the answer

At $e$ errors put $t=n-e$, $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ and $L=B+1$. Either challenge fails as soon as one of the following tests succeeds. The tests examine supports and finite-field linear equations. Their inputs contain the actual evaluation points. [Answer definitions and correctness](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean)

| Test | What certifies failure | Work for one error count |
|---|---|---|
| MCA | For each scalar, select exactly $s=\max(t,k+1)$ positions. Solve the folded-agreement equations together. Each scalar's joint-agreement equations must strictly reduce the dimension of that common solution space. | For $1\le B<q$, fix two scalar labels at zero and one. There are $\binom{q-2}{B-1}$ scalar sets and $\binom{n}{s}^L$ support assignments per set. For $B=0$ the sole scalar may be fixed at zero. For $B\ge q$ failure is impossible. |
| List | Choose one support of exactly $t$ sites for each of $L$ list members. Overlap equations force consistency with one center. An inclusion–exclusion sum of powers of $q$, using Vandermonde kernel dimensions and width $m$, is positive exactly when distinct compatible words exist. | $\binom{n}{t}^{L}$ ordered support choices. The general sum has $2^{\binom{L}{2}}$ terms per choice. |

The fixed-size MCA test preserves the original event for each received pair. A folded support on which the rows have no joint explanation contains a size-$(k+1)$ subset on which the second row already fails interpolation. Keeping that subset and padding inside the original support to $s$ preserves failure. An affine change of the scalar parameter preserves the folded-agreement and joint-agreement conditions and places two selected scalars at zero and one. Hence the MCA error at radius one already occurs at $\max(0,n-k-1)$ errors. [Fixed-size and normalized rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ExactSizeMcaSupport.lean)

Lagrange interpolation supplies explicit parity equations on each MCA support. For lists, whenever $\binom{L}{2}\le q$, only the base kernel and one comparison per pair are needed. This condition holds at $ε=2^{-128}$ throughout $q<2^{256}$, at every positive width. The number of support choices can still be enormous. The Lean functions `mcaBoundaryWith` and `smoothListBoundary` compute the first failure from explicit coordinates, field operations and an integer budget. MCA also takes an enumeration of the field. The theorem `smoothCorrectAtPrizeTolerance` proves that passing the integer quotient of the field order by $2^{128}$ yields the stated canonical security conditions. A separate prime-field program implements the tests and compares them with direct exhaustive decoding on three length-three fixtures over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_5$, including width two, across all four error counts. [MCA rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportRank.lean) · [Explicit parity equations](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportParity.lean) · [List Vandermonde theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportRank.lean) · [One comparison per pair](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportForcedPair.lean) · [Small-field probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_supplied_domain_rank_thresholds.py)
