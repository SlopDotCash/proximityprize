# Proximity Prize thresholds and proof status

Lean proves an exact finite algebraic formula for **both operational thresholds** on every supplied smooth Reed–Solomon code at the four prize rates. The formulas use ranks of linear systems on the evaluation points to find the first failing integer error count. They can be prohibitively expensive to evaluate. Several production inputs have explicit numerical answers, while others still have only certified numerical bounds. [Universal theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean) · [Numerical integration assessment](integration-assessment-2026-11-21.md)

The [human-review manuscript](support-rank-thresholds.tex) gives proofs of the two rank tests, the four-rate benchmarks at lengths 128 and $2^{30}$, their comparisons with Johnson and the first designated prime’s half-rate ceiling of 313174699 errors. Build the PDF with `tectonic docs/research/proximity-gap/support-rank-thresholds.tex` from the repository root.

## Statement and endpoints

`ProximityPrize.RankReview.correctForEveryDomain` proves `FullPrizeReview.correctForDomain` for any finite field of size $q$, any supplied smooth domain of length $n>0$, any nonnegative tolerance $ε$, every dimension $k$ realizing rate $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$ or $1/16$, and every positive list width $m$. For every real radius in $[0,1]$, it classifies affine-line MCA security and the worst interleaved closed-ball list size. The sponsor uses $2^{-128}$ as an example tolerance. The companion paper highlights $k\le 2^{40}$ and $q<2^{256}$. Its list challenge asks for the radius and explicitly permits inefficient decoding. [Lean specification](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/FullPrizeContract.lean) · [Sponsor statement](https://proximityprize.org/) · [Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi, §1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680)

The sponsor's literal wording asks for a **largest safe real radius**. Closed Hamming balls are constant between grid points. If the first failing error count is $e>0$, the safe radii are exactly $[0,e/n)$, with supremum $e/n$ and last safe grid count $e-1$. Failure at zero gives an empty safe set. If every count passes, radius one is the attained maximum. At positive dimension and width the radius-one list contains all $q^{km}$ words, so at $ε<1$ the literal list maximum never exists. The operational formula identifies the boundary in each case. [Discrete boundary and endpoint lemmas](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankBoundary.lean) · [MCA maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_MCAGoodRadiusMaximum.lean) · [List maximum criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ListClosedForm.lean)

## How the formulas determine the answer

At $e$ errors put $t=n-e$, $B=\lfloor εq\rfloor$ and $L=B+1$. Either challenge fails as soon as one of the following tests succeeds. The tests examine supports and finite-field linear equations. Their inputs contain the actual evaluation points. [Answer definitions and correctness](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankReview.lean)

| Test | What certifies failure | Work for one error count |
|---|---|---|
| MCA | For each scalar, select exactly $s=\max(t,k+1)$ positions. Solve the folded-agreement equations together. Each scalar's joint-agreement equations must strictly reduce the dimension of that common solution space. | For $1\le B<q$, fix two scalar labels at zero and one. There are $\binom{q-2}{B-1}$ scalar sets and $\binom{n}{s}^L$ support assignments per set. For $B=0$ the sole scalar may be fixed at zero. For $B\ge q$ failure is impossible. |
| List | Choose one support of exactly $t$ sites for each of $L$ list members. Overlap equations force consistency with one center. An inclusion–exclusion sum of powers of $q$, using Vandermonde kernel dimensions and width $m$, is positive exactly when distinct compatible words exist. | $\binom{n}{t}^{L}$ ordered support choices. The general sum has $2^{\binom{L}{2}}$ terms per choice. |

The fixed-size MCA test preserves the original event for each received pair. A folded support on which the rows have no joint explanation contains a size-$(k+1)$ subset on which the second row already fails interpolation. Keeping that subset and padding inside the original support to $s$ preserves failure. An affine change of the scalar parameter preserves the folded-agreement and joint-agreement conditions and places two selected scalars at zero and one. Hence the MCA error at radius one already occurs at $\max(0,n-k-1)$ errors. [Fixed-size and normalized rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ExactSizeMcaSupport.lean)

Lagrange interpolation supplies explicit parity equations on each MCA support. For lists, whenever $\binom{L}{2}\le q$, only the base kernel and one comparison per pair are needed. This condition holds at $ε=2^{-128}$ throughout $q<2^{256}$, at every positive width. The number of support choices can still be enormous. The Lean functions `mcaBoundary` and `listBoundary` compute the first failure from explicit coordinates, field operations and an integer budget. MCA also takes an enumeration of the field. The theorem `correctAtPrizeTolerance` proves that passing the integer quotient of the field order by $2^{128}$ yields the stated canonical security conditions. A separate prime-field program implements the tests and compares them with direct exhaustive decoding on three length-three fixtures over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_5$, including width two, across all four error counts. [MCA rank theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportRank.lean) · [Explicit parity equations](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SimultaneousSupportParity.lean) · [List Vandermonde theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportRank.lean) · [One comparison per pair](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ListSupportForcedPair.lean) · [Small-field probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_supplied_domain_rank_thresholds.py)

The executable search stops at the first failing error count. For positive widths it switches to the single-pair list test whenever the field order covers the number of pairs. The Lean MCA evaluator generates the free scalar labels from the set with zero and one removed and produces supports directly at their required size. On the order-four subgroup of the field with five elements, a direct enumeration of all 390625 received pairs gives worst bad counts 1, 4, 4, 4 and 4 in order of increasing errors. The executable Lean test independently has first failure at one error for budget three and at zero errors for budget zero. **List budgets with a short numerical formula.** On every injective domain, for $1\le k\le n$ and positive width, the worst list at $n-k$ errors has exactly $C=\binom{n}{k}$ words. If $\lfloor C/(k+1)\rfloor\le B<C$ and $B<q$, the first failure is $n-k$. If $C\le B<q$, it is $n-k+1$. For other budgets the rank test still applies. Further results include first failures of 94 at length 128 and quarter rate over a degree-27 extension, and 268435457 at length $2^{30}$ and half rate on a field with budget two. At every length through $2^{128}$ the scalar list cutoff transfers to every positive width over every finite field. Prime-field sequences have different MCA behaviors on the two sides of the entropy threshold, and the critical exponent itself permits different outcomes. ## Prize submission

The sponsor describes the statement as preliminary. Its guidelines call for acceptance at a reputable field-appropriate conference or journal, a publicly archived academic version, and an emailed PDF explaining the result and its relationship to prior work. AI-assisted work requires human verification and editing. The judges assess significance and eligibility. The repository records the formal result, while numerical production evaluations, peer review and prize adjudication have their own evidence requirements.
