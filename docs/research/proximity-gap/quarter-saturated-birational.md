# The four-head minor map at the quarter-rate successor must be birational

Let $n=2^{30}$, $d=n/4-1$ and let $H$ be the designated smooth group at the second prime. Suppose four polynomial heads exceed the scalar budget $2n$ at $n/2$ errors. Their three difference columns give a homogeneous $2\times3$ matrix $V$ with entries of degree $d$. Its three signed maximal minors represent a map $f:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^2$ of degree $2d$. It is birational onto its image over the algebraic closure. In particular every proper rational quotient is excluded, including covers with no symmetry. The written proof uses the actual fresh labels and works on any injective domain having this saturation structure. [Saturation and the projective map](quarter-saturated-symmetry.md)

## Why a rational cover repeats fresh labels

The four triple determinants have degree $2d$ and disjointly labelled simple pair strata as in the saturation argument. The $2\times3$ matrix has rank two at every geometric point of $\mathbb P^1$. At an allocated double site its projective kernel is exactly the relation expressing equality of the matched two heads. These six projective kernel vectors are distinct. The full inverse image of each of these six distinguished plane points is exactly its double stratum, since all roots of each triple determinant are already accounted for. The determinants have simple roots at these sites.

A degree-$d$ homogeneous scalar row annihilating the vector of signed minors is a constant linear combination of the two rows of $V$. Indeed at each double site its entries satisfy the corresponding head equality. On a triple with $2d$ double sites, the two determinants involving the new row are multiples of the original determinant by a constant, by the root bound. Cramer's rule gives a constant combination on two columns. The last column agrees therewith on its $2d>d$ assigned double sites, and hence agrees identically. The two leading rows are independent because the matrix at infinity has rank two. Thus there is no nonzero syzygy of degree $<d$. If $f$ were not birational onto its image, Lüroth's theorem would give a rational cover $\phi:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1$ of degree $q\ge2$ and a plane map $g$ with $f=g\circ\phi$. Choose homogeneous coordinates $[A:B]$ of degree $q$ for $\phi$, without a common zero, and coordinates $[G_1:G_2:G_3]$ of degree $m$ without a common zero for $g$. The compositions $G_i(A,B)$ likewise have no common zero. They agree with the signed minors up to a single nonzero constant, so $qm=2d$. Work in homogeneous binary forms. For $\ell=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, the map $(U_1,U_2,U_3)\mapsto\sum U_iG_i$ on degree-$\ell$ triples has kernel dimension at least $3(\ell+1)-(m+\ell+1)$. Pulling a syzygy back by $[A:B]$ preserves linear independence since a nonconstant map of projective lines is surjective. Odd $m$ would give a nonzero syzygy of degree $q\ell<d$. Therefore $m=2\ell$ and $q\ell=d$. There are now at least two independent degree-$\ell$ syzygies downstairs. Their pullbacks span the entire two-dimensional row space upstairs. Consequently every original head difference vector is obtained by evaluating two fixed degree-$\ell$ forms at $(A,B)$.

For two sites in one $\phi$-fiber, the evaluated matrices $V$ therefore differ by a common nonzero scalar. They have the same projective kernel, so a fiber containing a double site consists entirely of sites of the same pair stratum. Every such fiber is unramified because the vanishing triple minors have simple roots there. Hence there are exactly $(n-4)/q$ double fibers. Within each fiber the two mismatching heads give at most two fresh projective directions in total. The four singleton sites contribute at most twelve more. Thus all four heads give at most $2(n-4)/q+12\le n+8<2n$ directions, contradicting the hypothesized overrun. The residual irreducible plane curve has degree $2d=536870910$ and geometric genus zero.

## A finite arithmetic test on the six marked strata

For each head $i$, take the monic locator $K_i(X)$ whose roots are the three edge strata in the triangle omitting $i$. The four triple determinants are their nonzero scalar multiples and satisfy a signed linear relation. Thus **any four coefficient rows** of the four locators must give a singular matrix over the named prime field. An observed rank of four, using even a few coefficients, rigorously excludes that partition without solving for any heads.

For a production example put $s=2d/3=178956970$. Test all allocations where each of the six labelled edge strata is one consecutive block of exactly $s$ powers of the specified generator and the four singleton positions lie in the six intervening gaps. The start of one block can be set to exponent zero by rotating the entire allocation. Up to renaming the heads there are $30$ edge orders and $\binom95=126$ gap patterns. All **3,780** systems, representing **90,720** choices of gap pattern and labelled edge order, have rank four already on their eight leading and trailing coefficient rows. The singleton ownership can be arbitrary because it has no effect on the six double strata. Thus no such system can realize the four heads. This includes allocations with no labelled rotational symmetry.

The test computes the first three power sums on each block and on its inverse block as geometric series. Newton's identities give the three nonconstant endpoint coefficients at each end after allowing for the constant product of roots. Direct polynomial multiplication on small blocks checks the coefficient convention. Modular elimination uses the explicit generator and field modulus of the second named prime. The test covers these marked blocks; arbitrary partitions on the full domain remain open.
