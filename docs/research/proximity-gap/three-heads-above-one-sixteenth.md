# The second-prime cutoff through dimension 71,582,789

On every injective evaluation domain of length $n=2^{30}$ at the second designated prime, the first failing MCA error count at every dimension $2\le K\le71,582,789$ is **715,827,882**, for every positive interleaving width. This extends the cutoff by **4,473,925 dimensions** above rate $1/16$, up to $K=\lceil n/15\rceil$. The extension has a written proof. Lean proves the double interpolation, the degree and allocation checks, and the final three-head covering implication. The bound on sections of a nonlinear factor uses the written normalization and surface-intersection argument. The end-to-end Lean theorem previously covers all $2\le K\le2^{26}$, and gives a distinct answer in dimension one. ## A sextic and its sections

Write $d=71,582,788$, $e=715,827,881$, and $t=n-e=357,913,943$. First work at dimension $d+1$. For a fixed pair of received words, select one polynomial $q_z$ and its nonjoint witness for each bad scalar $z$. A double interpolant $Q(X,Z,Y)$ contains all the points $(z,q_z)$ over the function field. Its value degree is at most six, its total scalar and value degree is at most 25, and its weight for $(X,Z,Y)$ of weights $(1,0,d)$ is at most $2t-1$. At these parameters the number of unknowns exceeds the scalar linear conditions by exactly
$$\sum_{j=0}^{6}(26-j)(2t-jd)-77n=658.$$
The double root of a specialization at every agreement site forces that specialization to vanish identically. [Actual interpolation in Lean](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SixthDegreeThreeHeadWindow.lean)

Factor over the algebraic closure, taking primitive representatives of the factors over the coordinate function field. Assign each selected label to one factor. Factors independent of $Y$ together account for at most 25 scalar labels. There are at most six distinct factors of positive value degree. For a factor with value degree $b\ge2$, use the section bound with the full weight $W=2t-1$ and $m=25$. If $X_b=(2b-2)m-b(b-1)+1$ and $Z_b=(2b-2)W-b(b-1)d+1$, the caps in increasing degree are
$$88,968,\quad 486,488,\quad 1,338,153,\quad 2,720,889,\quad 4,649,234.$$
These are the integers $X_b+\lfloor ((8mb+2)X_bZ_b+2mn+(n-d)(m+1))/(t-d)\rfloor$. The largest required characteristic cutoff is 5,010,795,211, below the designated prime. [Section bound, including the ramification hypotheses](hodge-section-variance.md#a-population-inequality-without-a-weight-restriction)

Put $L=5,000,000$. A factor of value degree one carrying more than $L$ labels gives an actual affine head $q_z=A+zB$ with $A,B$ polynomials over the original field. For clarity, at a site where the received line is not an identity for the factor, at most $m$ labels agree. Among the identity sites, each site misses at most $m$ labels by primitivity and linearity in $Y$. Take the $m+1$ labels with fewest misses. Each misses at most $\lfloor mn/(L-m)\rfloor$ identity sites. There are at least $t-\lfloor mn/L\rfloor$ identity sites. Therefore any three of the chosen labels agree together on more than $d$ sites, since
$$t-\lfloor mn/L\rfloor-3\lfloor mn/(L-m)\rfloor>d.$$
Their determinant over the coordinate function field vanishes, so all $m+1$ lie on a single affine line. Restricting the irreducible factor to that line gives a scalar polynomial of degree at most $m$ with $m+1$ roots. Interpolation between two selected polynomials gives $A,B$ of degree at most $d$. [Identity-site argument](hodge-section-variance.md#production-specialization)

## Counting the actual affine heads

Call heads with more than $L$ assigned labels large, and write $j$ for their number. A head with $M$ labels and joint core of size $c$ satisfies $M\max(1,t-c)\le n-c$ because fresh agreement sites of different labels are disjoint and every witness needs at least one. Hence $M\le e+1$, and a large head has $c\ge t-\lfloor e/(L-1)\rfloor=357,913,800=:C$. All remaining assigned points together number at most $(6-j)L+25$. If $j\le2$, this gives at most 1,451,655,789 labels.

If four large heads contain a noncollinear triple, their four cores have sum at most $n+4d$. This includes the case of a collinear triple among the four. Here $4C-(n+4d)=71,582,224>0$, so every collection of four or more large heads is collinear. For any $j\ge3$ collinear large heads, their union $U$ of joint cores has $|U|\ge jC-\binom j2d$ by the pairwise root bound. At a site of $U$, all the heads yield at most one distinct fresh label, because they evaluate to collinear pairs and the received pair equals one of them. Elsewhere there are at most $j$ fresh labels. The contribution of these heads and all remaining labels is at most
$$jn-(j-1)\left(jC-\binom j2d\right)+(6-j)L+25.$$
The maximum at $j=3,4,5,6$ is **1,518,239,425**. [Four-core determinant and fresh-label count](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md#core-multiplicity)

It remains to consider exactly three noncollinear large heads. If the total exceeded the budget $2n$, each would have at least
$$2n+1-2(e+1)-3L-25=700,827,860$$
assigned labels. Twice this exceeds $n$, so the fresh-site inequality forces each core to have size at least $t-1$. Their three-head determinant has degree at most $2d$, giving a core union of size at least $3(t-1)-2d$. A selected polynomial outside all three heads can agree with the received pair after folding on at most $d$ sites of each core. It would have at most $n-3(t-1)+5d=t-5$ agreements in total. This contradiction puts every selected label on one of the three heads, whose total capacity is $3(e+1)=2n-2$. Thus the predecessor is safe. [Determinant covering proof](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ThreeHeadDeterminantCover.lean)

Dimension monotonicity carries safety to every smaller positive dimension because the agreement count exceeds $d+1$. At 715,827,882 errors the existing three-head locator construction already exceeds $2n$ for all $3\le K\le d+1$, since $3(n-715827882)\le n+2K$. The dimension-two construction gives the same failure there. The predecessor and failure, interleaving invariance and the discrete Hamming boundary yield the asserted exact cutoff. Dimension one has its separate threshold. ## What changes at the next dimension

At $K=71,582,790$ one has $n+5(K-1)=4t-3$. The three-head outside-agreement bound then allows exactly $t$ agreements. Equality would require all three cores to have exactly $t-1$ sites, the determinant to spend its full $2(K-1)$ degree on their excess overlap, and an outside witness to hit every site off their union and exactly $K-1$ distinct sites in each core without double counting. The interpolation and small-population steps have slack if the total degree 25 is raised. Occupancy of this equality configuration remains a separate question.
