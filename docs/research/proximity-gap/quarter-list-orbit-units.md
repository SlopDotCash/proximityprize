# Small-height functions on the two orbits of quartic poles

This argument counts polynomial solutions in the harmonic case of the length-independent quarter-rate proof. The base field is $K=k(X)$ with $k$ algebraically closed. Heights of elements of a finite separable extension $L/K$ are degrees of their pole divisors on its smooth projective curve, divided by $[L:K]$. This normalization is unchanged under further finite extensions. Denote it by $h$. The zero function and constants have height zero.

Let $d=n/4$, allowing real degree bounds, and suppose a large group lies on $g=A+B V$, with $A,B$ coprime, $\deg_Y A\le5$, $\deg_Y B=4$, $\operatorname{wt}(B)\le4d+\ell$, and $0\le\ell\le n/1000$. The coordinate and ordinate weights here are $1,d$. Assume the preceding harmonic reduction: $B$ is squarefree over $K$ with cubic invariant $J=0$, its primitive Hessian square root is $Q=q_2Y^2+q_1Y+q_0$ of weight at most $3d+\ell$, and
$$
 \phi(Y)=-\frac{q_1Y+2q_0}{2q_2Y+q_1}
$$
commutes with $\delta=\partial_X-(A/B)\partial_Y$. Write $r=-q_1/(2q_2)$ and $\Delta=q_1^2-4q_2q_0$. The constant section at infinity is horizontal and $r=\phi(\infty)$ is horizontal. A horizontal section is a solution of the differential equation in the indicated affine or projective coordinate. Discard $f=r$ if it occurs. The remaining $\phi(f)$ are rational solutions, with no finite pole at any site where the leading quartic coefficient $B_4$ is a unit. If the characteristic is finite, assume $p>1000n$. ## Good reduction outside a finite set

Call a finite base point *stable* if $B_4$, the invariant $I_0$ and the resultant of $A,B$ are nonzero there. Call a received point *clean* if it has order three, the quartic specializes to a triple received root $y$ and a distinct fourth root, $B_4$ and $q_2$ are units, $\Delta$ has a simple zero, and the total affine intersection length at that abscissa is five, all at $y$. Let $E$ include infinity and every finite abscissa which is neither stable nor clean. If $m_0$ is an upper bound for the number of grid sites of order different from three, one can take
$$
       |E|\le 1+7m_0+12\ell .
$$
Indeed reserve one zero of $I_0$ and five orders of the resultant at each order-three site. Failure of the clean conditions there costs an extra order, or a zero of $B_4$. This uses $C\Delta=4I_0$ for $4BB_{YY}-3B_Y^2=CQ^2$. A site with nonzero cubic Taylor coefficient has $Cq_2\ne0$. If that coefficient vanishes at an order-three site, $I_0$ vanishes at least twice. Outside this set include the other grid sites, the remaining zeros of $I_0$ and the resultant, and the zeros of $B_4$. The degree bounds on these three polynomials are $n+2\ell$, $5n+9\ell$, and $\ell$. The same accounting bounds the number of grid sites that fail to be clean by $7m_0+12\ell$, even if some are stable. The resultant degree bounds its full valuation, including intersections at infinity. The four roots of $B$ form two pairs under $\phi$, with no fixed root. Indeed after putting the harmonic roots at the fourth roots of unity, the Hessian roots are zero and infinity, and the involution is multiplication by $-1$. Let $L$ be the field which labels the two pairs, so $m=[L:K]\le2$. Over $L$, let $D_1,D_2$ be the monic quadratic polynomials with these respective pairs as roots. Put $v=D_1/D_2$, a rational function of $Y$. The ratio $(v\circ\phi)/v$ is constant in $Y$, by its divisor. Evaluation at a fixed point of $\phi$ shows this constant is one. Hence $v$ is a degree-two quotient coordinate for $\phi$, taking the orbit of infinity to one.

The extension $L/K$ is unramified at every stable and clean abscissa. At a stable point the four quartic roots have distinct finite reductions, since $J=0$, $I_0\ne0$ and $B_4$ is a unit. All four lift over $k[[X-x]]$ by Hensel's lemma. At a clean point the fourth simple root lifts over the same ring; its mate under $\phi$ belongs to the local field and is integral since it is a root of the monic integral quartic. This identifies that pair over the local field and identifies the complementary pair. On each point of $L$ over this abscissa the two monic quadratics therefore reduce, in some order, to $(Y-y)^2$ and $(Y-y)(Y-z)$ with $z\ne y$.

For every remaining word $f$ the two rational functions $v_f=D_1(f)/D_2(f)$ and $1-v_f$ have no zeros or poles outside the places over $E$.

* At a stable point, $f$ avoids the four roots because $B(X,f)=0$ there would force $A(X,f)=0$. The specialized involution has nonzero determinant since $C\Delta=4I_0$. The specialized monic quadratics have disjoint roots and define a degree-two map. Its fiber at one is the orbit of infinity, counted with the appropriate multiplicities if infinity is fixed. Thus a finite equality $v_f=1$ at this point would require $f$ to meet $r$. If $r$ is finite, the differential equation is regular at the common value and the two sections must coincide. Indeed if their difference had finite positive order $a<p$, its derivative would have order $a-1$, while the difference of the two regular right sides has order at least $a$. The contact order is bounded by $\deg_X(q_2 f+q_1/2)\le2d+\ell<p$. The same proof uses no characteristic bound in characteristic zero.
* At a clean point a nonhit $f$ avoids $z$, since otherwise $A,B$ would intersect at a second ordinate. The two reduced quadratics and their difference then take nonzero values at $f(x)$. At a hit the two quadratic evaluations have positive order and their product has order exactly two by the clean intersection calculation. Both have order one. Their difference is proportional to $Y-r$ over $L$ since the monics have equal leading coefficients and $v(r)=1$. Its linear coefficient is a unit by the two displayed reductions. The contact of $f$ with $r$ is simple: at a clean point $\phi(f)=r+\Delta/[4q_2^2(f-r)]$ would have a pole at a higher-order contact, contrary to $B_4(x)\ne0$. Thus the difference also has order one. This proves the claim for both functions.

There are no identically zero evaluations among these functions: $B(X,f)\ne0$, and a generic fiber at one contains just $r$ and infinity. The argument at stable points allows $q_2(x)=0$, since the projective involution still has good reduction there.

## Height and ramification

For a nonconstant separable function $u$ whose values $0,1,\infty$ are supported on the places over $E$, Riemann–Hurwitz gives
$$
 m h(u)\le 2g(L)-2+\#\{P:P\mid E\}.
$$
Indeed the contributions from the three fibers to the different have total degree at least $3mh(u)-\#\{P:P\mid E\}$. Since $L/K$ is tame of degree at most two and is unramified away from $E$, the right side equals $m(|E|-2)$ by Riemann–Hurwitz for $X$. For $u=v_f$ there is no inseparable nonconstant case under the stated characteristic bound. To see this, pass to a field splitting the four quartic roots $\rho_i$. At a place there, the product of $\max(1,|\rho_i|)$ equals the maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of the monic quartic, by multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean Gauss norm. After summation and normalization this gives $\sum h(\rho_i)\le4d+\ell$ using the unscaled coefficients. Hence $h(v_f)\le4h(f)+\sum h(\rho_i)\le8d+\ell$. The unnormalized degree on $L$ is less than $p$. A nonconstant inseparable function would have degree at least $p$. In particular every $v_f$ has normalized height at most $|E|$.

Their derivatives with respect to $X$ satisfy
$$
 h(v_f')\le 2h(v_f)+(2g(L)-2+2m)/m\le 2h(v_f)+|E|.
$$
At a pole of $v_f$ of order $a$, the differential $dv_f$ has pole order at most $a+1$. The positive zero divisor of $dX$ has degree at most $2g(L)-2+2m$, since $X$ has total pole degree $m$ and is tame at infinity. The second inequality uses the ramification bound for $L/K$.

Let the images of the two fixed points of $\phi$ in the $v$ coordinate be $w_+,w_-$. They are negatives of each other. In a coordinate $z$ with the harmonic roots at $1,i,-1,-i$, the quotient coordinate is a nonzero scalar multiple of $(z^2-1)/(z^2+1)$ or its reciprocal; its branch points come from $0,\infty$. Thus $W=w_+^2=w_-^2$ belongs to $L$. At a clean point put $h_0=Y-r$, $k_0=\Delta/(4q_2^2)$. Each quadratic orbit factor is $h_0^2-a_j h_0+k_0$, since $\phi$ acts by $h_0\mapsto k_0/h_0$. The two $a_j$ are integral there, one reducing to zero and the other to $z-y\ne0$. In the local quadratic extension adjoining $\sqrt{k_0}$, its order is one and that of $k_0$ is two. At $h_0=\sqrt{k_0}$ the respective evaluations $2k_0-a_j\sqrt{k_0}$ therefore have orders two and one, in some order. Hence $W$ has a simple zero or pole **at every place of $L$ above each clean abscissa**. The normalized height $h(W)$ is at least half the number of clean abscissas. An upper bound needed for positive-characteristic derivative tests follows from $h(c)\le3d+\ell$ for either root $c$ of the primitive quadratic $Q$, by the same Gauss-norm argument. Thus $h(W)\le2(4(3d+\ell)+4d+\ell)<9n$.

For use in the terminal coefficient estimates, $v$ has two distinct unramified poles of its **induced vector field**, one at each of the marked values zero and infinity. Indeed each is the image of two simple poles of $-A/B$, at which the quotient map has invertible differential. Away from them the field is regular: at a ramified point the fixed section is horizontal, so in a local parameter vanishing there its derivative also vanishes there, and differentiation preserves regularity downstairs. Consequently $\delta v=c_{-1}/v+c_0+c_1v+c_2v^2+c_3v^3$. The cubic growth is the allowed simple pole in the coordinate $1/v$ at infinity.
