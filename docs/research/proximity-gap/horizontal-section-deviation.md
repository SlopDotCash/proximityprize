## The ramification and exceptional incidences on a fibre

Use the normalized degree-$b$ cover and its minimal resolution from the section construction. Write $B_X=(2b-2)m-b(b-1)+1$ and $B_Z=(2b-2)W-b(b-1)d+1$ for the bounds on the degree of the reduced branch curve on a vertical and horizontal line, respectively. Here $b\ge2$ is the ordinate degree, $m$ bounds total scalar and ordinate degree, and $W$ bounds coordinate and ordinate weight. Remove at most $B_X$ scalar labels as in that construction. The retained $N$ sections have distinct scalars. Write $c_z$ for the negative self-intersection on the resolution and $T=\sum_z c_z$. On the normal surface the negative self-intersection of each selected section is at most $c_z$. [Surface construction and adjunction](section-incidence-hodge-index.md#the-surface)

Let $B$ be the reduced branch divisor. In tame degree $b$ each ramification coefficient $e-1$ on the normalization is at most $(1-1/b)e$. The pullback of an effective divisor in Mumford's pairing is effective. The minimal-resolution discrepancy in the cited construction is subtracted. Thus the Jacobian divisor $R$ on the resolution is bounded coefficientwise by $(1-1/b)\varphi^*B$. A selected section $C_z$ is isomorphic to its horizontal line and is not contained in the pullback. At a point where that line meets $B$ with multiplicity $u>0$, the local intersection of $R$ with $C_z$ is an integer at most $(1-1/b)u$, hence at most $u-1$. [Ramification and the resolution](section-incidence-hodge-index.md#the-surface) · [Mumford's intersection product](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1007/BF02698717/)

Remove horizontal lines from $B$. On retained scalar lines this does not affect the intersections. Write $(U,V)$ for the remaining bidegrees, with $U\le B_X$ and $V\le B_Z$, where $V$ is the degree of projection onto the scalar line. If the remaining curve is empty there are no contributions. Otherwise its normalized components give separable tame maps to that line. For each point $y$ of the reduced curve let $r_y$ be its number of branches and $\delta_y$ its delta invariant. Summing the contact deficiencies $u-1$ over the scalar line gives the total ramification of these maps plus $\sum_y(r_y-1)$. The genus formula on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, including disconnected normalizations, and Riemann–Hurwitz give the exact sum
$$2U(V-1)-\sum_y(2\delta_y-r_y+1).$$
At each singular point $\delta_y\ge1$ and $\delta_y\ge r_y-1$, so the last summand is at least one. If $P$ denotes the number of singular branch points at retained labels, this gives
$$T+P\le2B_X(B_Z-1).$$
The field characteristic exceeds $b$ and the branch projection degrees for all factors in the numerical check. [Riemann–Hurwitz](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B) · [Branches of a curve](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C3Z)

At any one site $x$, at most $m$ labels are exceptional roots from the residual binary form in the section construction. The singular branch points on the vertical line are at most $B_X$. If the line is a component of the reduced curve, remove it and intersect with the remainder. The line itself is smooth and other vertical lines are disjoint. This gives a uniform exceptional count $E_x\le m+B_X$ at **each site**, including sites on vertical components of the branch divisor. Globally the count is at most $mn+P$.

## Bounding the section population from one site

At a site let $I_z\in\{0,1\}$ be the agreement indicator, $\theta=N^{-1}\sum_z I_z$ and $g=f_x/b$ the normalized degree of the truncated received divisor $A_x$. Put $T_x=A_x-g\Phi_X$. Both $T_x$ and the horizontal sections lie in $\Phi_Z^\perp$. Distinct horizontal sections are disjoint. Give each section the sign of $T_x\cdot C'_z$ and apply Hodge index to their signed sum. Orthogonality removes every cross term, giving
$$\left(\sum_z|T_x\cdot C'_z|\right)^2\le(-A_x^2)T.$$
This use of all sections at a single site bounds the absolute deviations even when their signed sum is zero. [Signed-sum lemma](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/HorizontalSectionDeviation.lean)

The fibre estimate needed here is $-A_x^2\le mb\min(g,1-g)$. The bound $m f_x$ follows by clipping the orders of $w=Y-L_x(Z)$ at each vertical component. For the complementary divisor $\Phi_x-A_x$, the same clipping applies to $(X-x)/w$. Its horizontal zeros at finite scalar lie over poles of $Y$, whose order is bounded by the order of the leading coefficient of the ordinate polynomial by reversing its equation. At scalar infinity use $Y'=Y/Z$, which costs one extra scalar-infinity divisor. The leading coefficient has scalar degree at most $m-b$. Removing its exact vertical factor at $x$ and applying the projection formula bounds the negative self-intersection by $(m-b+1)(b-f_x)$. The two complementary divisors have the same self-intersection. [Clipping and the projection formula](hodge-section-variance.md#truncating-the-order-of-the-received-function)

Away from the $E_x$ exceptional labels one has $T_x\cdot C'_z=I_z-g$. Even for an exceptional label, $C'_z\cdot A_x$ is in $[0,1]$. Thus with $L=\theta(1-g)+(1-\theta)g$ one has
$$NL\le \sqrt{mb\min(g,1-g)T}+E_x.$$

Take $a=7/50$. When $a\le\theta\le1-a$, one has $L\ge a$ and $L^2\ge4a(1-2a)\min(g,1-g)$. For $g\le1/2$, use $L\ge a+(1-2a)g$ and square the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. Complement both fractions for $g\ge1/2$. Using $E_x\le m+B_X$ and $T\le2B_XB_Z$, a single such site gives
$$N\le\sqrt{625bmB_XB_Z/126}+50(m+B_X)/7.$$

If there is no such site, the chord of $y=\theta^3$ through $a,1-a$ lies below the cubic at every site. The section construction gives $\sum_x\theta_x\ge t$ and $N\sum_x\theta_x^3\le Nd+(n-d)(m+1)$. Since $a(1-a)=301/2500$ we obtain
$$N\bigl(2199t-301n-2500d\bigr)\le2500(n-d)(m+1).$$
Use the larger of these last two caps and add back the removed $B_X$ labels. For factors on which the existing per-section weighted moment estimate is sharper, use it with $K=457/500$. Its variance budget improves to $b[2mB_X(B_Z-1)+mn]$, since $mT+P\le2mB_X(B_Z-1)$. ## The half-rate certificate

The primary multiplicity, ordinate-degree and total-degree triple is $(9,12,2092862)$ and the companion triple is $(17,24,185)$. The respective interpolation surpluses are **13,592** and **349,515,892**. Apply the two-interpolant and rich-head allocation argument with cutoff **50,230,909**. For $j$ rich affine heads and total assigned ordinate degree $l$, a common nonlinear factor of ordinate degree $b$ has $W\le9t-1-(l-b)d$ and $m\le185-j$. A factor absent from the companion is covered by the cutoff. The largest total occurs at four rich heads and assigned ordinate degree twelve. The cap includes up to 2,092,862 scalar-only labels. Lean also bounds every allocation by 2,025,212,836 using a per-slot inequality. The exact integer partition maximum is one smaller. Interleaving stability transfers it to every positive width.
