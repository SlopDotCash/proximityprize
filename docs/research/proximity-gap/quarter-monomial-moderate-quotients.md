# Three arbitrary target values on primitive monomial quotients of size at least 512

The three-head score bound holds on every normalized dyadic quotient from 512 to the full first named subgroup. The proof below treats large quotients directly and checks four smaller sizes by exact finite-field arithmetic.

Consider the cross ratio of four different scaled Laurent monomials on the first named prime field. Divide their exponent differences by their gcd as in the [degree reduction](quarter-monomial-cross-ratio.md). On the resulting group of order $8192\le N\le2^{30}$, if the resulting nonconstant map has degree $m<N/4$, the number $r$ of sites mapping to any three distinct projective values is at most $9N/16$. Since $r\le3m$, this implies $2r-3m\le9N/16$. In particular these three-head constructions cannot improve the quarter-rate MCA witness at 480,946,859 errors. The result extends the [arbitrary-target bound at larger quotients](quarter-monomial-target-compression.md).

Call the three values arising from a pair of coincident monomials the collision targets. In counting other targets the unreduced pairing equations give upper bounds even at a common zero of the pairing products.

## A power agreeing with a projectivity

Let $H$ be cyclic of order $M$, and let $Y^d=G(Y)$ at $b$ elements of $H$ for a projectivity $G$ over the algebraic closure. Unless $G(Y)$ is a scaling or a reciprocal scaling, $b(b-1)\le2(M-1)$. Indeed, for $h\in H$ other than one, every pair of matches at $Y,hY$ satisfies $G(hY)=h^d G(Y)$. Clearing denominators gives a polynomial of degree at most two. An identity would make the single zero and the single pole of $G$ fixed by $Y\mapsto hY$, forcing them to be zero and infinity. Summing the bound of two over $h$ counts all distinct ordered pairs. [Lean theorem over any field](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/PowerProjectivityMatches.lean)

When two of the four monomials have distinct constant values $A,B$, each noncollision target is equivalent to an invertible projectivity carrying the third value to the fourth and fixing $A,B$. This follows by using $(Y-A)/(Y-B)$ as projective coordinate; the prescribed cross ratio fixes its multiplicative factor. Rescaling the two variable entries rescales the argument and the output and preserves the preceding two special types. A scaling fixing $A,B$ must be the identity, which is a collision target. The reciprocal type can arise at only one target and requires $B=-A$, with map $Y\mapsto A^2/Y$.

If some original exponents repeat, divide out their common monomial power. For at most two distinct exponents primitivity gives a single exponent difference one, and the pairing products give $m\le2$. Otherwise take the equal-exponent pair as $A,B$, and write the other two powers as $X^u,X^v$ with their coefficients. Primitivity lets us take $u$ odd. Each ordinary noncollision target then has at most $1+\sqrt{2N}$ matches by the preceding argument reparametrized by $Y=X^u$. The possible reciprocal target contributes at most $\gcd(N,u+v)$ sites if the exponent sum is nonzero modulo $N$. The span bound for three distinct exponents is $m+2$, so $|u+v|\le2m+4<N$. If it is zero as an integer, the reciprocal equation is either empty or makes the original cross ratio constant. When $4\mid u+v$, both exponents are odd and $\gcd(N,u-v)\le2$, so there are at most six pair-collision sites in total. Otherwise the reciprocal contributes at most two and the global pair-collision bound is $N/2+4$. Thus $r\le N/2+6+3(1+\sqrt{2N})<9N/16$ throughout the claimed range. The global pair-collision bound is established in the [exponent and grid-gcd proof](quarter-monomial-target-compression.md).

## Four distinct exponents

Suppose first every pair gap has grid gcd at most $N/8$. If two exponents of each parity occur, there are at most $N/4+4$ collision sites. If three share a parity, the two smaller gcds in that triple coincide and are at most $N/16$ when the largest is $N/8$; otherwise all three are at most $N/16$. Adding the three mixed pairs gives the same bound. Apply the [simultaneous compression of three opposite pair sums](quarter-monomial-target-compression.md#compressing-three-frequencies-at-once) with $K=15$, for which $2K^3<N$. On its ordinary cosets the fraction of sites mapping to the three targets is below $6/15$. If there is a harmonic exceptional coset, the equal or opposite equations for its one fixed exponent pair cover at most $2N/8$ sites, since the original exponents are distinct. Without such a coset the exceptional sites are all pair collisions. Thus
$$
 r\le (N/4+4)+(3N/4-4)6/15<9N/16 .
$$

Put $M=N/4$. If a gap has grid gcd at least $N/2$, the degree bound forces the exponent shape $0,M,2M,M+b$ with $b$ odd. The first three terms take three fixed values on each coset of $\mu_M$. Their two short pair equations coincide and lie in the long pair equation by the [degree and cancellation theorem](quarter-monomial-cross-ratio.md#the-only-possible-large-scale-exponents). At most two cosets have a repeated one of these three values. On every other coset the fourth monomial permutes a scaled copy of $\mu_M$, and its cross ratio with the three distinct fixed points is injective. Hence $r\le 2M+6$.

It remains that the maximum gap gcd is exactly $M$. Choose such a pair and divide the four monomials by the first monomial power. On each of the four cosets of $\mu_M$ the selected pair has constant values $A,B$. Each of $A=B$ and $A=-B$ holds on at most one coset. The other two values have exponent differences $u,v$ from the first one. At least one, say $u$, is odd. Write $d=v/u\bmod M$ and parameterize each coset by $Y$ so the two remaining entries are proportional to $Y$ and $Y^d$. On a coset with $A\ne B$ an ordinary noncollision target therefore has at most $b_0=1+\sqrt{2M}$ sites. A reciprocal target is possible only on the coset $A=-B$.

If $d=0\bmod M$, three entries are constant on each coset. A coset where two of them agree is entirely made of pair collisions. There are at most two such cosets by the global $N/2+4$ bound. On the rest the cross ratio of the fourth entry with three distinct constants is injective. This gives $r\le2M+6$.

If none of the three targets is a collision target, reserve the cosets $A=B$ and $A=-B$ if present. Each remaining coset supplies at most $3b_0$ matches. Since $M>3b_0$, $r\le2M+6b_0$. The same reservation suffices even if there are collision targets when $4\mid d+1$. In this case $u,v$ are odd and $\gcd(N,u-v)=2$, since multiplication by the odd $u$ preserves the valuation of $d-1\bmod M$. Globally the four mixed pairs give at most four sites, and the pair of moving entries at most two. Outside the reserved cosets this adds at most six collision sites, so $r\le2M+6b_0+6$.

In the remaining case at least one target is a collision and $\gcd(M,d+1)\le2$. Each coset with $A\ne B$ then contributes at most $2b_0$ ordinary noncollision matches, and the possible reciprocal equation on its single coset contributes at most two more sites. If $A=B$ on some coset, including it in the global pair-collision bound gives $r\le2M+4+6b_0+2$. If $A=B$ nowhere, the raw pair-collision count improves to $M+4$. Indeed for $v$ odd the four mixed pairs give at most four sites and the moving pair at most $M$. For $v$ even, its two differences from the fixed pair have the same grid gcd $\gcd(M,v)\le M/2$ since $d\ne0\bmod M$; the three other pairs involving the odd exponent each give at most one site. We obtain $r\le M+4+8b_0+2$. All the displayed bounds are below $9N/16$ for $M\ge2048$. [Integer cutoff and projectivity checks](../../../scripts/probes/probe_quarter_monomial_moderate.py)

## The finite quotients from 512 to 4096

On the same first named prime the score bound $2r-3m\le9N/16$ also holds for $N=512,1024,2048,4096$. The following checks use the literal prime and permit monomial coefficients and targets anywhere in its algebraic closure.

On $\mu_{128}$ an arbitrary power $Y^d$ agrees with a projectivity at at most **four** sites unless that projectivity is a scaling or reciprocal scaling. If five matches exist, a pair of cyclically consecutive matched exponents has gap $h\le\lfloor128/5\rfloor$. Rotate to place the first at exponent zero, scaling the target by the appropriate $d$-th power; the projectivity now fixes one. The coordinate $u=1/(Y-1)$ turns every projectivity fixing one into an affine map. The second point fixes its intercept once its slope is specified. Every further point determines the slope by one field division. The identity and the reciprocal become $u\mapsto u$ and $u\mapsto-1-u$. A nonconstant affine map is injective, so exponents giving a repeated previous output are safely omitted. The checker treats every $h$ in the indicated range and every $d$ modulo 128. Two finite chart points already determine the affine line over the prime field even for a projectivity originally given over an extension.

Restricting the grid or partitioning a larger one into cosets now gives a cap $b_0=4\max(1,G/128)$ when $G$ is a power of two between 32 and 1024. Every coset of order 128 in the argument has its power image in a single scaled copy of that group. The exceptional projectivity types are invariant under these scalings. [Finite projectivity verifier](../../../scripts/probes/probe_monomial_power_graphs.py)

If exactly three original exponents occur, the repeated-exponent proof can be sharpened without a finite search. An improved score would force each of the three fibers to have more than $9N/32-m/2>5N/32$ sites, since each other fiber has at most $m$ sites. An ordinary noncollision target has at most $1+\sqrt{2N}<5N/32$ sites. If a reciprocal target is possible, $A=-B$. Its equation is $CDX^{u+v}=A^2$, and the four potentially large targets, including the three collisions, have caps
$$
 \gcd(N,u)+\gcd(N,v),\quad \gcd(N,u)+\gcd(N,v),\quad
 \gcd(N,u-v),\quad \gcd(N,u+v).
$$
An identically true equation for either of the last two targets makes the original rational map constant, by the span bound above. For mixed parity the last two nonempty equations each give at most one site. For two odd exponents the first two bounds are two. Thus at most two targets can be large in the reciprocal case. In its absence an improvement would require all three collision targets, which the fixed-target theorem excludes.

For four distinct exponents let $G$ be the largest grid gcd of their gaps. The case $G\ge N/2$ has already been settled by the degree cancellation argument above, valid for these sizes as well. At $32\le G\le N/4$, apply the fixed-pair coset proof with the large-gap pair on cosets of size $G$; their number is now $q=N/G$. The global pair-collision bound there is $2G+4$: for three same-parity exponents the smaller two grid gcds are at most $G/2$ whenever the largest is $G$, and with two exponents of each parity there are two same-parity pairs. The other ingredients need only that the chosen pair gap have grid gcd exactly $G$ and that all other gaps have gcd at most $G$. In particular if $A=B$ nowhere the bound improves to $G+4$ as above when $d\ne0\bmod G$. Repeating the coset counts with the new $b_0$ gives the respective upper bounds
$$
 2G+3(q-2)b_0+6,\qquad 2G+6+2(q-1)b_0,\qquad G+6+2qb_0,
 \qquad 2G+3(q-2).
$$
The last case has three constant entries on each coset. The verifier tests every $N,G$ here against $9N/16$.

It remains to handle $G<32$. Since one gap is odd, translate and multiply the exponent labels by an odd unit modulo $N$ to obtain $0,1,a,b$ with $2\le a<b<N$. The translation scales all entries by one common monomial. Multiplication by a unit permutes the grid. For the upper count we use the unreduced target equations on that permutation; every preimage of a target satisfies its unreduced equation, including at an original base point.

On a dyadic subgrid of size $J$ dividing $N$, the six pair sums are $1,a,b,1+a,1+b,a+b$ modulo $J$. The same sums apply on every coset, with arbitrary nonzero modified monomial coefficients. Suppose the four exponent residues are distinct and at most one pair of opposite sums coincides. Pair sums from different opposite pairs cannot then coincide. The three pairing products in the cross-ratio pencil are differences of the three opposite-pair expressions $T_iT_j+T_kT_l$. Each nonzero member of the pencil uses at least two such expressions with nonzero coefficients; at most one of those expressions can vanish identically on the coset. Every unreduced target equation therefore has a nonzero coefficient after combining like terms. If an odd relabeling modulo $J$ puts all six sums in a cyclic interval of width $w\le3J/16$, every target equation has at most $w$ roots on the coset by the polynomial root bound. Lifting all cosets gives $r\le9N/16$.

The integer checker tries all such subgrids and odd relabelings for each normalized tuple. At $N=1024,2048,4096$ it covers all tuples with $G<32$. At $N=512$ there are two exceptions to this interval test. In 64 tuples, $G=16$ and the exponent residues modulo 16 comprise two doubled entries. They are of opposite parity by primitivity. On each coset of size 16, divide by the first common power. There are two constant values and two constant multiples of the same permutation monomial. If either pair is identically equal, that coset is covered by one of exactly two pair equations, each with at most 16 sites globally. Otherwise the cross ratio has degree at most two in the permuting variable. It is nonconstant because a scaling fixing the two distinct nonzero constants must be the identity and the two variable coefficients are distinct. Three targets therefore cover at most $32+6(512/16)<9N/16$ sites. [Complete exponent census](../../../scripts/probes/probe_monomial_pair_sum_widths.cpp)

The other six tuples are affinely equivalent modulo 512 to $(0,1,77,163)$. On cosets of order 64 their six frequencies are $(1,13,35,14,36,48)$, all distinct. A nonzero linear combination of these powers cannot vanish at thirteen of the sites. Thirteen or more zeros would contain a cyclic block of five zeros with span at most $\lfloor4\cdot64/13\rfloor=19$, by averaging the distances from a zero to the fourth subsequent zero. Rotate its first zero to exponent zero. For every choice of the other four exponents in $1,\ldots,19$, the verifier finds rank five in the five-by-six evaluation matrix and evaluates a generator of its one-dimensional null space at all 64 sites. All 3,876 such evaluations have only five zeros. Rank and kernel dimension persist under arbitrary field extension. Each target thus has at most twelve sites per coset, giving $r\le3(512/64)12=9N/16$. [All short-block ranks and zero counts](../../../scripts/probes/probe_monomial_power_graphs.py)
