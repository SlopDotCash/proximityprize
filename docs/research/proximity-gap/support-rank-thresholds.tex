{\LARGE\bfseries Exact finite rank tests for\\[2pt] Reed--Solomon proximity thresholds\par}
\vspace{.25em}
{\large Affine-line mutual correlated agreement and interleaved list decoding\par}
\vspace{.5em}
\textbf{Abstract.}
For a Reed--Solomon code with prescribed evaluation points, two finite matrix tests determine the first error count at which an affine-line mutual correlated agreement (MCA) budget or an interleaved list budget is exceeded. One test asks whether each of several sets of joint interpolation equations raises the rank of a common matrix. The other counts distinct compatible polynomial tuples by inclusion--exclusion. Both remove received words from the search. The tests are exact at every positive width and include small fields. At $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$ and $q<2^{256}$ we characterize when a largest safe real radius exists. All eight operational boundaries are explicit on a length-32 family, a length-128 subgroup and a length-$2^{30}$ subgroup of a 129-bit prime field. At length $2^{30}$ and rate $1/16$, smooth-domain MCA thresholds follow an exact formula on 581,610,155 consecutive integer budgets. At budget $2^{31}$ all eligible smooth fields first fail at 715,827,882 errors. The general enumeration can be impractical on codes of cryptographic size.

\section*{1.\quad Parameters and the operational endpoint}
Let $D=(x_i)_{i\in I}$ be $n>0$ distinct elements of $\F$, and let $1\le k\le n$. Write $C=\rs(D,k)$ for evaluations of polynomials of degree $<k$. The sponsor specifies a multiplicative subgroup of power-of-two order and rate $k/n\in\{1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16\}$. The smooth-domain interface also admits nonzero cosets and permuted coordinates. The matrix tests apply to any injective domain, including over extension fields.

Fix a tolerance $\varepsilon\ge0$, a positive interleaving width $m$, an error count $0\le e\le n$, and set $B=\lfloor q\varepsilon\rfloor$, $L=B+1$, $t=n-e$. A width-$m$ codeword consists of $m$ rows in $C$. A column is erroneous if at least one of its rows disagrees with the received column. List failure means that some closed ball contains at least $L$ distinct codewords. For MCA, $\gamma$ is drawn uniformly from $\F$ and the word tested is $u+\gamma v$, for fixed $u,v\in\F^I$. Section~2 gives the precise event. The companion paper selects affine lines in stating the grand challenge \cite{abf}.

\textbf{Boundary theorem.} Each test gives the least failing grid count $e$, if any. A closed ball at real radius $\delta\in[0,1]$ uses $\lfloor n\delta\rfloor$ errors. Failure persists as the ball grows or the required agreement shrinks. Thus the safe radii are $[0,e/n)$, empty if $e=0$. For $e>0$, $e-1$ is the last safe grid count and the supremum $e/n$ fails. When all grid counts pass, the safe set is $[0,1]$.

\textbf{Literal largest safe radius.} Fix $0<k<n$, $m\ge1$ and $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$. For MCA with $q<2^{256}$, a largest safe real radius exists exactly when $\binom{n}{k+1}\le B$, and its value is then one. Each $(k+1)$-site circuit witnesses at most one bad scalar. Conversely, for $N=\binom{n}{k+1}$ a second-moment bound gives a pair with $M$ bad scalars at radius one satisfying $Nq\le M(q+N-1)$. As $B^2<q$, $M\le B$ forces $N\le B$. The \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/28d95ce2e7db421c464f85c9e984343f1c165863/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointAdjacentFieldReview.lean}{attainment theorem} applies to every injective domain. For lists over a field of any size there is \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/28d95ce2e7db421c464f85c9e984343f1c165863/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/ListClosedForm.lean}{no largest safe real radius}. The full ball contains $q^{km}>B$ words, so the open-endpoint alternative above always applies.

\section*{2.\quad A simultaneous rank test for bad scalars}
For $S\subseteq I$, let $C|_S$ be the restrictions to $S$ of words in $C$. For fixed $u,v$, a scalar $\gamma$ is bad at $e$ errors precisely if there is an $S$ of size at least $t$ such that
\[
 (u+\gamma v)|_S\in C|_S,\qquad
 (u|_S,v|_S)\notin (C|_S)^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Both clauses use the same witness $S$. A joint explanation on a different support leaves this witness valid. MCA failure means that one received pair has at least $L$ bad scalars, since the maximum bad probability is the maximum bad count divided by $q$.

Choose $L$ distinct scalars $G\subseteq\F$ and one support $S_g$ of size at least $t$ for every $g\in G$. If $L>q$ there is no such choice and MCA passes. Construct a matrix $P_S$ on $n$ coordinates whose kernel is the set of rows $w$ satisfying $w|_S\in C|_S$. For $|S|\le k$ it has zero rows. Otherwise, take any $k$ anchors $A\subset S$ and for each $i\in S\setminus A$ use the linear equation
\[
  w_i-\sum_{j\in A}w_j\!
       \prod_{a\in A\setminus\{j\}}
       \frac{x_i-x_a}{x_j-x_a}=0 .
 \tag{2}
\]
All denominators are units. Lagrange interpolation makes (2) necessary and sufficient. Stack the blocks $[\,P_{S_g}\ \ gP_{S_g}\,]$ over $g\in G$ into a matrix $M$ on $(u,v)$. Write $K_g=[\,0\ \ P_{S_g}\,]$.

\head{Theorem 1.}
MCA fails at $e$ errors if and only if there is a choice $(G,(S_g))$ for which, for every $g\in G$,
\[
 \rank \begin{bmatrix}M\\K_g\end{bmatrix}>\rank M .
 \tag{3}
\]

\textit{Proof.} Put $V=\ker M$. Every point of $V$ satisfies all the folded equations. At $g$, condition $P_{S_g}v=0$ then implies $P_{S_g}u=0$ as well. Thus the points of $V$ with a joint explanation at $g$ form $J_g=V\cap\ker K_g$. Condition (3) is exactly $J_g\subsetneq V$. Any pair witnessing (1) for all $g$ lies outside all the $J_g$ and forces these strict inclusions.

Conversely, a vector space over $\F$ is never covered by $1\le h\le q$ proper linear subspaces. If its dimension is $d\ge1$, their union has size at most $1+h(q^{d-1}-1)<q^d$, since all contain zero. In dimension zero there are no proper subspaces. Apply this observation to the $|G|\le q$ spaces $J_g$. A single point outside their union supplies the requisite common pair. Selecting any $B+1$ bad scalars in the forward direction completes the equivalence. \hfill$\square$

For one grid count there are $\binom qL$ scalar sets and at most $(\sum_{s=t}^n\binom ns)^L$ support assignments per set. Each assignment needs at most $L+1$ ranks of matrices with $2n$ columns. With $s=\max(t,k+1)$ one can use exactly $s$ sites for every scalar. A bad support has a noninterpolating row $v$. Interpolate $v$ on any $k$ anchors and add one site where that interpolation disagrees. This $(k+1)$-subset detects noninterpolation. Enlarge it inside the original support to $s$ sites, preserving both parts of (1). If $s>n$, no such support exists.

For $1\le B<q$, also fix two labels to $0,1$. Given two original labels $\alpha,\beta$, replace $(u,v)$ by $(u+\alpha v,(\beta-\alpha)v)$ and use parameter $(\gamma-\alpha)/(\beta-\alpha)$. This invertible change preserves folded words and joint interpolation on every support. The reduced enumeration uses $\binom{q-2}{B-1}$ scalar sets and $\binom ns^{B+1}$ assignments per set. For $B=0$ translating a single bad label to zero suffices.

\section*{3.\quad Counting compatible interleaved lists}
Choose $L$ labeled supports $S_1,\ldots,S_L$ of size exactly $t$. A candidate polynomial for label $a$ has $k$ coefficients. For each $a<b$ and $i\in S_a\cap S_b$, impose $f_a(x_i)=f_b(x_i)$ on one scalar row. The resulting matrix $A$ has $kL$ columns. These overlap conditions are sufficient for one received row to agree on every prescribed support: use the common prescribed value wherever at least one support covers a site and fill the other sites arbitrarily.

Let $E$ be the set of unordered pairs of labels. For $T\subseteq E$ append to $A$ the $k$ coefficient equalities $f_a=f_b$ for each $\{a,b\}\in T$. Denote the enlarged matrix by $A_T$ and write $\nu_T=kL-\rank A_T$. Polynomial equality and codeword equality coincide by $k\le n$ and injectivity of $D$.

\head{Theorem 2.}
The list budget fails at $e$ errors if and only if, for at least one of the $\binom nt^L$ support assignments,
\[
  N(S_1,\ldots,S_L)
     =\sum_{T\subseteq E}(-1)^{|T|}\,q^{m\nu_T}>0 .
 \tag{4}
\]

\textit{Proof.} The overlap equations ensure that a single center can be chosen independently at every site and in every row. If all pairs in $T$ collide as interleaved words, the rows are independent elements of $\ker A_T$, giving exactly $q^{m\nu_T}$ labeled message tuples. Inclusion--exclusion over the pair-collision events counts exactly the distinct compatible tuples. Conversely, from a center with $L$ distinct words select any $t$ agreeing sites for each. Trimming its agreements preserves compatibility and distinctness. This gives a positive term count in (4). \hfill$\square$

\head{Corollary 3.}
If $\binom L2\le q$, (4) is positive precisely when $\nu_{\{a,b\}}<\nu_\varnothing$ for every pair. Indeed, if even one equality is forced in one row it is forced in every row. Otherwise the single-pair collision kernels are proper subspaces of $\ker A$. The covering argument of Section 2 supplies a scalar compatible tuple avoiding every collision. It extends to $m\ge1$ by adjoining zero rows. If $L=1$, the conditions are vacuous and there is a compatible tuple.

At $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$ and $q<2^{256}$ this corollary always applies. Set $Q=2^{128}$. For $B=\lfloor q/Q\rfloor\ge1$, $B+1\le Q$ and $\binom{B+1}{2}\le BQ\le q$. The case $B=0$ is immediate. This replaces up to $2^{\binom L2}$ nullities per pattern by at most $1+\binom L2$. The $\binom nt^L$ support patterns still make direct computation prohibitive for many inputs. In particular the theorem by itself does not quantify the distance of a production threshold from the Johnson or capacity radius. The latter question motivates the concrete benchmarks below \cite{abf}.

\head{Corollary 4.}
Let $0\le\varepsilon<1$, $C_k=\binom nk$, and $B=\lfloor\varepsilon q\rfloor$.
If $B\ge\max(C_k,C_{k+1})$, MCA is safe through radius one and the first failing
list count is $n-k+1$, for every injective domain and every positive width.
Indeed, each bad scalar requires a $(k+1)$-subset on which only that scalar can
interpolate the fold. This bounds the total by $C_{k+1}$ at every radius.
A list at $k$ agreements has at most $C_k$ words by interpolation. At $k-1$ agreements
all $q$ multiples of a locator occur in one ball. More generally, for
$B\ge C_{k+1}$ and $B\ge\lfloor C_k/(k+1)\rfloor$, the first list failure is $n-k$
if $B<C_k$, and $n-k+1$ otherwise. The proof of the sharper list step appears in
Section~4.2. For $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$ and $q<2^{256}$, Section~1 and the exact $k$-agreement list show that the joint condition $B\ge\max(C_k,C_{k+1})$ is also necessary at each prize rate. At 128-bit tolerance and positive even length, the \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/041479b5856356a744188bd634d627524c40090f/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointLargeFieldCriterion.lean}{exact all-rate criterion} for MCA to pass everywhere and lists to first fail at minimum distance is
$q\ge2^{128}\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$. The central binomial coefficient dominates all the others and is attained by the half-rate list. The required field size can exceed $2^{256}$ at long lengths. The \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/d6dc87eefe9bd2a20d9e4f5d207079eaaffdb844/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointLargeFieldReview.lean}{joint rank theorem} evaluates both answers.

\section*{4.\quad A complete four-rate numerical benchmark}
Take $p=65\cdot2^{239}+1$, a prime, and its subgroup of order $128$. Fix $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$ and any $m\ge1$. Then $B=65\cdot2^{111}$. The Proth certificate is $3^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\bmod p$. Table~1 gives the first failures. For example, at half rate and 62 errors MCA already fails, while every list remains within budget.

Rate & Dimension & First list failure & First MCA failure\\\midrule
$1/2$ &64&63&62\\
$1/4$ &32&97&All grid counts pass\\
$1/8$ &16&113&All grid counts pass\\
$1/16$ &8&121&All grid counts pass\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[4pt]

\textbf{List bounds.} At $t\ge k$ agreements, different interleaved words cannot share $k$ agreement sites with a center. Counting $k$-subsets gives at most $\lfloor\binom nk/\binom tk\rfloor$ words. For $k=64$, this is $\le B$ at $t=66$. Among the $\binom{128}{65}$ subsets of 65 sites, some $B+1$ have the same product since there are only 128 products and $\binom{128}{65}>128B$. Let $P_S=\prod_{i\in S}(X-x_i)$ and fix $S_0$ in that fiber. The distinct messages $(P_{S_0}-P_S)/X$ have degree $<64$ and agree with $P_{S_0}(x)/x$ on $S$. For $k=32,16,8$, already $\binom{128}{k}\le B$ at $t=k$, while all $p$ multiples of a locator on any $k-1$ sites agree with zero there. Scalar examples embed into all positive widths.

\textbf{MCA upper bounds.} On a bad witness $S$ with $s\ge k+1$ sites, $v|_S$ fails interpolation. At least $\binom{s-1}{k}$ of its $(k+1)$-subsets detect this failure. To see this, the matrix of columns $(1,x,\ldots,x^{k-1},v(x))$ has rank $k+1$, and every $k$ columns are independent. Induct on $s$ from $s=k+1$. If deleting a column drops the rank, every $k$ remaining columns make a basis with it. Otherwise each deletion has at least $\binom{s-2}k$ bases, and each basis survives $s-k-1$ deletions. The resulting bound suffices. On each detecting subset at most one scalar can interpolate the fold. Consequently at $s\ge67$, $k=64$, the bad count is at most $\lfloor\binom{128}{65}/\binom{66}{64}\rfloor\le B$. The bound $\binom{128}{k+1}\le B$ suffices for all radii at the other three dimensions.

\textbf{MCA lower bound.} Use the same product construction with 66-subsets in dimension 65. It yields $B+1$ polynomials $f$ of degree $\le64$ agreeing with one word $y$ on 66 sites, since $\binom{128}{66}>128B$. Their values are pairwise distinct at some $z\notin D$, by $64\binom{B+1}2<p-128$. Use the received rows $y(x)/(x-z)$ and $1/(x-z)$. At $\gamma=-f(z)$ the fold agrees with $(f(X)-f(z))/(X-z)$ on those sites. The second row cannot interpolate there in degree $<64$, because $(X-z)g(X)-1$ would have 66 roots while being nonzero at $z$. The distinct $\gamma$ give the required excess. Both half-rate boundaries $62/128$ and $63/128$ exceed the finite Johnson radius $1-\sqrt{63/128}$ and lie below the relative minimum distance $65/128$.

\section*{4.1.\quad A complete benchmark at length $2^{30}$}
Fix $n=2^{30}$, $p=11\cdot2^{125}+1$ and $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$. The integer budget is
$\lfloor p/2^{128}\rfloor=1$. A Lucas certificate proves $p$ prime. The unit
$20759207997786210761536297572531909971$ has order $2^{30}$ in $\mathbb F_p$.
Take its powers as the evaluation domain. Every entry below holds for every positive list width.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}\toprule
Rate & Dimension & First list failure & First MCA failure\\\midrule
$1/2$  & $536{,}870{,}912$ & $268{,}435{,}457$ & 1\\
$1/4$  & $268{,}435{,}456$ & $402{,}653{,}185$ & 1\\
$1/8$  & $134{,}217{,}728$ & $469{,}762{,}049$ & 1\\
$1/16$ & $67{,}108{,}864$  & $503{,}316{,}481$ & 1\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[4pt]

\textbf{List proof.} The minimum block distance is $n-k+1$ at every positive interleaving width.
Two words in the same ball of integer radius $e$ have distance at most $2e$.
Conversely, take two codewords at minimum distance and form a center by dividing their differing coordinates as evenly as possible. Both lie at distance at most $\lceil(n-k+1)/2\rceil$. Thus this is the first error count with list size greater than one.

\textbf{MCA proof.} At zero errors at most one scalar has a full-domain folded explanation without a joint one, since two such folded explanations can be solved for the two original rows. A two-spike received pair gives at least two distinct bad scalars at one error. Monotonicity extends the failure to every larger radius. The
\href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/d7087b90e15b9ccb4232d248c47061e49a06c074/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankProductionDomain.lean}{production theorem}
identifies both rank predictions with the numerical counts. Its symbolic evaluation avoids enumerating support families on this domain.

\textbf{Relation to Johnson and capacity.} Write $d=k-1$. The relative minimum distance is $1-d/n$ and the Johnson radius is $J=1-\sqrt{d/n}$. A boundary $e/n$ strictly exceeds $J$ exactly when $(n-e)^2<nd$, with $e=n$ for an all-safe answer. At length 128 both thresholds in Section~4 exceed $J$ at every rate. The three lower-rate list boundaries equal the relative minimum distance, while the half-rate list and MCA boundaries fall short by two and three errors respectively. At length $2^{30}$ in the budget-one field, the list boundaries are strictly below $J$. More generally this strict inequality holds at every length at least 16 and rate at most one half when the list budget is one.
The \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/d7087b90e15b9ccb4232d248c47061e49a06c074/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/RankJohnsonComparison.lean}{integer comparison and Johnson proofs}
apply the closed-ball endpoint convention in Section~1.

More generally, capacity has error count $n-k$. Any bad MCA agreement requires at least $k+1$ sites, since both rows interpolate on every $k$-subset. The MCA error is therefore constant from $n-k-1$ errors through radius one. Whenever MCA fails, its first failure is strictly below capacity. For lists, the worst ball at capacity has exactly $\binom nk$ words. With $\varepsilon<1$, the first failure is $n-k+1$ if that binomial fits the budget, and at most $n-k$ otherwise. These \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/9d7bba7b3f451feeba585d93d6f6452c27b2aaed/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/CapacityBoundaryReview.lean}{capacity comparisons} apply at every eligible rate and every positive list width.

At 128-bit tolerance under $q<2^{256}$, the largest dyadic length admitting both all-safe MCA and first list failure at $n-k+1$ is 128 at rates $1/2,1/4,1/8$, and 256 at rate $1/16$. At every larger length both binomial counts exceed the budget. The BLS12-381 scalar field realizes the stated outcomes at every eligible dyadic length up through each ceiling. The \href{https://github.com/SlopDotCash/proximityprize/blob/9603b9a0deef879bd9d4caf9c91383ea619e0303/ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/JointDyadicCeilings.lean}{sharp length theorem} constructs the smooth domains and proves the failures above the ceilings.

\section*{5.\quad A half-rate ceiling at length \texorpdfstring{$2^{30}$}{2 to the 30}}

\textbf{Relation to known results.} The sponsor's companion paper develops the challenge from proximity gaps and correlated agreement and asks what happens between the Johnson and capacity radii on smooth domains \cite{abf}.
