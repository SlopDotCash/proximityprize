# On 64 quotient sites, beating the first-prime quarter MCA attack requires only three heads

Put $n=2^{30}$, $s=n/64$ and $D=n/4-2$. On the first named subgroup suppose the selected polynomial witnesses have the form
\[
     A+zB+J(P+zQ)f_z(X^s),
\]
where the two rows $(A,B)+J(P,Q)f_z(X^s)$ have degree below $n/4$. An attack of this form improves 480946859 errors **if and only if three heads of this same form already do so**. There is no limit on the number of selected quotient polynomials, the locator $J$ or the received rows. Equivalently, an improvement exists exactly when a degree-$m\le15$ rational function on the first named $\mu_{64}$ has three fibers of total size $r$ with $2r-3m\ge37$. The existence of such a partial three-head pencil, and the unrestricted production threshold, remain open. [Three-head reconstruction](quarter-three-head-root-score.md) · [32-site four-head theorem and extraction](quarter-four-heads-32-fibers.md)

The argument uses a separate sharp bound. On $\mu_{64}$ in characteristic zero or in **either** named prime, suppose $P,Q$ are monic equal-degree grid vanishers of two disjoint sets. If $P/Q$ takes a third value at **seven** grid sites, both vanishers are even. Six sites are attainable with a non-even vanisher. This holds for every such degree and the third value may be chosen freely.

## Seven centers

For the named grids, constancy of two disjoint monic vanisher ratios at two sites transfers to the complex grid with the same exponents. Work there and let $w=1_{Z(P)}-1_{Z(Q)}$. Three centers of equal exponent parity already force both endpoint sets to be antipodal on the opposite parity. If both parities have at least three centers, the two sets are globally antipodal. Otherwise at least five centers share a parity; rotate to make them even. On the odd sites $w$ is already antipodally invariant. [Order-64 transfer](mu64-unit-lattice-transfer.md) · [Three-center theorem](quarter-dyadic-parity.md#three-equal-ratios-in-one-parity-class)

Use 48 signed variables: one for each of the 32 even sites and one for each of the 16 odd antipodal pairs. The variables at the five centers vanish. For each remaining variable use one row weight per site it represents. The sum of its weights is zero because the two endpoint sets have the same size.

Let $z$ be the primitive 64th root. For a nonzero difference $k=2^a u$ of site exponents, $u$ odd, factor $1-z^k$ as the product of $1-z^{u+64h/2^a}$ for $0\le h<2^a$. Fold each factor's index modulo sign to one of the 16 odd residues below 32. This gives an integer vector of exponents of the independent positive numbers $|1-z^r|$. At a center $x$, summing these vectors for $x-y$ against the site weights $w(y)$ gives a signature for $|P/Q(z^x)|$. Equal ratios force equal signatures. [Independence and signs](quarter-dyadic-parity.md#independence-within-a-real-abelian-field)

Half the five even exponents to get a five-set in $\mathbb Z/32$. Up to translation and odd multiplication there are 454 such sets. The latter operation permutes the primitive-unit indices and preserves all tested equalities. The verifier enumerates all $\binom{31}{4}=31465$ five-sets containing zero and marks the normalized images of every representative. At a representative, use the balance row and the 64 rows comparing the 16 entries at four centers with the first center. Set the five center variables to zero and compute the kernel on the 43 remaining variables modulo 101. Every actual integral signed solution is in this kernel. For a kernel permitting an asymmetric even-site pair, enumerate every free-coordinate choice from $\{-1,0,1\}$ up to overall sign, reconstruct the dependent coordinates, and retain only ternary asymmetric vectors. Calculate their **integer** signatures at every site outside the support, allowing all centers in the final count.

Both modulus 101 in forward coordinate order and modulus 103 in reverse order give 434 kernels permitting asymmetry, maximum relevant nullity 12, and 2199 surviving vectors across the representatives. The largest class of equal signatures has six sites. This proves the seven-center theorem. For sharpness take the two endpoint exponent sets
\[
 \{10,13,18,29,42,44,45,50,60,61\},\qquad
 \{11,12,22,27,28,30,43,54,59,62\}.
\]
Their ratio agrees exactly at the six exponents $0,4,8,16,36,56$ in the fiber of exponent zero on both named fields. The first set contains 44 but omits its antipode 12. Transfer gives the same example in characteristic zero. ## Four arbitrary quotient words

It suffices to work at the incumbent predecessor $e=480946858$, with $t=n-e$. Suppose none of the three-head quotient maps has score above 36. The exact three-head accounting then bounds the population assigned to any triple by
\[
             3n-6(t-1)+3D+36s=n-4.
\]
Indeed divide the common gcd out of its scalar differences. If their reduced quotient degree is $m$, there are at most $D-ms$ physical common-locator sites, and the pair-only incidences outside them are bounded by the three quotient fibers times $s$. The nonconstant direction costs at least one row degree. If a population is at most one, the other two give at most $2(e+1)$ labels, which suffices as well. A constant direction for a putative quartet is excluded by the same core-union and fresh-label bound as on 32 sites; that bound is independent of quotient size. Select exactly $n+1$ assigned labels for a putative quartet. Removing any one of its heads now gives $L_i\ge5$ for every head. [Three-head site inequality and small-population alternatives](quarter-four-heads-32-fibers.md#the-four-populations)

In the four scalar polynomials subtract the first and split out the common gcd of their differences into the locator. Let $m=\max\deg(f_i-f_j)\le15$. No four scalar values coincide at a quotient site. Count its four possible partitions as $a,b,c,d$, in order of block sizes $1111,211,22,31$; their sum is 64. Write $\ell\le D-ms$ for the number of physical locator roots. If $J_0$ counts the total fresh assigned incidences on the four heads, $C$ their total joint core size, and $u=\sum_i(t-1-c_i)_+$, then $J_0\ge n+1+5u$ and $C\ge4(t-1)-u$. Thus for any $0\le\eta\le5$,
\[
                   J_0+\eta C\ge n+1+4\eta(t-1).
\]
At an uncovered nonlocator site the fresh incidence caps in the four partitions are $4,3,2,2$. At a covered site there is at most one fresh label on the common direction and the largest matched block has size $1,2,2,3$, respectively. Thus the local charge is at most the greater of its uncovered cap and one plus $\eta$ times its largest block. A locator has cap $\max(1,4\eta)$. Start with $s$ physical copies per quotient site and spend at most $D-ms$ locator promotions on the largest positive gains. The checker tests these integer inequalities at the intersections of the listed affine bounds as well as at zero and five. Only sufficient exclusions are needed from this price test.

Let $\Delta=6m-b-2c-3d$ be the sum of the six edge deficiencies in distinct grid roots, so $\Delta\ge0$. Of the 64-site shapes, the only surviving $(m,\Delta)$ are $(14,0),(15,0),(15,1),(15,2)$ with respectively $1,6,3,1$ possible $(a,b,c,d)$. In particular every triangle of heads has at least one full edge.

Distribute $\Delta$ over the six edges and the $d$ triple sites over the four triangles. For a triangle let $b_0$ be its number of triple sites, $d'=m-b_0$, and $\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3$ its edge deficiencies. The full edge has exactly $m$ simple grid roots, hence the common gcd of the three differences is the simple locator of these $b_0$ sites. Removing it gives a degree-$d'$ rational pencil. Its three disjoint fibers on the grid have total size at least $3d'-\sum_i\delta_i$. This allows extra reduced roots at a previously common site. The resulting score $3d'-2\sum_i\delta_i$ cannot exceed 36 under our hypothesis. When $d'=0$ all reduced differences are constant. When at least two edges are full and $d'-\sum_i\delta_i\ge7$, the seven-center theorem makes all three reduced differences even. An odd $d'$ is then impossible.

The integer checker tries every distribution of both deficits and triple sites in all eleven shapes. A distribution is removed if some reduced score exceeds 36 or if a required even pencil has odd degree. In each of the **80** remaining distributions, at least two triangles have even reduced differences. This concludes the finite part without a search over received words. [Exact shape and triangle census](../../../scripts/probes/probe_quarter_four_heads_mu64.py)

For each of the two triangles, its three nonzero raw edge differences therefore transform under $X\mapsto-X$ by the same rational multiplier, the ratio $L(-X)/L(X)$ of its triple locator. The triangles share one edge, so their two multipliers coincide. Their union contains five edges and subtraction gives the same transformation for the sixth. Hence all raw differences transform by one multiplier $\rho$. Their common gcd is one; since $\rho$ times every difference is a polynomial, its denominator is constant. Degrees then make $\rho$ constant, and applying the involution twice gives $\rho=\pm1$. If $\rho=-1$ every difference is divisible by $X$, contrary to their common gcd. Thus the four scalar differences are even. The quartet descends to the order-32 quotient and its predecessor impossibility gives the contradiction. [Exact 32-site conclusion](quarter-four-heads-32-fibers.md)

For an arbitrary number of initial quotient words the same extraction argument as on 32 sites applies. If the projective direction is in $K(X^s)$, at most 64 fresh labels are possible on the core union; the bounds there still give $64+4h_1+2047<n$. Otherwise any extracted head with at least three selected labels is one of these quotient words. Four extracted heads have just been excluded. If there are three, their capacity is at most $n-4$ and at least five selected labels remain. Outsider packing gives one repeated additional head covering them all, again a forbidden quartet. Conversely a quotient three-head score at least 37 improves the predecessor by the locator and allocation construction, since $3n-6(t-1)+3D+37s=n-4+s>n$. The existing 32-site construction also belongs to the 64-site class.
