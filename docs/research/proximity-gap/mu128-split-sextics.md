# Full sextic pencils on the named 128-site subgroups

At either named prime, a rational function on \(\mu_{128}\) with three disjoint six-point fibers and degree at most six factors through \(X^2\). Explicitly, after moving the three target values to zero, infinity and one, the numerator and denominator are scalar multiples of monic cubics in \(X^2\). Every one of the three fibers is a union of antipodal pairs. On \(\mu_{64}\), **16** is the exact maximum of the useful coincidence count among four distinct cubics. On \(\mu_{128}\), four distinct sextics have at most **34** useful coincidences and four distinct septics have at most **38**. Here “sextic” and “septic” allow degrees below six and seven. ## The full sextic fibers

Write \(L_A/L_B\) for the monic locator ratio, absorbing leading constants into the third fiber value. A two-site comparison on \(\mu_{128}\) lifts from either named prime to the complex numbers. If the third full fiber has three even and three odd exponents, the three-center parity lemma applied in both directions forces both \(A\) and \(B\) to be globally antipodal. Thus their locators are polynomials in \(X^2\), and so is the polynomial defining their third fiber. That polynomial has six distinct grid roots and degree at most six. Its full root set is antipodal, making its even and odd counts both even. This excludes the proposed three-three split. [Transfer theorem](mu128-unit-ellipsoid-transfer.md) · [Parity lemma](production-ratio-lifting.md#the-parity-constraint-in-the-complex-field)

Rotate so at least four full-fiber exponents are even. As in the septic argument the supports are antipodal on odd exponents, the normalized locators belong to the even cyclotomic subfield, and any odd member of the third fiber brings its antipode. The six test sites therefore have one of the two shapes in the existing census. Use all **48,446** affine orbit representatives and the same 96 ternary variables, kernel equations and root-site exclusions. Search for ordinary positive and negative weights six. The exact enumeration yields **1,828** directed candidates. Direct evaluation at each named prime leaves **142** unordered triples of supports in this list; duplicates across representatives are removed. For each of the 142 triples the program checks antipodal closure of all three sets. Affine changes of exponent preserve that closure. Since every true candidate was represented up to such a change and the two-site ratio condition lifts in both directions, this proves the assertion for the whole subgroup. [Search and kernel equations](mu128-septic-six-site-fibers.md#exact-finite-search)

## Four cubics on the 64-site subgroup

For four words write \(C=\sum_x(\max_y\#\{i:f_i(x)=y\}-1)\). Six cubic pair differences give \(C\le18\). If \(C\ge17\), at most one unit can be lost to a missing pair root or to a site where distinct pairs coincide. In either case there are two triples of words whose pair differences each have three roots, pairwise disjoint within that triple. With one missing pair root choose the edge opposite the deficient one; the two triangles containing this edge are full and have no shared site. With all edges full and at most one possible triple match, choose two of the other triangles. A quadruple point alone would lose three units. These two triangles share a word pair.

Pull each cubic triangle back by \(X\mapsto X^2\) from \(\mu_{64}\) to \(\mu_{128}\). Its three full six-point fibers occur in the census above. Projecting their exponents modulo 64 thus enumerates every possible cubic triangle up to the affine exponent action. Attach both triangles to the same root set \(A\) for their common word pair. To do so, the verifier sends \(A\) to a canonical affine representative, then includes every stabilizer image of the two remaining supports in both orders. Subtract the first word and scale the second to be \(L_A\). If another word has roots \(B\) and meets \(L_A\) on \(T\), then its coefficient is uniquely \(L_A(t)/L_B(t)\) for \(t\in T\). Independently form both third words this way and count coincidences of the reconstructed quartet at all 64 sites. Translations of exponents scale the variable, so they preserve all counts. For the odd multipliers the final calculation evaluates each quartet at **all 32 primitive roots separately in each field**. This handles the simultaneous compatibility of the two scale factors directly in the named fields.

The maximum in this class is **16** in each field. The four words with sixteen distinct double sites on the order-32 subgroup achieve at least 16 on the order-64 subgroup by restriction to those sites. Consequently 16 is exact for all four cubics. ## Higher degrees

If four sextics had \(C\ge35\), at most one unit would be lost from the maximum pair total 36. The two-full-triangle argument again applies. Every difference in those two triangles is a scalar multiple of a polynomial in \(X^2\), by the full sextic classification. After subtracting one of the words, all four therefore come from cubics on \(\mu_{64}\). Their coincidence count is twice the cubic count, at most 32. This proves \(C\le34\).

For four septics let \(D=\sum_{i<j}(7-r_{ij})\), where \(r_{ij}\) is the number of roots of difference \(i,j\), and \(W=\sum_x(r(x)-c(x))\), with \(r(x)\) the number of equal pairs and \(c(x)\) the useful coincidences. Thus \(42-C=D+W\). Suppose \(C\ge39\). If a quadruple site exists, it costs three units, so \(D=0\) and all other sites cost zero. Subtract one word and divide the four words by the linear factor of the quadruple site. Away from that site there are at least \(39-3=36\) useful coincidences. This contradicts the sextic bound.

Otherwise at most \(W\) triples of words have an all-equal site. Call a triangle eligible when its ordered pair counts are \(7,7,7\) or \(7,7,6\), in any order. For \(D=0,1,2,3\) the minimum numbers of eligible triangles are respectively \(4,4,2,1\). For \(D=2\), a single edge missing two roots leaves two triangles avoiding it, while two edges missing one leave at least two containing at most one deficient edge. For \(D=3\), an edge missing three leaves full triangles avoiding it; if two edges are deficient, a triangle avoids the heavier edge and contains at most the lighter one. With three edges each missing one, the remaining three full edges cannot be pairwise disjoint, so two of them lie on an eligible triangle. The minimum in each case exceeds \(3-D\ge W\). Some eligible triangle has pairwise disjoint root sets, contradicting the exclusion of a \((7,7,6)\) fiber pattern. Thus \(C\le38\). The small-grid first error count admitting four words remains in \(\{87,88\}\). [Septic fiber exclusion](mu128-septic-six-site-fibers.md)
