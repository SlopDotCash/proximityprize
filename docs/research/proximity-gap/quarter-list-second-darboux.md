# Second-prime quarter-rate safety at the cubic interpolation pivot

At length $n=2^{30}$ and rate $1/4$, every closed ball through **547,807,172 errors** on the second named prime contains at most **2,147,483,648** words. This raises the first-failure lower bound from 543,870,915 to **547,807,173**. It applies on any injective grid and at every positive interleaving width. The previous normalized-curve proof at the same radius required a budget of $53n$. The exact named threshold remains open. If a list exceeds $2n$, a single linear factor $A+B V$ must then carry at least $M=2n-3L-b=2066952990$ sections. Retain exactly $M$. The closed-form argument supplies a first integral $T$ of ordinate degree $m\le(M-1)/(755385698-1)<3$. The separability comparison $6dM^2<p$ holds on the named field. Only the existence and degree of the integral are needed here. Write $F=-A/B$ in an affine ordinate chart. The given differential equation has $F=-T_X/T_Y$. At an ordinate ramification point $\alpha$ of $T$, of index $j$, write its local expansion in a constant target chart as $T=\beta+c(Y-\alpha)^j+\cdots$. The pole order of the vector field is at most $j-1$. This follows on differentiating the expansion with respect to $X$ and $Y$, since $j<p$. At unramified points it is regular. The same statement in reciprocal coordinates includes the source and target points at infinity. Riemann–Hurwitz on the ordinate projective line now bounds the total pole order by $2m-2\le2$. Since $A,B$ are coprime, their rational vector field has total pole order $\max(\deg_Y B,\deg_Y A-2)$. Indeed the finite poles sum to $\deg_Y B$ and the coefficient in the inverse ordinate $Z=1/Y$ is $Z^2 A(X,1/Z)/B(X,1/Z)$. A factor of total degree five has either $\deg_Y A=5$ or $\deg_Y B=4$. In both cases the pole order is at least three. This excludes the remaining factor and proves the stated all-center bound.
