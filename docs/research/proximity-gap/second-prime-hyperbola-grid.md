# A six-head half-rate MCA witness on the second prime

At length $n=2^{30}$ and dimension $k=2^{29}$ the second named subgroup has a received pair with **2,415,919,114 distinct bad scalars at 402,653,185 errors**. Its budget is 2,147,483,648. The same probability lower bound holds at every positive interleaving width and at every dimension from 536,870,912 through 671,088,638. This lowers the previous half-rate ceiling by 26,843,545 errors. The exact first failing count remains open. [Lean theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecondPrimeHyperbolaGrid.lean) · [Independent finite-field check](../../../scripts/probes/probe_second_prime_hyperbola_grid.py)

Write $s=n/16$, let $w=g^s$, and put $t=x^s$ at an evaluation point $x$. The six heads have coordinate pairs $(a,t/a)$ for $a\in\{1,w,w^2\}$ and $(t/b,b)$ for $b\in\{1,w^3,w^6\}$. Each pair lies on $uv=t$. One head from each family agrees when $t=ab$. The nine products are the distinct powers $w^0$ through $w^8$, each with exactly $s$ preimages. Distinct points of this hyperbola have distinct second coordinates. A line meets it in at most two points, so different points determine different slopes through a given owner. [Quotient and fiber checks](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecondPrimeSixteenFiber.lean)

Choose a common locator $J$ with $7s-2$ distinct roots outside the nine pair fibers. On each pair fiber, use the value of its coincident heads for the received pair. Assign the two leftover nonroot sites to the first head. Write each head as $(U_i,V_i)$ in the original coordinate $x$. For field parameters $c,d$ use the two polynomials $cJU_i+dXJV_i$ and $JV_i$. Their degrees are at most $8s-1$, inside the half-rate code. Five heads have $10s-2$ defining agreements and the first has $10s$. A foreign site adds one agreement for its folded scalar. Any joint explanation would already be fixed by the core and would contradict the second-row discrepancy at the foreign site.

For a foreign head $i$ at a site owned by $h$, that scalar is
$$
c\frac{U_h(x)-U_i(x)}{V_i(x)-V_h(x)}-d x .
$$
The coefficient of $d$ distinguishes sites. The coefficient of $c$ distinguishes foreign heads at one site. Each collision forbids one proper line in the $(c,d)$ plane, and the field order exceeds the number of pairs of proposed labels. There is consequently one choice of parameters giving every label at once. Each pair fiber supplies four labels per site, and each of the two leftover sites supplies five. The count is $36s+10$. The minimum number of agreements is $10s-1$, giving $6s+1$ errors. [Simultaneous label theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/PlanarQuartetMass.lean)

## How early a rectangular hyperbola grid can fail

The first over-budget counts from this construction at half rate are **357,913,942** and **402,653,185** on the first and second named primes, respectively. Here a candidate head needs at most one new agreement beyond its joint core. On ordinary sites the heads have forms $(a,T/a)$ and $(T/b,b)$ for distinct nonzero vertical and horizontal parameters, with a common polynomial quotient $T$ of positive degree $d$. The common locator has $r$ distinct roots. The linear site-separating shear imposes $r+d+2\le n/2$. This includes every rectangular subfamily and every choice of the polynomial, roots and ordinary received values. The unrestricted first-prime construction already fails by 313,174,699 errors. The unrestricted second-prime half-rate threshold remains open. [First-prime bound](rate-half-seven-twenty-fourths.md) · [Predecessor proof for grids](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/HyperbolaGridObstruction.lean)

Suppose $a$ vertical and $b$ horizontal active heads have at least $c=n-e-1$ joint agreements each, and put $m=a+b$. Each ordinary received point can serve at most one head from each family. A double agreement at site $x$ identifies one vertical parameter $A$, one horizontal parameter $B$, and a root of $T(x)-AB$. Each pair admits at most $d$ sites. Counting jointly owned sites gives both inequalities
$$
mc\le mr+2(n-r),\qquad mc\le mr+(n-r)+ab d.
$$
Each active head can supply at most $e+1$ one-fresh scalars, one per site outside its core. Dense subsets of heads may be considered separately, because deleting other heads preserves every retained core.

At the second prime, let $n=16s$ and take $e=6s$. Six active heads would have $ab\le9$, $c=10s-1$ and $r\le8s-d-2$. The first incidence bound yields $4d\le4s-2$ and the second yields $4d\ge4s+4$. Thus at most five heads are active and supply at most **2,013,265,925** labels, below the $2^{31}$ budget. The six-head construction above succeeds one step later. At the first prime and $e=357913941$, any three heads have $ab\le2$. The second incidence bound simplifies to $3c\le n+2(r+d)\le2n-4$, while $3c=2n-2$. At most two heads contribute **715,827,884** labels, below the $2^{30}$ budget. These bounds also hold at every smaller error count. [Polynomial incidence theorem and both label caps](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/HyperbolaGridObstruction.lean)

For the matching first-prime grid let $s=n/8$, use $T(X)=X^s$, one vertical head at $1$ and two horizontal heads at $1,w$ for a primitive eighth root $w$. The two pair fibers contain $s$ sites each. Choose $3s-2$ common roots outside these fibers. Allocate the remaining $3s+2$ ordinary singleton sites in quantities $44,739,244$, $178,956,971$ and $178,956,971$ to the vertical head and the two horizontal heads. The joint cores then have $715,827,882$, $715,827,881$ and $715,827,881$ sites. Every foreign site supplies a distinct scalar for its head through the same simultaneous slope-separation theorem as in the six-head construction. The resulting pair has **1,073,741,828** distinct bad scalars at **357,913,942 errors**, exceeding the budget by four. The construction works at every positive interleaving width.
