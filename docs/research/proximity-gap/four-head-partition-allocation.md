# Reconstructing the first-prime half-rate obstruction from four scalar polynomials

At the first named prime and half rate, for every error count from **268,435,457 through 286,331,153**, an unrestricted MCA overrun is equivalent to the following finite test. Choose four distinct scalar polynomials of degree at most **536,870,910**. Compute the sizes of the 15 possible equality partitions of their values on the actual subgroup. An integer allocation of those counts must give all four heads at least $t-1$ agreements and strictly positive excess in (1), where $t=2^{30}-e$. This covers 17,895,697 consecutive error counts. The test removes the received pair and decoding scalars from the search. The four polynomials are still unknown, so the numerical first failure is open. ## Four heads cover every witness

Set $n=2^{30}$, $d=n/2-1$, $e_*=286331153$ and $t_*=n-e_*=787410671$. Choose one original witnessing support and polynomial for each of more than $n$ distinct bad labels at $e\le e_*$. These same choices still witness failure at 312,400,000 errors. Apply the primary/companion classification at that larger error count to the chosen family. It puts at least 946,807,831 assigned labels on at least four collinear rich polynomial heads. Each rich head has more than 44,746 labels. This invocation uses the written geometric bridge in the larger-radius proof. The interpolation ranks and allocation prices used there passed the Lean kernel. [Classification and its verification scope](first-prime-half-blowup-climb.md)

If a head carries $L$ assigned labels and its full joint core has size $c$, each label needs at least $\max(1,t-c)$ fresh sites outside that core on its original support. A site outside the core gives at most one scalar to that head. Thus $L\max(1,t-c)\le n-c$. This gives $L\le e+1$ even when $c\ge t$, and $(L-1)(t-c)\le e$ when $c<t$. Every rich head has at least $C=t_*-7000=787403671$ core sites.

There are at most five rich heads. Six cores of size at least $C$, with pair intersections of size at most $d$, would give $48C\le20n+30d$. Indeed the site multiplicity $v$ satisfies $8v\le v(v-1)+20$, so summing and counting ordered pairs gives the bound. The left side exceeds the right by 214,412,398.

Order the at most five rich populations decreasingly and pad by zeros. The four largest are at least 189,361,567, 165,119,170, 124,715,175 and 43,907,185, respectively. For the $(i+1)$-st, use the ceiling of $(946807831-i(e_*+1))/(5-i)$. Their core deficits $(t-c_i)_+$ are therefore at most $1,1,2,6$. The union of even two full cores leaves at most $n-2C+d=35805393$ sites uncovered, fewer than the smallest displayed population.

Write the rich heads as one polynomial pair plus multiples of a common coprime direction $(P,Q)$ and let $s=\max(\deg P,\deg Q)$. A covered site giving a fresh agreement to a different head satisfies $P(x)+zQ(x)=0$. If the direction is constant, covered sites together account for at most one distinct fresh scalar. All the rich heads together then account for at most $1+5\cdot35805393<946807831$ labels. Hence $s\ge1$. For a fixed label $z$, its fresh agreements in the full core union use at most $s$ sites by polynomial root counting. If $t-c_i>s$, every label assigned to that head uses a site outside the union, different for each label of that head. This contradicts the uncovered-site bound for each of the four largest heads. Their deficits are at most $1,1,\min(2,s),\min(6,s)$.

Suppose a chosen polynomial at scalar $z$ differs from the specialization of all four top heads. Select $t$ of its agreement sites. Its intersections with the four cores have size at most $d$ each. Each of the six core-core intersections has size at most $d-s$, since two heads differ by the primitive direction times a nonzero scalar polynomial of degree at most $d-s$. The five sets, with site multiplicity $v$, obey $6v\le v(v-1)+12$. Consequently
$$
 6(5t_*-2-\min(2,s)-\min(6,s))\le12n+20d-12s .
$$
The left side exceeds the right by 10 at $s=1,2$, by 16, 22, 28 and 34 at $s=3,4,5,6$, and then increases by 12 per unit of $s$. This excludes every outsider. Assign all labels to those four heads. Each population is at least $n+1-3(e_*+1)=214748363$, so $2(L_i-1)>e_*$ and each core has size at least $t-1$.

## The 15 equality partitions and their exact capacity

Subtract one of the polynomial heads and divide its differences by the primitive direction. Bézout's identity makes the four scalar multipliers $f_i$ polynomials, pairwise distinct, of degrees at most $d-s\le d-1$. Coprimality ensures the direction never vanishes in both coordinates at a site. Thus two evaluated heads agree exactly when their $f_i$-values agree.

Let $N_\pi$ count the sites with equality partition $\pi$ of the four indices. At each such site choose a block $B\in\pi$ to match, or leave a hole. Denote the counts by $a_{\pi,B}$ and $h_\pi$, with $h_\pi+\sum_B a_{\pi,B}=N_\pi$. The core of head $i$ has size $\sum_{\pi,B\ni i}a_{\pi,B}$. An occupied site can yield one fresh label if at least two blocks exist. A hole can yield one fresh label per block. The excess of this capacity over $n$ is
$$
 \sum_{\pi:\,|\pi|\ge2}(|\pi|-1)h_\pi-a_{\{0123\},0123}. \tag{1}
$$
For necessity, take the actual matching blocks and holes of the original pair. Give each assigned label a fresh site. In a covered site every fresh label cancels the same direction, and an uncovered site admits at most one scalar for each distinct head value. Since there were more than $n$ labels, (1) is positive.

Conversely, realize any integer allocation by assigning that many sites of each partition to each block or hole. Use the four polynomial pairs $(Xf_i,f_i)$, whose degrees are at most $d$. At an occupied site with chosen value $w$, receive $(xw,w)$. Every nonunanimous occupied site supplies the distinct scalar $-x$ using a differing head. At a hole choose the received values $(u,v)$ with $v$ different from all four head values and $u\ne xv$. Every distinct head value $w$ supplies a different scalar $(xw-u)/(v-w)$. Choose $u$ to avoid all earlier labels and all ordinary labels. This forbids at most $16n+1$ possibilities, below the field size. For each label combine its fresh site with $t-1>d$ sites of its head's core. Any joint explanation would equal that polynomial pair on the core sites and disagree at the fresh site. Thus one received pair attains the allocation's exact capacity in distinct literal MCA-bad labels. Only univariate polynomial gcds are needed for the 15 counts. For a partition $\pi$ take the gcd of $X^n-1$ and the differences $f_i-f_j$ needed to equate each block. Its degree is $\sum_{\rho\succeq\pi}N_\rho$, since the domain polynomial is squarefree. Start with the one-block partition and subtract the counts of strict coarsenings to recover each $N_\pi$. The discrete partition contributes $n$ directly. After fixing the polynomials the remaining unknowns are nonnegative integers attached to these partitions. The probe compares the gcd calculation with direct evaluation. On the 16 nonzero base-field sites in $\mathbf F_{17^2}$ it allocates the four sextics from the punctured-list example and checks 18 distinct bad labels at five errors. On the known production septic tuple the 14 gcd calculations and allocation recover exactly 1,073,741,825 labels at 313,174,699 errors. ## Repair when some four-head cores are short

The reconstruction also gives an exact four-head test throughout the known half-rate first-failure bracket for families covered by four collinear heads of nonconstant direction. Suppose the heads cover $L>n$ labels and put $u_i=(t-1-c_i)_+$. Each assigned label on head $i$ needs at least $1+u_i$ distinct fresh incidences. Let $H$ be the number of holes of the original allocation and $M$ its capacity. Then
$$
 L+\sum_i L_i u_i\le M\le n+3H .
$$
Throughout the bracket, assigning a label to any head containing its chosen witness leaves $L_i\ge n+1-3(e+1)>3$ for every head. Thus $H>\sum_i u_i$. Use distinct holes to fill each deficient head's core up to $t-1$. Each new occupied site loses at most three units of capacity, and no other core loses a site. The repaired allocation has capacity at least $L$ and the construction above realizes it. The unrestricted conclusion in the first section ends at 286,331,153 errors. Later failures can involve additional heads or witnesses outside the four chosen polynomials.
