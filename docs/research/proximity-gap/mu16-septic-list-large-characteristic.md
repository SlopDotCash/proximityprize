# The half-rate list on the sixteenth roots in large characteristic

On the subgroup of order 16 in characteristic zero or characteristic $p>2^{109}$, every ball of five Hamming errors contains at most **three** polynomials of degree at most seven. Three occur, and a ball of six errors contains four. These statements hold at every positive interleaving width. Consequently the half-rate first failing error count is **six at budget three** for this entire characteristic range. In particular it holds at every prime field with $3\cdot2^{128}\le p<4\cdot2^{128}$ and $16\mid p-1$. On every eligible budget-three field the matching MCA first failure is **three errors**. Both named prime fields have the three-word maximum on their sixteen-site subgroups. The earlier campaign proved the five-error exclusion at one 130-bit budget-three prime. ## The equations on the five pivot sites

A hypothetical four-word list has a word with exactly five errors, since a ball of four errors contains at most one word by minimum distance nine. Subtract that word, leaving a center nonzero on a five-set $E$ and zero at the other eleven sites. Each of the three remaining words agrees with the center on either all five pivot sites or four of them. Fewer would require at least eight roots among the other eleven sites.

For a word agreeing on all five sites choose six of its roots outside $E$. Write it as $L_A a$, where $L_A$ is the monic vanisher of those six sites and $a$ is a polynomial of degree at most one. For a word agreeing on exactly four pivot sites choose all seven of its roots outside $E$. It is a nonzero constant times their vanisher. We call the five-site and four-site choices full and partial, respectively. A realization after a change of characteristic is allowed to agree also at an omitted pivot site.

For any two chosen words let $J$ be their common required pivot set, of size at least three. Their chosen root sets $A,B$ satisfy $|A\cap B|+|J|\le7$, since the words are distinct. In particular $|A\cup B|>7$, guaranteeing distinctness under any realization of these supports by nonzero words of degree at most seven. Cancel $L_{A\cap B}$ from their agreement equations. The two remaining locators have degree at most five, since $A,B$ both lie in the same eleven-set. At $y\in J$ the equation has at most four columns, of the forms $L_S(y)$ and $yL_S(y)$ up to sign. A partial word only needs the constant coefficient column. The pivot amplitudes must be nonzero at every site where they are used.

## A norm bound for all pair tests

Work in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ for a primitive sixteenth root, and let $\pi=1-\zeta$, of norm two. The valuation of $\zeta^i-\zeta^j$ at $\pi$ is $2^{v_2(i-j)}$ for distinct exponents. A locator column on $S$ can therefore be divided by $\pi^{v(S)}$, where
$$
 v(S)=\min_{i\notin S}\sum_{j\in S}2^{v_2(i-j)}.
$$
This only removes powers of two from the norm. A subdeterminant of size at most four uses at most four sites. In each embedding $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^t$, bound its column's squared Euclidean length by the sum of the four largest numbers $\prod_{j\in S}|\zeta^{ti}-\zeta^{tj}|^2$ as $i$ varies. This remains a bound if some entries are zeroed or multiplied by $y$. The product of these four sums for $t=1,3,5,7$, divided by $2^{v(S)}$, bounds the product of column lengths across all eight embeddings, using complex conjugation.

The exact interval computation enumerates the subsets of at most five exponents. Its base-two ceilings for one normalized column are 8, 15, 20, 24, 26 and 28, in degree order. Computing the fourth power before rounding gives the joint ceiling **109** for four columns. Hadamard's inequality shows that every nonzero pair minor has odd norm at most $2^{109}$. Thus all pair ranks and all of these determinant tests agree in characteristic zero and in every characteristic $p>2^{109}$, under any reduction onto the chosen sixteenth roots. ## Compatibility of three words transfers

Consider a fixed choice of all three required pivot subsets and root sets. For a full and a partial word, normalize the partial word's scalar to one. Its values determine the required affine factor of the full word uniquely on their four common pivots, by interpolation on any two. Its consistency is tested by the three-column minors above. The factor on an extra pivot must be nonzero. To check that test by rank, adjoin the row requiring the full word to vanish at that pivot, with its locator evaluated there and the partial coefficient set to zero. This is again a minor of the bounded type. For two partial words one common pivot determines their scalar ratio; the remaining comparisons are two-column minors.

For two full words consider the ratio of their locators at the five pivot sites. Either all ratios are equal or the ratios must be the five values of a nonconstant Möbius function. This follows because the two affine factors in a realization are nonzero on all five sites. Equalities between individual ratio values are tested by two-column minors. When all five are distinct the first three determine the unique projectivity, and the four-column minors test all remaining pivot equations. Its reduction at a prime above $p$ is a projectivity with the same prescribed values. Indeed a primitive two-by-two matrix having singular reduction could send the three distinct pivot reductions to at most two distinct values. In the constant-ratio case the two evaluation planes coincide. Two affine polynomials that coincide up to the fixed scale at two pivots then coincide at all five.

With at least two partial words, pairwise compatibility suffices. Normalize at one of the at least two pivots common to all three words. Partial words now agree on their overlapping pivots, and a possible full word is uniquely fixed by either partial one since there are at least two common values. Its two resulting affine factors coincide there and hence everywhere. Any pivot unconstrained by all words may receive an arbitrary nonzero value.

With one partial word, it prescribes both full words on the same four pivots. When their locator ratio is a nonconstant projectivity, agreement on those four forces their affine factors to be the factors representing this projectivity. Three distinct pivot/ratio values already determine it, so the fifth equality follows. When their locator ratio is constant, two common values suffice. Nonvanishing at the fifth pivot was included in the mixed-pair tests.

Finally suppose all three words are full, with locators $L_1,L_2,L_3$. Write $r_{ij}$ for the constant or Möbius interpolant of $L_i/L_j$ on $E$. The equality $r_{12}r_{23}=r_{13}$ holds as rational functions: after clearing denominators the difference has degree at most three and five distinct zeros. If a ratio is constant, a compatible full pair supplies the factors up to scale. When all ratios are constant one can take constant affine factors. If all ratios are nonconstant, $r_{12}$ and $r_{13}$ share precisely one of their zeros and poles in the following sense. They either have the same zero or have the same pole, since their quotient has degree one; they cannot share both. The three prescribed words are compatible exactly when the pole is common. In that case choose an affine form vanishing at the common pole (a nonzero constant for a pole at infinity) as the first factor, and multiply it by $r_{12}$ and $r_{13}$ to obtain the other two. A common pole cannot be a pivot, since the ratios are finite there. Conversely any simultaneous affine factors express both ratios with the same denominator. A shared zero or a shared pole persists under reduction because each nonconstant projectivity has good reduction, and the third ratio remains nonconstant. Thus their type, and consequently the whole triple's compatibility, is the same in characteristic zero and in every characteristic under consideration.

This transfers the full-support four-list decision in both directions. The published census excludes four at $p=(3\cdot2^{48}+69)2^{80}+1>2^{109}$. For a realization over an extension of this field, all unique scalar ratios, affine factors and projectivities used in the argument are already defined over the prime field. In the only free full case, all three locator ratios are constant, and constant affine factors realize the list there. Unconstrained pivot values can also be chosen there. Hence the prime-field exclusion suffices. The all-center test uses 147 dihedral representatives of the five pivot positions and all algebraically compatible projective line intersections, including coincident lines. It covers arbitrary center values by the support reduction above. A pivot with fewer than five errors is already covered whenever another word has five, and two words with at most four errors are impossible. ## The MCA boundary on the same input

For every budget-three field in the stated family, every received pair is safe through two errors and a pair fails at three. The all-domain staircase and spike theorem applies since $8+6\le16$ and $8+4\le16$. For an explicit witness, receive $(0,0)$ at twelve sites and $(-j,1)$ on the other four, using distinct field elements $j=0,1,2,3$. At scalar $j$ the zero polynomial matches the fold on its exceptional site and the twelve common sites. Both rows of any joint explanation there would vanish identically by the twelve ordinary sites, contradicting the exceptional row. For every other scalar any polynomial matching thirteen sites would be zero at at least nine ordinary sites, hence identically zero, and its fold matches at only twelve. This pair therefore has exactly four bad labels. The operational suprema for MCA and lists are respectively $3/16$ and $6/16$, with failure at the displayed closed radii.
