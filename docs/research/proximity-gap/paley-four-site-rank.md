# A four-coordinate ternary rank test equivalent to Paley discrepancy

Fix an odd prime $p$. For any $X\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ put $P_X=(\chi(x-y))_{x,y\in X}$, with $\chi$ the quadratic character and $\chi(0)=0$. Let $N(X)$ be the number of **unordered four-subsets** $T\subseteq X$ for which $P_T$ is singular modulo $3$. The following statements are equivalent.

* For every $0<\alpha<1$, there is $\delta>0$ such that for all sufficiently large $p$ and every $A,B\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ of sizes at least $p^\alpha$, the absolute quadratic discrepancy is at most $|A||B|p^{-\delta}$.
* For every $0<\alpha<1$, there is $\eta>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $p$, **every single set of the one size** $n=\lceil p^\alpha\rceil$ satisfies
  $$
    \left|\frac{N(X)}{\binom n4}-\frac14\right|\le p^{-\eta}.
  $$

In coding terms, form the ternary linear code generated by $[I_n\mid P_X]$ over $\mathbb F_3$. Start with its first $n$ columns. Swap the four identity columns indexed by $T$ for their corresponding four columns in the right block. The resulting $n$ columns fail to be an information set precisely when $T$ is counted by $N(X)$, by expansion in the retained identity columns. This is a sufficient rank statistic with fixed matrix entries and a prescribed distributional target. The operational Reed–Solomon cutoffs do not estimate these ternary minors. The uniform four-site count remains open. ## The signed four-cycle controls a prescribed rectangle

For $M_{xt}=\chi(x-t)$ on $A\times B$ define
$$
 G_A(t,b)=\sum_{x\in A}M_{xt}M_{xb},\qquad
 E(A,B)=\sum_{t,b\in B}G_A(t,b)^2.
$$
Write $a=|A|$, $b_0=|B|$ and $S=\sum_{x\in A,t\in B}M_{xt}$. Cauchy–Schwarz first on the $a$ row sums, then on the $b_0^2$ column pairs, gives $S^4\le (ab_0)^2 E(A,B)$. Expanding the four factors and interchanging the roles of rows and columns gives $E(A,B)=\sum_{x,y\in A}(\sum_{t\in B}M_{xt}M_{yt})^2$. These two sums of squares show that adding sites on either side only increases the energy. In particular $E(A,B)\le E(X,X)$ if $A\cup B\subseteq X$. [Kernel-checked rectangle inequality and monotonicity](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/PaleyBipartiteCrossRatio.lean)

On four distinct vertices $x,y,t,b$, the summand $M_{xt}M_{xb}M_{yt}M_{yb}$ is the quadratic character of the cross-ratio $(x-t)(y-b)/((x-b)(y-t))$. On the projective line take $v_x=(x,1)^t$ for finite $x$, $v_\infty=(1,0)^t$ and $M_{xy}=\chi(\det[v_x,v_y])$. In particular $M_{\infty x}=1$ and $M_{x\infty}=e=\chi(-1)$ at finite $x$. A projectivity multiplies each entry by one global sign and by one sign at each vertex, by taking determinants of its action on these lifts. All four signs cancel around a cycle. For a principal minor the transformed matrix is $\pm D P_T D$ up to reindexing, with diagonal $D$ invertible modulo three. Thus both $E$ and $N$ retain their values with infinity included. Every proper subset can be placed in the finite chart by choosing a pole outside it.

## Exact normalization and the four-site determinant

With $c=|A\cap B|$, the contribution to $E(A,B)$ from repeated vertices is
$$
 ab_0^2+b_0a^2-ab_0-2c(a+b_0)+3c.
$$
Indeed the terms with $x=y$ contribute $\sum_x(b_0-\mathbf1_{x\in B})^2$, and those with $t=b$ contribute $\sum_t(a-\mathbf1_{t\in A})^2$. Their overlap contributes $ab_0-c$. Every other repeated vertex lies on a zero matrix entry.

For four distinct sites choose one cross-ratio $\lambda$. Write $r=\chi(\lambda)$ and $s=\chi(1-\lambda)$. Permuting the four vertices gives the six usual transforms of $\lambda$ four times each, with coincidences counted with multiplicity. The character values on these transforms are $r,s,ers$ eight times each. Their sum is $24e$ if $r=s=e$ and $-8e$ otherwise. Thus $r=s=e$ defines a projective type independent of the labeling.

The integral determinant of the four-site matrix is $3-2(r+s+ers)$. In its permutation expansion the three double transpositions each contribute $1$. The six four-cycles occur in reversed pairs and contribute twice the negative of the three cycle signs. It follows that the determinant is $3-6e$ for the distinguished type, and $3+2e$ otherwise. At $p\equiv1\pmod4$ the two values are $-3,5$. At $p\equiv3\pmod4$ they are $9,1$. Exactly the distinguished type is singular over $\mathbb F_3$. [Lean determinant and modulo-three test for all six edge signs](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/PaleyBipartiteCrossRatio.lean)

Combining the two counts, for every $X$ of size $n$ the full energy is
$$
 E(X,X)=n(n-1)(2n-3)+8e\left(4N(X)-\binom n4\right).
$$
The formula includes small sets under the convention $\binom n4=0$ for $n<4$. It suffices to bound the upper deviation of $N(X)$ when $e=1$ and its lower deviation when $e=-1$. Nonnegativity of $E$ bounds the opposite deviation by $n(n-1)(2n-3)/32$, an $O(1/n)$ error in density. Direct integer determinants and Gram squares verified the full identity on all projective subsets over $p=3,5,7,11,13$.

## Proof of the uniform equivalence

Assume the rank condition, fix $A,B$ with $a,b_0\ge p^\alpha$, and put $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Independently sample $m$ sites on each side. Their expected signed discrepancy is $m^2S/(ab_0)$ by linearity. Some actual sample $A',B'$ therefore has at least the original relative absolute discrepancy. Enlarge $A'\cup B'$ to a set $X$ of size exactly $n$; the two samples may overlap. Monotonicity and the principal formula give
$$
 \left(\frac{|S|}{ab_0}\right)^4
 \le \frac{E(A',B')}{m^4}
 \le \frac{E(X,X)}{m^4}
 \ll n^{-1}+p^{-\eta}.
$$
For example, the discrepancy statement follows with $\delta=\min(\alpha,\eta)/8$ for all sufficiently large $p$.

Conversely assume discrepancy at exponent $\alpha/2$ with saving $\delta$. In the expression for $E(X,X)$ fix $y,t\in X$. Sum over the remaining $x,b$ by the signs of $M_{xt}$ and $M_{yb}$, omitting any zero. The inner sum $\sum_{x,b} M_{xt} M_{xb} M_{yt} M_{yb}$ is a signed combination of four actual subrectangles of $X$. Terms with both sides at least $p^{\alpha/2}$ total at most $n^2p^{-\delta}$ in absolute value since their areas are disjoint. Each other term is at most $np^{\alpha/2}$. Summing in $y,t$ gives $E(X,X)/n^4\le p^{-\delta}+4p^{-\alpha/2}$. The exact normalization then gives the rank criterion, for instance with $\eta=\tfrac12\min(\delta,\alpha/2)$.

## Where a bad rectangle forces bad inversion poles

For $t\in B$ let $g_t(x)=(x-t)^{-1}$ and omit $t$ from both inputs. Let $R_t$ be the largest $|S(g_t(A\setminus\{t\}),Y)|$ over all $Y\subseteq g_t(B\setminus\{t\})$. For $b\ne t$, the row at $g_t(b)$ in the inverted chart is $\chi(-1)\chi(b-t)G_A(t,b)$. To compute $R_t$ it suffices to separate the positive and negative inverted row sums and take the larger absolute total. Since $|G_A(t,b)|\le a$, this gives a bound on the original energy at the **specified partner poles**:
$$
 E(A,B)\le a^2b_0+2a\sum_{t\in B}R_t .
$$
The diagonal contributes at most $a^2b_0$, and in each off-diagonal row the sum of absolute values is at most $2R_t$. Lean verifies the inverse row identity including possible poles in $A$ and the finite bound. [Kernel-checked recovery at prescribed poles](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/PaleyBipartiteCrossRatio.lean)

In particular if $|S|\ge h ab_0$ and $h^4>1/b_0$, put $v=(h^4-1/b_0)/4$. The average $R_t/(ab_0)$ is at least $2v$. Each lies in $[0,1]$, so at least $vb_0$ poles have $R_t\ge v ab_0$. At every such pole the sign selection recovers a partner with at least $vb_0$ sites and relative discrepancy at least $v$ (using $a$ as the size upper bound even if the pole was removed). A power-saving bound on this **local average inside every prescribed $B$** would suffice. Averaging over all $p$ field poles can miss a whole polynomial-size partner concentrated among the exceptional poles. [The all-poles estimate and its range](paley-cross-ratio-moments.md)
