# Four saturated quarter-rate heads have no labelled projective involution

Let $n=2^{30}$, $k=n/4$ and $d=k-1$. Let the domain be the $n$th roots of unity in the second named prime field. At $e=n/2$ errors, suppose four distinct two-row polynomial heads $W_i=(A_i,B_i)$, of row degrees at most $d$, account for more than $2n$ distinct bad scalar labels for one received pair. Then every triple of heads is noncollinear over the rational function field. There is exactly one singly covered coordinate for each head. Every other coordinate belongs to exactly two head cores. The six sets of double coordinates have no common nonidentity rotation or reflection preserving their six separate pair labels.

More generally the same configuration cannot be obtained by pulling four affine heads back through a proper dyadic quotient and then applying a common invertible rational affine change of their two rows, even if that change has poles. The conclusion concerns four-head attacks at this single error count. The known unrestricted four-head attack on the second prime uses 565,631,854 errors. ## Equality in the four-head count

Write $t=n/2$, $C_i$ for the full joint agreement core and $c_i=|C_i|$. Assign each represented label to one head and let $L_i$ be the resulting counts. A head has at most $t+1$ labels. If $c_i\le t-2$, it has at most $1+\lfloor t/2\rfloor$, because each label requires at least $t-c_i$ distinct fresh sites and different labels use disjoint fresh sites on a fixed head. Overrunning $2n$ forces $L_i\ge t-2$ for all four heads, hence $c_i\ge t-1$. In particular $I=\sum c_i\ge4(t-1)=n+4d$.

Write $U=|\bigcup C_i|$. When all heads are collinear, the general inequalities $M\le4n-3U$ and $3U\ge2I-6d$ give $M\le 2n-2d$. Otherwise $I-U\le4d$. Equality is forced throughout: $U=n$, $I=2n-4$ and every $c_i=t-1$. The total number of mismatched head-site pairs is $4n-I=2n+4$, so at most three of these opportunities can be lost to repeated, nonexistent, or unused fresh labels. [Four-head counting inequalities](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md#core-multiplicity)

Suppose three heads are collinear and the fourth is off their line. If $r$ is the degree of the primitive direction, the bound $I-U\le4d-2r$ forces $r=0$. Equality in the pair-root counts for the collinear triple forces all three pair intersections to have size $d$ and the triple intersection to be empty. At each of these $3d$ distinct coordinates the third head differs from the matched pair by the same constant direction up to a nonzero scalar. All these head-site opportunities together yield at most one distinct fresh scalar. The resulting loss $3d-1>3$ excludes this case.

For a noncollinear triple the determinant of the three rows $(1,A_i,B_i)$ has degree at most $2d$. At a site contained in $j$ cores of the triple the determinant vanishes to order at least $\max(0,j-1)$. Sum over the four triples. A site covered by $m=1,2,3,4$ heads contributes respectively $0,2,5,8$. These numbers are at least $2(m-1)$, and the total degree bound is exactly $8d=2(I-U)$. Thus $m\le2$ everywhere. Every triple determinant has degree exactly $2d$ and its roots are simple, consisting exactly of the three labelled double strata for pairs within that triple. In particular the four determinants have no common root, even over the algebraic closure, and their leading coefficients are nonzero.

Let $s_{ij}$ count the double sites for pair $ij$. Every triangle of the complete graph on the four heads has edge-weight sum $2d$. Subtracting these four equations gives $s_{12}=s_{34}$, $s_{13}=s_{24}$, and $s_{14}=s_{23}$. The sum at each vertex is therefore $2d$. Its core size is $t-1=2d+1$, leaving one singleton for that head. If a head pair had constant projective direction, its $c_i+c_j-2s_{ij}\ge2d+2$ mismatched opportunities on its two cores would together yield at most one fresh scalar. Hence every difference vector has nonconstant projective direction. Since a pair difference vanishes on $s_{ij}$ sites, $s_{ij}\le d-1$. Each of the three opposite-pair sizes is consequently at least two.

## Rotation certificate from a single triple

Translate one vertex of a triple to zero, so its other two columns are $(a,b),(c,f)$. The determinant $P=af-cb$ has exactly $2d$ nonzero roots. Suppose that at each such $x$ the same labelled pair coincides also at $\lambda x$. The polynomial
\[
 Q(X)=(a(\lambda X)-\lambda^d a(X)) f(X)
      -(c(\lambda X)-\lambda^d c(X)) b(X)
\]
has degree less than $2d$ by cancellation of its top row's degree-$d$ coefficients. It vanishes on all $2d$ roots of $P$. Indeed the column that is zero, or the two columns that agree, have the same property in the matrix defining $Q$. Thus $Q=0$. At zero this gives $(1-\lambda^d)P(0)=0$, and $P(0)\ne0$. Therefore $\lambda^d=1$. On a dyadic root subgroup $d$ is odd, so no nonidentity rotation preserves all three labelled strata. This argument applies to each individual triple. ## The projective involution obstruction

Homogenize the two-by-three matrix $V$ of differences from the first head to degree $d$ on the projective line. Its rank is two at every geometric point. At a finite point this follows from the lack of any common zero of its three maximal minors: the fourth triple determinant is their signed sum. At infinity it follows from the exact determinant degrees.

Suppose a projective involution preserves every double stratum with its pair label. Every maximal minor of $V$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $2d$ whose simple zero divisor is therefore preserved. Over the algebraic closure the characteristic is odd, so conjugate the involution to $(X,Y)\mapsto(-X,Y)$. Each of the four minors is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $+1$ or $-1$. Their signed sum is zero, by the affine determinant identity. No two minors are proportional: their root sets intersect in just the pair stratum common to their two triangles, of size at most $d$, whereas each has $2d$ roots. If both signs occurred, projecting the signed sum to its two eigenspaces would give a nontrivial linear relation on a subset of at most two minors. This is impossible. All four signs are positive, since four odd minors would all vanish at $(0,1)$.

An even homogeneous polynomial of even degree has even vanishing order at either fixed point. Therefore none of these simple-rooted minors vanishes at either fixed point. In the new affine coordinate, every triple again has exactly $2d$ nonzero finite roots, grouped into its three labelled strata, and these are preserved by $x\mapsto-x$. Applying the rotation certificate would give $(-1)^d=1$, contrary to odd $d$. This rules out every projective involution preserving the six labelled double strata. A nontrivial rotation of a dyadic group has a power of order two. Each reflection $x\mapsto a/x$ already has order two.

There is also a direct argument for a rational affine change of a pulled-back configuration. Over the function field, write $V(X)=M(X)U(X^q)$ with $q$ even and $M$ an invertible rational matrix; arbitrary common translation disappears on taking differences. Then $V(-X)=R(X)V(X)$ with $R(X)=M(-X)M(X)^{-1}$. At each finite point, choose two columns of $V$ whose minor is a unit in the local ring. This identity expresses $R$ using only regular entries there, so $R$ is polynomial. Choose two columns with determinant of degree $2d$. Cramer's rule expresses each entry of $R$ as a quotient with numerator degree at most $2d$ and denominator degree $2d$, so $R$ is constant. The leading columns have rank two and $d$ is odd, forcing $R=-I$. Evaluation at zero gives $V(0)=0$, contradicting its rank there. This excludes common rational mixing without assumptions on its zeros or poles. The general unsymmetrical four-head case at this count remains open.


## An exact test for a proposed allocation

There are no free polynomial parameters once a feasible labelled allocation is given, apart from common translation and a constant invertible linear change of the two rows. To see this, set head zero to zero. Form the scalar linear system for three unknown polynomials of degree at most $d$ by requiring their four head values, including the zero head, to agree on every assigned pair stratum. The two actual rows $A,B$ are independent scalar solutions. For any other solution $u$, take the triple $0,1,2$. Its determinants with rows $(u,B)$ and $(A,u)$ vanish on all $2d$ distinct roots of the actual determinant for that triple. Their degrees are at most $2d$, hence they are scalar multiples of that determinant, including the case of a zero multiple. Cramer's rule makes $u$ a constant linear combination of $A,B$ on the first two columns. After subtracting that combination, its third column vanishes wherever head three meets any of its peers, on $2d>d$ sites. It is zero as well. Thus the scalar solution space has dimension exactly two.

Conversely, on the named field, existence of a four-head overrun at this error count can be tested using only a labelled site partition. Use four singleton sites, one owned by each head, and six disjoint pair strata of the sizes above. Keep an allocation if the scalar linear system has dimension two; take any basis as the two row polynomials. At each coordinate receive the assigned head value. Require that its full set of matching heads is exactly the prescribed set. Finally form the projective direction of each of the $2n+4$ mismatching head-site pairs. Keep the allocation precisely when these give more than $2n$ different directions. All three checks are invariant under the choice of basis. Choose a constant invertible row change that puts none of these directions at the missing affine parameter: the projective line over the named field has more than $2n+4$ points. Each direction then gives a finite scalar agreeing at the $t-1$ core sites and its fresh coordinate. Any joint explanation on this support would equal both original head entries on the core since $t-1>d$, and would fail at the fresh site. This verifies each label as literally MCA-bad. The scalar linear system and projective-direction deduplication therefore decide any proposed partition without a search over received rows or polynomial heads. The number of possible partitions is still very large.
