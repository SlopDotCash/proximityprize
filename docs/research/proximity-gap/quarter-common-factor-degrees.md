# Quarter-rate safety through 533,150,000 errors

At length $n=2^{30}$, dimension $n/4$ and tolerance $2^{-128}$, every injective domain over the second benchmark prime is MCA-safe through **533,150,000 errors** at every positive width. At this count every received pair has at most **2,128,352,044** bad scalars, leaving **19,131,604** below budget. The designated subgroup first fails between **533,150,001 and 553,648,129 errors**, inclusive. ## One resultant and one total degree

Write $e=533150000$, $t=n-e=540591824$ and $d=n/4-1=268435455$. For each bad scalar $z$ choose one witnessing polynomial $q_z$ of degree at most $d$. Mixed Hasse interpolation on the actual two received rows gives primary and companion polynomials of shapes $(36,72,75769)$ and $(71,142,3565)$. Each shape gives the order, maximum value degree and maximum total scalar/value degree. The two surfaces have 15,151,548 and 14,490,855,831 more coefficients than constraints. Both vanish identically on every selected section. [Received-row construction](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterCommonFactorDegrees.lean)

Assign each section to one irreducible primary factor that contains it. Work over the coordinate function field, with primitive polynomial representatives. Value-independent factors cost at most 75,769 labels in total. Take the product $P$ of the distinct assigned positive-value-degree factors that do not divide the companion $Q$. An empty product contributes zero labels. Otherwise their value resultant is nonzero, and every assigned label is a root because the specialized polynomials both vanish at $q_z$. The degree in the scalar of this single resultant is at most $75769\cdot142+72\cdot3565=11015878$. Degrees of products add. Lean proves this label bound for any primitive product with no common factor. [Resultant and degree comparison](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterCommonFactorDegrees.lean)

Call a polynomial affine head rich when it has more than $H=75769$ assigned scalars. Every such head divides both surfaces, since substituting $Y=A+ZB$ gives more roots than either scalar degree. Distinct heads give different monic linear factors. Each has at least $C=t-8192$ joint core sites by $(L-1)(t-c)\le e$ for population $L$ and core size $c<t$. Every bad witness needs a fresh agreement, so $L\le e+1$ also when $c\ge t$. For $j\ge3$ the heads are collinear over the coordinate function field because $3C>n+2d$. The integral and Cauchy core-union bounds from the preceding quarter proof, applied with this $C$, give the combined rich-head allowance. [Fresh agreements and union of collinear cores](quarter-transverse-tangents.md)

After $j$ rich heads, the remaining common factors have total value degree at most $72-j$ and **sum of actual scalar/value degrees at most $3565-j$**. Small polynomial affine heads contribute at most $(72-j)H$ labels. A nonaffine common value-linear factor of total degree $m$ contributes at most $17m$. Indeed, if its population $L$ exceeded $17m$, then $mn/L+3mn/(L-m)<t-d$ since $4n\le16(t-d)$. At least $t-mn/L$ sites are identities on the received line. Among the $m+1$ sections with fewest misses there, any three meet on more than $d$ identity sites and are collinear. For $m=1$, two sections already give the line. Substituting the resulting polynomial line into the factor gives at least $m+1$ distinct scalar roots, forcing the factor to be that line. This gives the combined nonaffine linear cost $17(3565-j)$. [Identity sites and affine extraction](hodge-section-variance.md#production-specialization)

## Price of a common nonlinear factor

Let $m$ be the actual scalar/value degree of the factor, $b_0$ its value degree and $W=36t-1-jd$. Each rich factor consumes at least $d$ coordinate/value weight, so $W$ bounds the remaining factor's weight. Use the range $2\le m\le12$ or $13\le m\le3565-j$. Write $M$ for its upper endpoint and $b=\min(72-j,M)$. Then $b_0\le b$. The geometric estimates discard at most $(4b-2)m$ labels and bound the losses and denominator degrees of the retained sections by
\[
\sum_z\ell_z\le mR,\qquad \sum_z c_z\le mH',\qquad R=(2b-1)n,\quad H'=(6b+2)W.
\]
Indeed $G=(2b_0-2)m-b_0(b_0-1)$, the ordinary incidence loss is at most $(G+m)n$, and the Gauss-graph bound for the denominator degrees is $(6b_0+2)Wm$. The named characteristic exceeds $2Wb$, the discriminant coordinate degree and the polar degree. [Gauss graph and finite pole multiplicities](gauss-polar-section-heights.md) · [Regular agreements](logarithmic-section-heights.md#ordinary-differentiation-at-a-repeated-received-root)

For $j\le3$ use the degree-$s$ tangent determinant with $s=1,a=1$ in the low range and $s=4,a=3$ in the high range. Use $p=\min(j,s+1,3)$ fixed rich heads among its $h=\binom{s+2}{2}$ rows. Their evaluation rows are independent. For degree one, a constant and a separating linear coordinate distinguish two points. For degree four, each of up to three distinct points can be separated from the others by a product of at most two affine linear forms. Homogenization gives degree four with the same affine evaluations. This argument includes collinear points.

A nonzero determinant requires a sum of at least $D=ah t-\binom{a+2}{3}n-s(s+1)(s+2)d/3$ in the row weights $a\ell+s c$. Each fixed head costs at most $8192a$. Remove every variable row with $(h-p)(a\ell+s c)\ge D-8192ap$. At most $(h-p)m(aR+sH')/(D-8192ap)$ rows are removed. Any $h-p$ remaining rows together with the $p$ fixed ones give a zero determinant. If the combined evaluations spanned the full space, there would be a basis extending the fixed rows by $h-p$ remaining rows. They therefore lie on a nonzero plane curve of degree at most $s$. Both ranges ensure $m>s(s-1)$, so reflexivity and Bézout bound the remaining tangencies by $s m(m-1)$. Dividing by $m$ and using $m-1\le M-1$ gives a uniform price per unit of actual scalar/value degree. [Veronese determinants and tangent duality](tangent-polar-windows.md#a-determinant-bound-for-the-tangents)

For $j\ge4$ put $a=\lfloor j/2\rfloor$, $u_j=\lceil(2ajC-j(j-1)d)/(a(a+1))\rceil$ and $g_j=t+u_j-n-d>0$. Each tangent outside the common line pays $c_z+\ell_z\ge g_j$. The retained tangencies on the common line cost at most $m(m-1)$ by the degree-one polar. The extra charge per unit of $m$ beyond discarded labels is thus at most $M-1+\lceil(H'+R)/g_j\rceil$. [Cleared determinant and individual tangent cost](quarter-transverse-tangents.md#cost-of-a-tangent-outside-the-common-line)

Take the larger per-unit price for the two ranges and multiply by the shared budget $3565-j$. Add the rich heads, scalar-only labels, the single noncommon resultant, the small polynomial heads and the nonaffine linear allowance. For $0\le j\le72$ the maximum is **2,128,352,044**, at $j=3$. The integer probe also checks the 250,559 actual-degree and head-count cases at their largest admissible value degree. Monotonicity gives safety at every smaller error count. The Gauss graph and plane duality steps are written proofs. Lean checks the two interpolants, the common-factor resultant test, the positive denominators and the finite population sum.
