# A degree bound for every component of the production fiber correspondence

Put $n=2^{30}$, $h=n/4$, and $H=\mu_n$ at either named production prime. A rational function $F$ with three complete, distinct $H$-fibers of degree $h-1$ or $h+1$ must have degree at least **9,085,508 in each coordinate on every irreducible off-diagonal component of $F(x)=F(y)$**. Thus there are at most 29 such components. Every nontrivial factorization $F=U\circ V$ over the algebraic closure must have $\deg U\le29$. At degree $h-1$ its proper outer degree can only be $3,5,15,29$. At degree $h+1$ it can only be 17.

A four-cycle in the rate-half successor list argument gives two complete fibers of size $d=h$ and $t=h-1$ sites of a third fiber at each vertex. At least two adjacent vertices must have maps without antipodal scaling symmetry. At those vertices the same component bound applies apart from one possible graph $y=c/x$. A right factor of degree two must come from this reflection. All larger proper right factors have degree at least 9,085,509. These conclusions allow irreducible correspondences of large degree. ## Counting the protected sheet pairs

Suppose $F$ has degree $d<p$, two reduced fibers of size $d$ in $H$, and $t\ge d/2$ distinct protected sites in a third fiber. Let $W$ be an irreducible off-diagonal component, working over the algebraic closure. Its generic sheets correspond to a monodromy orbit of ordered unequal pairs of the $d$ sheets of $F$. Transitivity makes this a directed graph of constant in-degree and out-degree $r$. The projections of $W$ to both coordinate lines have degree $r$.

Every full fiber supplies $dr$ distinct ordered pairs on $W$. To count the partial fiber, take a place of the Galois closure over its value and specialize the $d$ sheets at that place. Each point of the geometric fiber is hit, with ramification multiplicity. For every protected site choose one sheet specializing to it. Deleting the other $d-t$ vertices of the directed graph removes at most $2(d-t)r$ edges. The surviving ordered pairs specialize injectively because the chosen vertices have pairwise distinct specializations. Each specialization lies on the closed correspondence $W$. A point singular on $W$ has at least one place on its normalization. Consequently there are at least $(d+2t)r$ different places with $x,y\in H$ on the normalization.

A multiplicatively dependent component has a primitive binomial equation in the torus. Its equal bidegrees give the graph $y=cx$ or $y=c/x$. The protected pairs give $c\in H$. The graph defines a deck transformation of $F$. Any nonidentity scaling has even order dividing $n$, and the order of a deck transformation divides $d$ by the fixed-field theorem. Inversion has order two. Thus odd $d$ excludes both types. At even degree, if $F$ has no antipodal scaling deck transformation, it has no nonidentity scaling deck in $H$ because every nontrivial subgroup of $H$ contains $-1$. Two distinct reflections would generate such a scaling, so there is at most one. A scaling deck outside $H$ is also impossible when $F$ has a nonempty full fiber in $H$.

## Bounding the independent components

On the normalization of a nontoric component $W$, let $S$ consist of the zeros and poles of the two coordinates, and let $g$ be the genus. The bidegree gives $g\le(r-1)^2$, $|S|\le4r$, and hence $\chi=2g-2+|S|\le2r^2$. The functions $x^n,y^n$ are multiplicatively independent modulo constants. Their degrees are $nr$ and their differentials are nonzero since the coordinate projections have degree $r<p$ and $p\nmid n$. The normalization and the functions descend to a finite extension of the base field. Theorem 2 of Corvaja and Zannier bounds the degree of the common zeros of $x^n-1,y^n-1$ by
$$
\max\{(54 n^2 r^2\chi)^{1/3},12n^2r^2/p\}.
$$
Both named primes exceed $12n^4$, so the characteristic term is less than one. Writing $P=d+2t$, the protected places give $Pr\le(108n^2r^4)^{1/3}$. Thus $r\ge\lceil P^3/(108n^2)\rceil>8$ in the three applications here. [Corvaja–Zannier, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1](https://doi.org/10.4171/jems/409)

For the sharper estimate, assume $F$ has no nonidentity scaling deck. Normalize the image $W'$ of $W$ under the power map $(x,y)\mapsto(x^n,y^n)$. The degree $J$ of $W\to W'$ equals the order of the stabilizer of $W$ in $H^2$. Indeed the ambient power map is a Galois cover on the torus and the stabilizer of a component acts freely there. If $(1,b)$ preserves $W$, then $F(by)=F(y)$ at its generic point. The coordinate $y$ is transcendental over the constants, so $b=1$. Projection of the stabilizer to the first coordinate is therefore injective and $J\le n$. Positive-characteristic descent preserves this degree because the torus power map is étale.

The image coordinate functions $u,v$ generate the function field of $W'$ and have common degree $nr/J\ge r>8$. The covering of smooth projective curves is tame and ramifies only among the zeros and poles of the two functions. Removing all these places gives Euler characteristics related by $J\chi(W')=\chi(W)$. Pullback also multiplies the common-zero degree by $J$, since the two valuations are multiplied by the same local ramification index. Use Corvaja–Zannier's Proposition 2 with its integer parameters set to 8 and 2. The hypothesis $10nr/J<p$ holds and both coordinate degrees exceed the small-degree alternative. Its coefficients for the height sum and the Euler term are $(8+3\cdot2)/(8\cdot2+8+2)=7/13$ and $(8\cdot2+8+2-1)/2=25/2$, respectively. After pullback this gives
$$
 Pr\le\frac{7}{13}nr+\frac{25}{2}\chi(W)\le\frac{7}{13}nr+25r^2,\qquad
 r\ge\left\lceil\frac{13P-7n}{325}\right\rceil.
$$
For $(d,t)=(h-1,h-1),(h+1,h+1),(h,h-1)$ the ceiling is 9,085,508 in each case. The bound before replacing $\chi(W)$ also forces $g\ge1+r[(13P-7n)/325-2]$ on each such normalization. [Corvaja–Zannier, Proposition 2](https://doi.org/10.4171/jems/409)

## Right factors and four-cycle vertices

The coordinate degrees of the off-diagonal components sum to $d-1$. For any right factor $V$ of degree $m>1$, the off-diagonal equation $V(x)=V(y)$ contributes components of $F(x)=F(y)$ whose degrees sum to $m-1$. In odd degree all of them are independent, so $m-1\ge9,085,508$ and $\deg U=d/m\le29$. The divisors in the opening statement follow from $h-1=3\cdot5\cdot29\cdot43\cdot113\cdot127$ and $h+1=17\cdot15,790,321$, with the latter factor prime.

At a four-cycle vertex its two neighbor sets are the zero and pole fibers of $F$. If $-1$ is a scaling deck, both sets are antipodally symmetric. If both opposite vertices had this symmetry, the same private set of odd size would have two symmetric completions by different overlap sites. Deleting one element from a symmetric set leaves its antipode as the unique unmatched site, so the completion is unique. Hence a vertex in each opposite pair lacks this symmetry and the two selected vertices are adjacent. At either one a right factor of degree $m\ge3$ cannot have all its off-diagonal components in the single possible reflection graph, so $m-1\ge9,085,508$. A right factor of degree two with this graph generates its invariant field, equivalently it is $x+c/x$ up to target Möbius change.
