# Every two-locator ratio comparison on the 32nd roots lifts at both designated primes

Let $A,B$ be arbitrary disjoint exponent sets of the same size and let $x,y$ be distinct exponents outside $A\cup B$, all modulo 32. For every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{32}$ with $p>2^{150}$, the equality
\[
  L_A(\zeta^x)/L_B(\zeta^x)=L_A(\zeta^y)/L_B(\zeta^y)
\]
holds over $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when it holds over $\mathbb C$, using a primitive 32nd root in each field. Both designated primes exceed the bound. Common roots of $A,B$ may also be cancelled before applying the result, provided the two evaluations are defined. In particular every test for constancy of an opposite-edge locator ratio on a specified marked stratum lifts at this length. The computation uses integer inequalities for absolute norms. [Exact certificate](../../../scripts/probes/probe_mu32_odd_ratio_norm.py)

## Removing the power of two from the norm

In $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{32})$ put $M=L_A(\zeta^x)L_B(\zeta^y)$ and $N=L_A(\zeta^y)L_B(\zeta^x)$. Every factor of $M$ and $N$ is, up to a root of unity, $1-\zeta^j$ for some $j\ne0$. Its norm is a power of two by the value at one of the relevant dyadic cyclotomic polynomial. The sole prime above two in $K$ is generated by $1-\zeta$, of absolute norm two. After interchanging $A,B$ if necessary, assume $M$ has at least the valuation of $N$ there. Then $M/N$ is an algebraic integer because $N$ has no other prime divisor.

If $M-N\ne0$, every odd rational prime dividing its norm also divides the nonzero integer $|\operatorname{Norm}(M/N-1)|$. Bound that integer by $\prod_{\sigma}(1+|\sigma(M/N)|)$. If any reduction modulo a prime above $p$ gives equality, then $p$ divides this same integer. This is where discarding the power of two saves height.

## Maximizing over all root sets

Translate $x$ to zero and put $h=y-x$. The odd multipliers act by Galois automorphisms, so representatives for $h$ are $1,2,4,8,16$. Index embeddings up to complex conjugation by $j=1,3,\ldots,15$. For each eligible site $a$ let
\[
 t_{ja}=\frac{|1-\zeta^{ja}|}{|1-\zeta^{j(a-h)}|}.
\]
Then $|\sigma_j(M/N)|=\prod_{a\in A}t_{ja}/\prod_{b\in B}t_{jb}$. Expand the product of eight squared binomials in the norm bound. For a choice $k\in\{0,1,2\}^8$ the coefficient is $2^{\#\{j:k_j=1\}}$. The corresponding term before its coefficient is the product of $w_a=\prod_j t_{ja}^{k_j}$ over $A$, divided by the same product over $B$. Sorting the 30 positive values gives a uniform upper bound by taking the fifteen largest in the numerator and the fifteen smallest in the denominator. This covers every possible size because adding the next largest/smallest pair weakly increases the ratio up to size fifteen.

Each term can be bounded separately, then summed. The resulting certified integer upper bounds for the five shifts are strictly less than $2^{142},2^{133},2^{131},2^{132},2^{150}$, respectively. The following upper bound is the largest of the five:
\[
  1059817979418695800597244705707477435417014081 < 2^{150}.
\]
Thus no prime in the stated range can destroy a nonzero comparison. A zero comparison reduces to zero since all its factors are defined and its denominators are nonzero.

The verifier encloses twice each cosine by repeated signed square roots from $2\cos(2\theta)=(2\cos\theta)^2-2$, and encloses each positive chord by another square root. It uses scaled integers with denominator $2^{100}$, directed rounding, and integer square roots throughout. For each of the $3^8$ terms it sorts the upper and lower endpoints separately and rounds its final bound upward. Run `python3 scripts/probes/probe_mu32_odd_ratio_norm.py`. This transfer applies on the 32nd roots, including when they lie in a much larger field. The billion-site subgroup does not follow from the same numerical bound.
