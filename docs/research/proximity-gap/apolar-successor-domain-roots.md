# Removing fixed domain roots at the half-rate successor

Let $n=4h=2^{30}$, $k=2h$, and $e=h+1$. Suppose a fixed degree-$h$ apolar locator $f$ represents the two rows by degree-at-most-$(3h-1)$ numerators $N_i$ satisfying $N_i(y)=f(y)w_i(y)$ on the entire domain $H$. Write $C=H\cap Z(f)$ and let $c=|C|$. If $c<h$ and an affine decoder head witnesses five distinct bad scalars, then the whole stack has at most $n$ bad scalars. The argument permits repeated factors of $f$ at the points of $C$.

The [off-domain five-label theorem](apolar-successor-three-cosets.md#a-fixed-rational-pencil-with-a-five-point-secant-stays-within-both-named-budgets) proves the case $c=0$. Assume $0<c<h$, put $H'=H\setminus C$ and set $G_C=\prod_{y\in C}(X-y)$. Both $N_i$ vanish on $C$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $G_C$ gives $N'_i,f'$, representing the original rows on $H'$. A decoding polynomial $P_t$ at scalar $t$ has residual numerator $N'_0+tN'_1-f'P_t$ on $H'$, of degree at most $3h-1-c$. If nonzero, it forces at least $|H'|-(3h-1-c)=h+1$ errors outside $C$. Thus this witness agrees on all of $C$, with exactly $h+1$ true errors in $H'$.

At most one scalar can have its entire fold equal to a codeword on $H'$, unless both rows restrict to codewords on $H'$. In the latter case $|H'|-e\ge2h=k$, so every witness polynomial coincides with the known folded codeword on $H'$. For a nonjoint witness at least one point of $C$ on its agreement set must differ from its pair of known row polynomials. Each such point contributes at most one scalar, giving at most $c$ bad labels.

In the remaining case at most one scalar is exceptional on $H'$. At least four of the five head decoders therefore agree on all of $C$. Writing the head as $P_0+tP_1$, two such scalars imply that $P_0,P_1$ match the respective rows on $C$. If the possible exceptional scalar is on the head, its decoder equals the exact restricted fold (the agreement contains at least $k$ sites of $H'$), and agrees on $C$ as well. A fold that is a codeword on the whole domain allows no other bad scalar: it and any other folded decoder give a joint explanation by solving the two scalar equations. Thus all five head witnesses are nonexceptional.

Take degree-below-$c$ interpolants $S_i$ of the rows on $C$. On $H'$ replace the rows by $(w_i-S_i)/G_C$, the decoder by $(P_t-S_0-tS_1)/G_C$, and the dimension by $k-c$. The new rows have numerator $N'_i-f'S_i$ of degree at most $3h-1-c$ over the denominator $f$ of degree $h$, which has no root in $H'$. Any nonexceptional witness gives a shortened witness. A joint explanation for the latter would extend by multiplying its two polynomials by $G_C$ and adding $S_i$, giving both original rows on the original full agreement set. The five decoders still form one affine head after shortening.

For clarity, the five-label count on this shorter length $n'=4h-c$ retains the same $e=h+1$. In its proof $b=n'-e-|B|$, and $5b\le e+b$ still gives $b\le h/4$. At reduced numerator degree $j=1$, the disjoint-root case gives at most $n'$, and the singleton case gives at most $3(e+1)+1<4h-1$. At $2\le j<h/4$, the number of classes remains below $2h$ because $n'\le4h$, and the nonsingleton count is at most $n'/2<2h$. At $j=h/4$, the large baseline agreement still has $e+j$ sites and removing coordinates only decreases the squared-weight sum in Cauchy–Schwarz. The upper bound $194$ remains valid. In every case there are at most $4h-1$ shortened bad scalars, leaving room for the one exceptional scalar.

The case $c=h$ permits two different decoding polynomials on precisely $k-1$ restricted agreement sites. The above argument does not dispose of that case.
