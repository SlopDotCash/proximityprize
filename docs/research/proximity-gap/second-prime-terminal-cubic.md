# Excluding a cubic at the last second-prime predecessor

Let $n=2^{30}$, $d=2^{26}-1$, $t=357913943$, and $e=n-t=715827881$. Consider $2n+1$ distinct bad scalars over the second production prime, each with one chosen polynomial section of degree at most $d$. Work over the algebraic closure $k$ of the prime field. The total-degree-34 interpolant of value degree at most four vanishes at all these points over $k(X)$. We show that it cannot have an irreducible cubic-in-value factor. This establishes the cubic case by polynomial identities and finite-dimensional linear algebra. [Quartic interpolation](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecondPrimeQuarticInterpolation.lean)

Suppose it has such a factor $F\in k[X,Z,Y]$, chosen primitive over $k[X]$. The remaining value-linear factor can carry at most $e+1$ points if it is an affine head, or at most 184 if it is a nonlinear component. Vertical factors carry at most 34 labels. Thus
\[
 M=2n+1-(e+1)-34=1431655733
\]
points lie on $F$. Let $m=\deg_{Z,Y}F\le34$, and let $W$ be its weight with $\mathrm{wt}(X,Y,Z)=(1,d,0)$. At $h=2^{25}$, every interpolant of weight $<t$, degree at most $h$ in $(Z,Y)$ and degree at most five in $Y$ is divisible by $F$, since $34h<M$. The coefficient-count comparison with quotients of degrees at most $(h-3,2)$ gives $3d\le W\le290805098$. [Low-degree factor estimates](second-prime-quadratic-discriminant.md) · [Weight comparison argument](second-prime-persistent-singularities.md)

Use the generic fractional-linear change of $X$ from the persistent-singularity proof. At least $M-(5m-6)$ of the labels have a simple section, and in the new chart $F_z=(Y-q_z)G_z$ satisfies
\[
 \deg_X G_z(X,q_z(X))=J=W-d
\]
for every one of them. This chart preserves polynomial sections, their received agreements and the degree bounds. The case $F\mid H(F)$ for the reverse Hessian has at most three selected points. In what follows take $F\nmid H(F)$.

## A finite polynomial matrix for powers of the Jacobian ideal

Over $K=k(Z)$ homogenize $F$ to a binary form $\mathcal F$ of degree three in $Y,B$. Homogenize $F_X,F_Y$ to degree three as well, writing $\mathcal U,\mathcal V$. The latter homogenization includes a factor $B$. Let $g$ be the sum, over finite $X$ and projective $Y$, of the minimum of the two branch valuations of $\mathcal U,\mathcal V$ on $\mathcal F=0$, using local nonvanishing trivializations. It equals the sum of the lengths of the local intersections with one generic polar $\mathcal U+\lambda\mathcal V$ at their common base points. Indeed a generic linear combination attains the minimum on each of the finitely many branches, and the proper local intersection length is the sum of its branch valuations. This definition includes the point at $Y=\infty$ when it is a base point.

Choose a generic constant $\lambda$ and put $P=\operatorname{Res}_{Y,B}(\mathcal F,\mathcal U+\lambda\mathcal V)$. The index-sum bounds give $\deg_X P=D=6W-9d-3$ and $\deg_ZP\le H_p=6m-9$. The choice of the $X$ chart guarantees the equality in the first bound. Fix a positive integer $a$ and put
\[
 U_0=aD,\qquad v=3a+2,\qquad N=(v+1)(U_0+aW).
\]
Consider the $N$-dimensional $K$-space of binary forms of degree $v$ and $X$-degree below $U_0+aW$. In the natural monomial coordinates take these columns:

- $\mathcal F$ times $X^i$ and each binary monomial of degree $v-3$, for $0\le i<U_0$.
- $\mathcal U^j\mathcal V^{a-j}$ times $X^i$ and each binary monomial of degree two, for $0\le j\le a$ and $0\le i<U_0$.
- $P^a$ times $X^i$ and each binary monomial of degree $v$, for $0\le i<aW$.

Their entries are polynomials in $Z$ of degree at most $aH_p$. Their span has codimension at most $ag$. Here is a proof that includes the projective value chart. The third group implements exact reduction modulo $P^a$ in every binary slot, since $\deg_X P^a=U_0$. Over the resulting Artinian coefficient ring $A=K[X]/(P^a)$ all coefficients can be represented by polynomials in $X$ of degree below $U_0$. Over each geometric base point the binary form $\mathcal F$ is nonzero by primitivity over $K[X]$. A constant linear change of binary coordinates puts a common point avoided by all those finitely many root sets at infinity. The value-leading coefficient of $\mathcal F$ is then a unit in $A$. After dividing by that unit, its dehomogenization is monic cubic. Therefore the homogeneous degree-$v$ part modulo the first group identifies with the full cubic algebra over $A$; the homogeneous degree-two multiplier part also represents every element, by ordinary division by the monic cubic. Changing binary coordinates here only identifies the image dimension of the given columns. It is never needed when evaluating them on a selected section.

Modulo the first and third groups, the second group thus gives exactly the ideal generated by all products of $a$ members of $(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)$ in that cubic algebra. The remaining quotient vanishes wherever one of the two generators is a unit. At a common base point its local length is at most the intersection length of the curve with $(\mathcal U+\lambda\mathcal V)^a$, because that element belongs to the ideal power and the extra equation $P^a=0$ can only decrease the length. This intersection has length $a$ times the corresponding sum of branch minima. Summing proves the claimed codimension estimate.

Take independent columns spanning this image over $K$; call the codimension $c\le ag$. Seek a polynomial $R$ of $Y$-degree at most two, scalar degree at most $s$ and weight strictly below $aJ$. Embed it in the column space's ambient binary degree by replacing each $Y^j$ by $Y^j B^{v-j}$. This gives $(s+1)(3aJ-3d)$ scalar unknowns when $aJ>2d$. For every independent column introduce a separate scalar-polynomial multiplier of degree at most $s-aH_p$. Equality of the resulting vectors requires at most $(s+1)N$ scalar equations and introduces $(s+1-aH_p)(N-c)$ multiplier unknowns. These equations have a solution with $R\ne0$ whenever
\[
 (s+1)(3aJ-3d-ag)>aH_p N .
\]
Indeed the selected columns are independent over $K$, so a nonzero solution with $R=0$ is impossible.

This is an identity of polynomials in $Z$. There are no scalar denominators to clear. On a simple selected section both $F_X$ and $F_Y$ are multiples of $G_z(q_z)$, since differentiating $F(X,z,q_z(X))=0$ gives $F_X=-q'_z F_Y$. The homogenized resultant $P(X,z)$ is also a multiple of $G_z(q_z)$ by its product factor from $Y-q_z$. All columns consequently specialize at $B=1,Y=q_z$ to multiples of $G_z(q_z)^a$. The weight of $R$ forces $\deg_X R(X,z,q_z)<aJ$, so it vanishes identically. A nonzero $R$ of smaller value degree than $F$ can do this for at most $3s+2m$ distinct labels by the scalar resultant.

## The two numerical cases

Set $\eta=12000000$, $s=400000000$. If $g<3J-\eta$, take $a=40$. Using $H_p\le195$ and $N\le7043747106720$, the displayed dimension surplus is at least
\[
 (s+1)(40\eta-3d)-40\cdot195\,N\ \ge\ 56528137246257411>0.
\]
The scalar resultant bound $3s+2m\le1200000068$ is less than $M-164=1431655569$. This excludes the case.

If $g\ge3J-\eta$, write $\ell_x$ for the two-variable multiplicity of $F$ at the received point over $x$. The [branchwise Hessian estimates](second-prime-persistent-singularities.md) give a contribution of at least $8\nu/3$ at finite singular branches. At every received branch of multiplicity $r$ the contribution is at least $3\nu+r\ge8\nu/3+r$. At infinity the loss after dividing by the value-leading coefficient is at most $2(W-3d)/3$ summed over all branches. Therefore
\[
 \sum_x\ell_x\le 15W-24d-\tfrac83g+\tfrac23(W-3d)
 \le \left\lfloor\tfrac{23W-54d+8\eta}{3}\right\rfloor
 \le1053546217.
\]
Apply the [polynomial-multiplier interpolation](second-prime-intrinsic-cubic.md) at $\mu=64$ and the same $s$. The jet codimension sum is at most $(\mu-1)\sum_x\ell_x+n$. The auxiliary polynomial has scalar and value total degree at most $s$, value degree below three, and weight below $\mu t$. Its coefficient count exceeds the total net restrictions by at least $428325079668489781$. It vanishes on all $M$ points, but its scalar resultant has degree at most $3s+2(m-3)<M$. This also gives a contradiction.

This proves the cubic exclusion without a condition on its Galois group. The earlier quartic and low-degree cases are separate inputs when applying the exclusion to the full bad-scalar bound.
