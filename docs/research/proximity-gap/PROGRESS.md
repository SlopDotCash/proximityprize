## 2026-11-21 · An exact MCA threshold from Reed–Solomon shortening

This entry uses downstream source revision `a15abf509f39bf87781119462b81d9c5e08a6848`. The following is a mathematical derivation. Kernel verification will be recorded separately.

Let $n=2^{30}$, $k=n/16$ and $Q=2^{128}$. Let $p$ be prime with $n$ dividing $p-1$. Set $B=⌊p/Q⌋$, $N=2⌊B/2⌋$ and $s=N-n$. Suppose $0≤s≤⌊n/14⌋$. On a multiplicative subgroup of size $n$, the first bad Hamming error count at tolerance $1/Q$ is exactly $N/2$. The supremum of the good real radii is $N/(2n)$. The largest good integer error count is $N/2-1$.

**Good side.** Extend the evaluation domain by $s$ fresh field elements. Let $Z$ be the monic product of their linear factors. Multiply each received row by $Z$ on the old domain and set it to zero on the fresh coordinates. If a scalar has a bad witness on $S$ at $e=N/2-1$ errors, multiplying the witness polynomial by $Z$ gives a witness of degree below $k+s$ on $S$ together with all fresh coordinates. Any two polynomials jointly explaining those rows there vanish at every fresh point. Dividing them by $Z$ and cancelling its nonzero values on $S$ would jointly explain the original witness. Thus each bad scalar stays bad under a single extension of the received rows, independent of the scalar. The longer code has even length $N$, agreement at least $N/2+1$, and $8(k+s)≤N$ because $7s≤n/2$. The theorem HalfPredecessorRateEighthFullWiring.halfPredecessor_badScalar_filter_card_le_length then gives at most $N≤B$ bad scalars for every original pair.

**Bad side.** At $e=N/2$, PackingBudgetFirstJump.overlap_epsMCA_lower_bound_of_supply gives at least $2e+2=N+2>B$ bad scalars. Its hypotheses hold since $n≤2e$, $e+k+1≤n$, and $2(2e-n+2)≤p-n$. Monotonicity and constancy on each Hamming step give the threshold. The case $s=0$ recovers the known half pin. Positive $s$ gives a field window with thresholds strictly above one half. The window excludes the second repository prime with budget $2n$. The general Paley estimate and the remaining prize parameters are open here.

## 2026-11-21 · Scalar list budgets and a rate-half exact slice

The closed-ball list test has a dimension-free reduction. Write $q=|F|$ and $B=\lfloor q/2^{128}\rfloor$. For any linear code over $F$, any positive interleaving arity, and any one radius, the scalar and interleaved tests $\Lambda\le B$ are equivalent whenever $\binom{B+1}{2}<q$. This includes every $q\le 2^{256}$. Indeed, $B\le 2^{128}$, so for $B\ge1$ one has $B(B+1)/2<B2^{128}\le q$. For any $B+1$ distinct interleaved words in one ball, pick a distinguishing coordinate for each pair. The row weights identifying the symbols there form a proper hyperplane with $q^{m-1}$ points. Some row weighting avoids all of these hyperplanes and projects the entire list and its center into a scalar ball of the same radius. Every original agreement survives. Embedding a scalar list into one row proves the converse. This reduces the outstanding list question to scalar RS and leaves its worst-case radius to be determined.

A direct construction gives an exact slice with large block length. Let $n=Ms$, $k=bs$, $1\le b<M$, and let $D$ be a cyclic subgroup of order $n$. Its $s$-power map has $M$ fibers of size $s$. Set $H(X)$ to the monic polynomial vanishing on $s-1$ points of one reserved fiber. Use the single received word $H(X)X^k$. For each $b$-subset $T$ of the other $M-1$ fiber values use the polynomial
$$f_T(X)=H(X)\left[X^k-\prod_{a\in T}(X^s-a)\right].$$
The two leading terms cancel, leaving degree at most $(s-1)+(b-1)s=k-1$. Its agreement set consists exactly of the $s-1$ reserved roots and the $bs$ points in the chosen fibers. Distinct choices have different agreement sets, hence give distinct codewords. This proves a list lower bound of $\binom{M-1}{b}$ at $k+s-1$ agreements, independently of the field characteristic.

For $M=4,b=2$, the three candidates have distance $s+1$ from the center. At radius $s$, the minimum distance $2s+1$ forces every list to have size at most one, also after interleaving. Thus for a budget of two the last good integer radius is $s$, and the supremum of the good real radii is $(s+1)/(4s)$. At $n=2^{30}$ the prime $680564733906249808969588989158290882561$ has this budget. The exact radius pair is $268435456,268435457$. The repository's first sponsor field has a different budget. ## 2026-11-21 · Exact list of the geometric-sum center

Let $n=Mh$, $M\ge3$, $h+1>M$, and let a field contain all $n$ distinct $n$th roots of unity. For the code on $\mu_n$ of dimension $k=(M-2)h$, set $w=1+X+\cdots+X^{(M-1)h-1}$. Its **entire** list at $h+1$ errors has exactly $M-1$ words. At most $h$ errors gives an empty list for this center.

To see this, for a candidate polynomial $P$ of degree below $k$ let $D$ be the monic vanishing polynomial of its error locations. The polynomial $D(w-P)$ vanishes on the domain, is monic, and has degree at most $Mh$. Consequently $D(w-P)=X^{Mh}-1$ and $\deg D=h+1$. The leading $h$ coefficients of $w-P$ are all one. Comparing the top $h$ coefficients in the product forces $D=X^{h+1}-X^h+aX+b$. Its $h+1$ distinct roots lie on $M$ fibres of $x\mapsto x^h$, so two lie on a common fibre with value $t$. There $D(x)=(t+a)x+b-t$. Two roots force $a=-t$, $b=t$, giving $D=(X^h-t)(X-1)$. Distinctness forces $t\ne1$. Each of the $M-1$ choices $t\in\mu_M\setminus\{1\}$ gives a distinct error set and the polynomial $P=w-(X^n-1)/D$ has degree below $k$: polynomial division, or comparison of leading coefficients in $(X^n-1)/D$, cancels the top $h$ coefficients. At every point in that error set the complementary vanisher is nonzero.

For $M=4$, the previously constructed quarter-coset center $X^{2h}(X^h-1)/(X-1)$ differs from $w$ by $(X^{2h}-1)/(X-1)$, which is a codeword. Thus the center gives exactly three candidates at the first nonunique radius even at $n=2^{30}$. A fourth candidate at the same radius would require a different code coset. The global maximum for budget three still needs proof. `python3 -O scripts/probes/probe_geometric_word_exact_list.py` independently enumerates every possible agreement-set vanisher at $(p,n,k)=(13,12,4),(17,16,8),(97,16,8),(31,15,5)$ and returns counts $2,3,3,2$. The general argument above is a paper proof.

## 2026-11-21 · Uniform upper bounds for the actual periods at both prize primes

For $n=2^{30}$, every nonzero frequency satisfies $|\eta_b|<1059557319$ at the first prize prime and $|\eta_b|<1059633924$ at the second. These improve the trivial ceiling $n=1073741824$ by more than fourteen million at each prime. The bound keeps linear dependence on $n$, well above the conjectured square-root scale.

**Dyadic determinant bound.** Let $p$ be an odd prime, $H\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ a subgroup of order $n$, and $d$ a power of two with $2d\mid n$. Then for every $b\ne0$,

$$
\frac{|\sum_{x\in H}\exp(2\pi i bx/p)|}{n}
\le 1-\frac{8}{d}(2p)^{-2/d}.
$$

Choose $h\in H$ of order $2d$. For each coset of $\langle h\rangle$, absorb its representative into $b$ and let $v_i$ be the representative in $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ of $bh^i$ for $0\le i<d$. The opposite $d$ elements give conjugate phases. It is enough to bound both signs of the mean of the $d$ real cosines.

For the positive sign take $a_i$ to be the least absolute residue of $v_i$ and set $m=p$. For the negative sign take $a_i=2v_i-p$ and set $m=2p$. In either case the signed cosine is $\cos(2\pi a_i/m)$ and $|a_i|\le m/2$. Form the $d$-by-$d$ integer matrix of multiplication by $A(X)=\sum_{i<d}a_i X^i$ modulo $X^d+1$ in the power basis. Its determinant is nonzero because $X^d+1$ is irreducible over the rationals and $A$ has degree below $d$ and is nonzero. Its columns are the signed cyclic shifts of the coefficient vector.

Modulo $p$, each successive shift is $h^{-1}$ times the preceding one, including the wraparound entry because $h^d=-1$. For the negative sign all entries are also odd, so the columns agree modulo $2$. For each column after the first, subtract a multiple of the first chosen to match its proportionality constant modulo $p$, and chosen odd when $m=2p$. The resulting $d-1$ columns are divisible by $m$. Thus $m^{d-1}\le|\det|$. Hadamard's inequality bounds $|\det|\le(\sum_i a_i^2)^{d/2}$.

The chord bound $1-\cos(2\pi u)=2\sin^2(\pi u)\ge8u^2$ for $|u|\le1/2$ now gives a deficit of at least $8m^{-2/d}/d$ in the signed mean. Taking the weaker of $m=p,2p$ and averaging the cosets proves the displayed inequality. At $d=256$ the guaranteed saving is $2^{25}/(2p)^{1/128}$. Exact integer powers put this quantity in $(14184505,14184506)$ and $(14107900,14107901)$ respectively. The probe also enumerates both sign branches for all frequencies at $p=17,97$ and $d=2,4,8$. Banks and Shparlinski give related estimates using squared least residues and the geometry of numbers. Let $n$ be the length of an injective Reed–Solomon evaluation domain in a finite field, $1\le k\le n/16$ the dimension, $d=k-1$, $e$ the integer error allowance and $t=n-e$ the agreement threshold. ## 2026-11-21 · Three-list bound for shifted low-degree centers at rate one half

Let $n=4h$ be a power of two with $h$ even, let $p\equiv1\pmod n$ be prime, and evaluate polynomials of degree below $2h$ on $\mu_n\subset\mathbb F_p$. Suppose $p^{n/8-1}>(2h+2)^{n/2}$. If a received word is represented on the domain by $w(x)=A(x)/x^t$ with $0\le t\le h$ and $\deg A\le3h-1$, its list at $h+1$ errors has at most **three** words. This includes every polynomial center of degree at most $3h-1$. The preceding geometric-sum example attains three. At $n=2^{30}$ the numeric hypothesis holds for every $p\ge 2^{128}$, since $2h+2<2^{30}$ and $128(n/8-1)>30n/2$.

**Error moments.** Cancel common powers of $X$ from the representation first, so $A(0)\ne0$ if $t>0$. A center equal to a codeword has only itself at this radius for $h>0$. For each other candidate $P$, the nonzero residual $A-X^tP$ has at most $3h-1$ roots, while agreement supplies at least $3h-1$. Hence it has exactly that degree and exactly those roots, and the error set $E_P$ has size $h+1$. Its monic vanisher $L_P$ satisfies $L_P(A-X^tP)=c_P(X^n-1)$ with $c_P\ne0$. For $t<h$, the leading $h-t$ coefficients of $A-X^tP$ are independent of $P$, so its leading coefficient $c_P$ is fixed and polynomial division shows the top $h-t$ coefficients of $L_P$ are fixed. For $t>0$, the identity modulo $X^t$ gives $L_P/c_P\equiv -1/A\pmod{X^t}$. In particular the first $t$ coefficients of $L_P/L_P(0)$ are fixed. Newton's identities therefore make $\sum_{x\in E_P}x^r$ independent of $P$ for every nonzero integer $-t<r<h-t$, using reciprocal roots for $r<0$.

**Lifting moment equalities.** For any two candidates write $f_j=1_{E_P}(g^j)-1_{E_{P'}}(g^j)$ with $g$ a generator of $\mu_n$, and $F(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} f_jT^j\in\mathbb Z[T]$. The moment equalities imply $F(g^r)=0$ modulo $p$ for every odd integer in that interval. These are at least $n/8-1$ distinct primitive $n$th roots. For a complex primitive $n$th root $\zeta$, the integer norm of $F(\zeta)$ is the determinant of multiplication by $F$ on $\mathbb Z[T]/(\Phi_n)$. Modulo $p$, evaluation at the distinct primitive roots diagonalizes this matrix. Its rank deficiency therefore forces the integer determinant to be divisible by $p^{n/8-1}$ (Smith normal form). Every complex conjugate has magnitude at most $\sum |f_j|\le 2h+2$, and there are $n/2$ conjugates. The numeric hypothesis forces norm zero. Since $\Phi_n=T^{n/2}+1$, reduction of $F$ gives $f_j=f_{j+n/2}$ for every $0\le j<n/2$.

**Excluding a fourth word.** This equality says the indicators of every two error sets have the same imbalance on each opposite pair $\{x,-x\}$. Since each error set has odd size $h+1$, some pair has nonzero imbalance in the first one. The same point of that pair belongs to every error set. For distinct candidates the minimum distance $2h+1$ gives $|E_P\cap E_{P'}|\le 2(h+1)-(2h+1)=1$. Four error sets would thus leave four disjoint $h$-sets after deletion of their shared point, in a space of size $4h-1$. This proves the cap. `python3 -O scripts/probes/probe_geometric_word_exact_list.py` checks all $\binom{16}{5}$ supports at each $t=0,1,2,3,4$ over $p=2013265921$, and verifies the production exponent comparison. The proof applies to the stated class of centers. A bound for arbitrary centers or a four-word witness is still required to pin the global budget-three threshold.

**General center test.** For any received word, set $M=X^n-1$ and use its degree-below-$n$ interpolant. A nonzero $Q$ of degree $\le h$ exists with $A=Qw\bmod M$ of degree $<3h$, by $h$ homogeneous linear constraints on $h+1$ coefficients. For every candidate $i$ with error locator $L_i$, $c_i=L_i(A-QP_i)/M$ is a constant. At most one vanishes. For distinct candidates, $A_{ij}=(P_i-P_j)L_iL_j/M$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most one. Both divisions are exact by evaluation on all of $\mu_n$. Thus $c_iL_j-c_jL_i=-Q A_{ij}$. Picking $j$ with $c_j\ne0$ shows all locators lie in $\operatorname{span}\{L_j,Q,XQ\}$. A general $Q$ can have exponents throughout $[0,h]$; the preceding moment argument does not impose its dense-window conclusion on arbitrary $Q$.

A stronger norm estimate covers **every denominator of degree at most $h/2$** at $n=2^{30}$, $p\ge2^{128}$. Suppose $w=A/Q$ with $\deg A\le3h-1$, $\deg Q\le h/2$, and $Q$ nonzero everywhere on $\mu_n$. The same factorization of $L_P(A-QP)$ supplies fixed leading locator coefficients, hence equal positive power sums for $1\le r<h-\deg Q$. This gives at least $h/4$ odd frequencies. Complex Fourier Parseval gives $\sum_{j\text{ odd}}|F(\zeta^j)|^2\le n\sum_u f_u^2\le 2n(h+1)$. The primitive embeddings number $n/2$, so AM–GM gives $|\mathrm{Norm}\,F(\zeta)|\le [4(h+1)]^{n/4}$. ## 2026-11-21 · General rate range of the two-line argument

Here is a consequence of the two-line proof above for sequences of growing RS codes on arbitrary injective domains. Suppose the rate tends to $ρ>0$, the agreement fraction tends to $α<1$, and $α^3>ρ$ and $3α>1+3ρ$. For sufficiently large lengths the maximum number of bad scalars is at most $2e+2$, where $e=n-t$ and $t$ is the required number of agreements.

Choose a fixed integer $L$ with $2/L<3α-1-3ρ$. Any subfamily of at least $e+2$ selected scalars eventually contains $L$ points on a line. Otherwise, a uniform triple of distinct selected points is collinear with probability at most $(L-2)/(e)$ by uniqueness of the line through a pair. Its expected number of common agreements is then at most $k-1+n(L-2)/e$. The coordinatewise third-moment lower bound gives at least $(α^3-o(1))n$, a contradiction. Each of two such lines has at least $t-n/L$ common row agreements. Their cores meet in at most $k-1$ positions, and an outside point agrees on at most $k-1$ sites of each core. It therefore has at most $n-2t+2n/L+3(k-1)<t$ agreements. In a purported family of $2e+3$ points, first find one line and then find a second among its at least $e+2$ outsiders. Since each line has at most $e+1$ bad scalars, some point lies outside both. This proves the upper bound.

## 2026-11-21 · A prime-averaged sixth-moment bound at the fifth-power scale

Let $n\ge2$ be a power of two, $H_p=\mu_n(\mathbb F_p)$ for $p\equiv1\pmod n$, and $E_0=15n^3-45n^2+40n$. For **every finite set of such primes**, without any repeated-entry tests,

$$
The [previous FS6 count](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_FS6AlmostAllPrimesWickRung.lean) bounded the number of Wick failures beyond $P$ by order $n^5\log n/\log P$. The new bound costs order $n^4/\log P$.

**Proof.** Take every ordered sextuple of exponents modulo $n$, with positive word polynomial $F(X)=\sum_{j=1}^6X^{e_j}$. Negating one half identifies zero words with equalities of two triple sums. For a primitive complex root $\zeta$, the words with $F(\zeta)=0$ are precisely the pairable words, since $\Phi_n=X^{n/2}+1$. They vanish at every split prime. Their number is $E_0$. For any remaining word its norm $N_F=|\prod_{u\in(\mathbb Z/n)^\times}F(\zeta^u)|$ is a positive integer. For a split prime $p$ let $r_p(F)$ count the primitive field roots where $F$ vanishes.
