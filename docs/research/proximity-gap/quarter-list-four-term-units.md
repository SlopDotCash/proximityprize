# A finite bound on the quarter-rate list from the branch discriminant

At the first named prime, length $n=2^{30}$ and $d=n/4-1$, every closed ball through **538,870,914 errors** has at most **510,014** codewords. The proof applies to all injective grids and every positive interleaving width. At this fixed length the proof works in characteristic zero or in prime characteristic $p>50000t+1$, where $t=2d-2000000=534870910$. Both named fields satisfy this bound. In particular 510,014 is a valid list cap at this radius on the second named prime as well. Suppose there are at least $510015$ words. Use the order-eight primary interpolation, the quadratic variance estimate, the quadratic discriminant and the primitive midpoint from the two-million certificate. Set the large group threshold to $L=250000$, the required dominant group to $M=260000$, the quadratic quotient order to 6000 and the exceptional quotient order to 50000 with derivative cap 25000. The lower linear comparisons retain order 6000. All ordinary and mixed Bézout comparisons are checked with these constants. The univariate denominator case at $D<4d-1$ follows without an isolated-solution estimate: radial sites force $B(x)=0$, while $n-|J|>3d>E$. A derivative-independent factor contains at most its ordinate degree many words, totaling at most fifteen. Two groups of size at least $L$ are excluded; a sole large linear group must carry at least $M$ words.

In the finite checker, all degree comparisons use the same $n,d,t$. The log unit derivative degree bound from the previous proof is replaced by a comparison with the number of branch points, proved below. The radical, polynomial-intersection and common-height arguments use $1000n<p$, which follows from the stated characteristic hypothesis at this length. The exact comparisons give a squarefree harmonic quartic $B$, the primitive quadratic fixed divisor $Q$ of discriminant $\Delta$, the horizontal rational midpoint $r$, and the involution $\phi$ commuting with the equation. The latter follows independently from the threefold contact count on $\phi(f)$, with a minimum positive interpolation surplus. Write $a=\Delta/(4q_2^2)$ and $h=Y-r$. Thus $\phi(h)=a/h$ and the quotient ordinate $z=h+a/h$ has reference section $\infty$. Every $f\ne r$ gives a finite trace $z_f$. Two sections have the same trace exactly when they are equal or exchanged by the involution. Omit the at most two words lying on the fixed divisor $Q=0$. Since $M$ is large, choose six distinct finite trace sections from the remaining words.

Let $I_0$ be the quadratic invariant of $B$. At each grid site reserve $i(o)$ zeros of $I_0$ and $b(o)$ plane intersections of $A,B$, as in the midpoint proof. Put in $S$ every order-three site with excess in either reserve, every site of different order with a positive reserve, and every remaining finite abscissa where the invariant or the resultant has an unreserved zero. We may take the degree charge from the sum of actual finite intersection lengths. Outside $S$ a point is either an order-three clean fiber (invariant order one and entire finite intersection length five at the received point) or has invariant a unit and no finite plane intersections. The finite support satisfies $s=|S|\le A_S=\deg I_0+(4D+5E-20d)+9n-5Z$ using a degree upper bound for $\deg I_0$. Independently let $N_{\mathrm{odd}}$ count finite places at which $\Delta$ has odd valuation. At an order-three site with invariant order one the specialization has a triple finite root and a different fourth root, the Hessian content is a unit and $\Delta$ has a simple zero. Such points cost an extra invariant zero whenever they are absent from the order-three sites. Since $i(o)+\mathbf 1_{o=3}\ge o-1$ at all allowable orders, $N_{\mathrm{odd}}\ge Z-n-\deg I_0$. The checker establishes $N_{\mathrm{odd}}>8s$. The equality $C\Delta=4I_0$ used here follows from binary-quartic covariance.

## Distinct reductions on the quotient

At a finite place where $I_0$ is a unit and $A,B$ have no common finite zero, $C\Delta=4I_0$ makes $\Delta$ a unit. Over $R=k[[X-x]]$ the primitive matrix of $\phi$ squares to $\Delta$ times the identity. Choosing a unit square root gives complementary integral eigenprojectors because $2$ is a unit. In suitable integral coordinates $\phi$ acts by $v\mapsto-v$ and the quotient map by $v\mapsto v^2$. Hence two quotient reductions collide exactly when their representatives coincide up to $\phi$ on the special fiber. Distinct horizontal sections cannot collide at a regular ordinate with positive finite contact $j<p$: their right-hand sides then differ to order at least $j$, while their derivatives differ to order $j-1$. Polynomial $f$ and the rational $\phi(g)$ have contact bounded by their heights. At a finite collision on a pole of the differential equation, the regular solution $f$ would give a common zero of $A,B$. A collision between the finite trace of $f$ and the trace of infinity requires $f=r$ in the special fiber and is excluded the same way whenever $r$ is finite.

The two branch sections of the quotient are the images of the two fixed sections $Q=0$. They avoid every marked trace and the reference infinity at the same stable place. The fixed sections are locally unramified there, are horizontal, and reduce outside the roots of $B$ because a smooth harmonic quartic and its Hessian have disjoint roots. If the ordinate is infinity, smoothness and the fixed reduction give $B_4(x)\ne0$, so the equation is regular there in $1/Y$. Contact with a fixed section is bounded by the degree of $Q(X,f)$ when $f$ is finite, or by the order of $q_2$ in the infinity coordinate. All such contact bounds are smaller than the characteristic.

At an order-three clean point the midpoint is regular, $a$ has order one, and for any polynomial section $h_f=f-r$ has order zero on a miss and order one on a hit (the blown-up regular-ODE argument in the midpoint proof). Exactly one of $h_f$ and $a/h_f$ is therefore a unit. The quotient trace $z_f$ reduces to the value of this unit. The associated original ordinate is a nonhit; if two distinct trace orbits had the same value, their regular unit representatives would be distinct horizontal solutions colliding away from the received point. At a pole of the equation this would require a second intersection in the fiber, and at a regular point positive contact is impossible. Thus the finite trace reductions are nonzero and distinct, and both branch sections reduce to zero. The reference infinity remains distinct from the finite traces and both branch reductions.

Normalize two finite traces $z_0,z_1$ and infinity by $q=(z-z_0)/(z_1-z_0)$. At a stable place, whether a generic quotient coordinate uses this affine expression or the integral one above is immaterial: the unique projective change taking three distinct marked reductions to $0,1,\infty$ is integral with integral inverse when expressed in a good model. A direct proof uses the three pairwise unit determinants of primitive column representatives. At the other clean places the trace chart already has these three distinct reductions. Consequently each additional normalized trace $q$ and $1-q$ are units outside $S$ and infinity. In this coordinate let $F(T)=T^2-A_1T+B_1$ be the monic branch polynomial. Its two roots are finite and disjoint from every normalized trace at each place under consideration. In particular $B_1$, $C_1=F(1)$ and $D_q=F(q)$ are units outside $S$ and infinity.

## A four-term equation in units

Since $A_1=1+B_1-C_1$, three nonzero rational functions
$$
u=B_1/q,\qquad v=C_1/(1-q),\qquad w=-D_q/[q(1-q)]
$$
are units outside $S$ and infinity and sum to $1$. A proper nonempty zero subsum would require $q=B_1$, $q=1-C_1$ or $q=B_1/(B_1-C_1)$; if $B_1=C_1$, the last equality is impossible. The four available additional traces are distinct, so one of them supplies a nondegenerate equation.

Here the projective height of $[1:u:v:w]$ is the maximum degree after clearing common denominators and removing common polynomial factors. If $u,v,w$ are linearly independent over $k$, their ordinary Wronskian in derivative orders $0,1,2$ is nonzero. Indeed use one common denominator to obtain polynomials of degrees below $p$, change basis to polynomials with distinct leading degrees, and use the Vandermonde determinant of those three degrees. Multiplying every function by their common denominator scales the Wronskian by its cube, as follows from the product rule and triangular row operations. The initial coarse height bound needed here is harmless: $z_f$ has height at most $5n$ by the defining trace, its two branch roots have monic equation $z^2-4a=0$, and $a$ has height below $3n$. Write $h$ for the maximum of the reduced numerator and denominator degrees. The affine normalization gives $h(q)\le20n$. Each of $B_1,C_1,D_q$ is $(z_j^2-4a)/(z_1-z_0)^2$ at its corresponding trace, so its height is at most $33n$. Hence the heights of $u,v,w$ are at most $53n,53n,73n$, respectively. The projective height after taking a common denominator is at most their sum, $179n<p$.

For $H=\prod_{x\in S}(X-x)$ the quantities $H u'/u$ and $H^2u''/u$ are polynomials of degrees at most $s$ and $2s$, respectively, and likewise for the other units. This follows by factoring each rational function into linear factors and applying a second derivative. Apply Cramer's rule to the three equations obtained from $u+v+w=1$ by differentiating zero, one and two times, with columns divided by $u,v,w$ and the last two rows multiplied by $H,H^2$. The determinant and the three replaced-column determinants have degrees at most $3s$. Therefore $h([1:u:v:w])\le3s$.

If the span has dimension one, all three functions are constant. If its dimension is two, it contains $1$ and each function is affine in a single nonconstant rational $z$. The nonconstant affine functions must have at least two distinct finite roots as functions of $z$. Otherwise their sum must cancel, giving either a proper vanishing subsum or the contradiction $0=1$. Every preimage of those two values and every pole of $z$ belongs to $S\cup\{\infty\}$. Normalize the two values to $0,1$. For the normalized $y$ both $y,1-y$ are units, and $y'$ is nonzero by the initial height bound below $p$. The two-by-two linear system for $y,1-y$ with first row $(1,1)$ and second row $(H y'/y,H(1-y)'/(1-y))$ has nonzero determinant and degree at most $s$. This gives $h(z)\le s$ and $h([1:u:v:w])\le s$. The same two-unit reasoning gives $h(q)\le s$, also when $q$ is constant.

Take common homogeneous representatives of $[1:q:1-q]$ and $[1:u:v:w]$. Their products give $h([1:B_1:C_1])\le4s$. If these three coordinates are represented by $[a_0:b_0:c_0]$, the discriminant of $F$ is
$$
              [(a_0+b_0-c_0)^2-4a_0b_0]/a_0^2 .
$$
The numerator is nonzero and has degree at most $8s$. The branch roots before the affine normalization are $\pm2\sqrt a$, so this discriminant has the same square class as $\Delta$. Every finite place of odd valuation must be a zero of the displayed numerator, including those whose valuation is negative. This contradicts $N_{\mathrm{odd}}>8s$.
