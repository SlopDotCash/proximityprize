# Proximity Prize proof assessment, 2026-11-21

The general MCA and list-decoding thresholds remain open. The repository determines exact thresholds for specific smooth Reed–Solomon codes, including both challenges at all four rates on one length-128 code and all four MCA rates on multiple length-`2^30` codes. A different field of the same length can have a different answer. The sponsor asks for the threshold of the given code at rates 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16, with constant interleaving width for the list challenge. [Sponsor statement](https://proximityprize.org/)

## What the Lean declarations establish

Let `n` be the block length, `q` the field size and `B = ⌊q/2^128⌋` the allowed count. An exact first bad error count `e` means the largest good integer error count is `e−1`. With closed Hamming balls the good real radii end just below `e/n`, so the operational supremum is `e/n` and the test fails there. The sponsor describes a largest good real radius. An account for submission should state both integer endpoints and the full real-radius predicate explicitly. ## Remaining proof obligations

1. **Finish the boundary-prime half-rate result.** For every pair of received rows, prove that at most 268435458 scalars are MCA-bad at 268435455 errors. The general apolar count gives 357913942 at this step, which exceeds the required cap by 89478484. The written polynomial argument derives the tighter cap by interpolating a locator on reciprocal coordinate and parameter charts. The missing end-to-end Lean step is to derive the chart comparison hypotheses from the actual bad family and feed the resulting count into `RateHalfApolarThreshold.boundary_prime`. This would establish the fourth rate on that particular prime. [Polynomial proof](rate-half-apolar-polynomial-proof.md) · [Chart comparisons](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ApolarReciprocalCharts.lean) · [Threshold consumer](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/RateHalfApolarThreshold.lean)
2. **Close the gaps for the named primes and extend the field and domain coverage.** A candidate first bad error count requires a cap for *all* received pairs at the preceding step and one over-budget witness at the candidate step. For example, reaching the known second-prime rate-1/16 ceiling would require ruling out more than `2n` bad scalars at 715827881 errors. The first-prime rate-half witness still needs the production Lean assembly and a matching floor or a smaller counterexample. The older `31/64` predecessor cap already has a formal counterexample. [Second-prime incidence argument](second-prime-conditioned-cores.md) · [First-prime witness](rate-half-seven-twenty-fourths.md) · [Refuted predecessor cap](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_SYZ3OverBudgetStackWitness.lean)
3. **Determine worst-case list sizes on the remaining codes.** For a field of size below `2^256` the scalar and positive-width interleaved codes pass the same integer list budget. This reduces that part of the challenge to proving a scalar list upper bound for every center and producing an over-budget list at the next step. A polynomial upper bound with unspecified constants or exponents does not settle a given literal budget. [Projection theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/InterleavedListProjection.lean) · [Exact list examples and bounds](exact-list-profile-from-capacity.md)
4. **For the spectral approach, prove the right estimate and its incidence consequence.** The uniform prime-field subgroup cancellation estimate remains open. Its all-finite-fields version has a subfield counterexample. The current BGK consumer also assumes a worst-case bound on bad scalars above the Johnson radius. The workbench supplies no implication from its flatness conjecture to that incidence bound. Direct counting, as in the exact results above, is another route. [Subfield counterexample](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ShawFlatnessSubfieldCounterexample.lean) · [BGK and incidence hypotheses](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_PrizeFloorOfBGK.lean) · [Workbench](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/PROXIMITY_PRIZE_WORKBENCH.lean)
