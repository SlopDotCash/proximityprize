# A harmonic quartic and its differential involution at first-prime quarter rate

At **536,970,914 errors** on the first named prime, an oversized quarter-rate list would force at least **1,046,898,267** polynomial solutions of a single equation $g=A+B V=0$. Its denominator $B$ must be a squarefree harmonic quartic in $Y$. The unique projective involution fixing its two Hessian roots must commute with the differential equation. Both fixed roots are algebraic solutions. The logarithmic derivative argument subsequently excludes this configuration and proves safety through **538,320,914 errors**. ## The two invariants on the grid

Put $n=2^{30}$, $d=n/4-1$, $t=2d-100000$, and $K=k(X)$ for the algebraically closed scalarized coefficient field $k$. Apply the preceding proof with this $t$ through the reduction to one factor, the lower-weight exclusions, and the upper-weight exclusions. The verifier now uses the interval $5d-1\le D\le5d+4000000$. Set $l=4000001$. Thus the weight of $B$ is at most $E=4d+l$. At all but $T\le5999835$ sites the order is precisely three, by the same local intersection and quotient inequalities. At each such site translate the ordinate by its received value $y$ and write $B(X,y+S)=\sum b_i(X) S^i$, padding through degree four. In the uniformizer $U=X-x$ the coefficient orders at indices $0,1,2$ are at least $2,1,1$.

In ordinary coefficient coordinates the binary-quartic invariants are
$$
 I=12b_4b_0-3b_3b_1+b_2^2,\qquad
 J=72b_4b_2b_0+9b_3b_2b_1-27b_4b_1^2-27b_3^2b_0-2b_2^3 .
$$
Both are invariant under translation in $Y$, as follows by substitution. Their degrees in $X$ are at most $2E-4d$ and $3E-6d$, respectively. Every displayed term of $J$ is divisible by $U^2$ at an order-three site, so $2(n-T)>3E-6d$ forces $J=0$ identically. The cases $\deg_Y B\le2$ are already excluded by the fixed leading coefficient at the order-three sites. If the binary quartic has a double root and no triple root, moving the double root to zero leaves $b_2\ne0$ and $J=-2b_2^3\ne0$ there. This check is projectively valid: under coordinate inversion the two invariants are unchanged, under scaling they are homogeneous, and translations preserve them. These operations generate the projective changes of coordinate over the algebraic closure. For a quartic with a triple or quadruple root, all roots are rational over $K$ by multiplicity and separability. Write $B$ as its content times primitive linear factors with repetitions. The content has degree at most $E-4d\le l$. Off its zeros, every order-three hit gives at least three zeros with multiplicity of $B(X,f)$, since the specialized polynomial has a triple root and each linear factor vanishing there evaluates to a multiple of $U$. This contradicts $3(t-T-l)>E$. For a repeated cubic, viewed with a fourth root at infinity, the double-root test forces a triple finite root. Thus $B=C L^3$ for a primitive linear polynomial $L$ of weight at most $(E-\deg C)/3$. At an order-three site where $L(x,y)\ne0$ the ordinary multiplicity of $B$ forces a double root of $C$. Every hitting word would therefore supply at least $t-T-\deg C/2$ roots of $L(X,f)$, more than its degree since $\deg C\le d+l$. A zero evaluation would contradict $B(X,f)\ne0$.

It remains to consider the squarefree binary quartic, which includes a squarefree cubic with one root at infinity. Over a separable extension put three of its four roots at $0,1,\infty$. Substitution of $Y(Y-1)(Y-\lambda)$ into $J$ gives $(1+\lambda)(2\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)$ up to a nonzero sign. Its vanishing means the four roots are a projective image of the fourth roots of unity. In this case $I\ne0$ (in the displayed chart $I=1-\lambda+\lambda^2$). At each order-three site $I$ vanishes. If $b_3(x)=0$ there, the formula for $I$ gives order at least two. Consequently at least
$$
 G=n-\big[2T+(4d+2l)-n\big]\ \ge\ 1053742156
$$
sites have order three and $b_3(x)\ne0$. At each such site the binary form has a triple received root and a different fourth root, possibly infinity. Write $b=n-G$ for the bound on the other sites.

If $B$ is cubic, write its rational central root as $r=-b_2/(3b_3)$ using the **untranslated** coefficients. The identity $J=0$ gives $b_0+b_1r=2b_3r^3$. If $r=S/T_0$ in lowest terms, $T_0^2\mid b_3$ follows on clearing denominators. Hence $\deg T_0\le(d+l)/2$. If $u=\deg S-\deg T_0>d$, comparison in the identity gives $\deg b_3+2u\le E-d$. This and $2\deg T_0\le\deg b_3$ give $2\deg S\le E-d$; the same bound follows from $u\le d$ in the other case. Thus $T_0 f-S$ has degree at most $(3d+l)/2$. At all the good sites the center equals $r(x)$ by the triple root, and the denominator is a unit. Since $t-b>(3d+l)/2$, each candidate would equal $r$ identically, which is impossible for a group of distinct words.

## The involution sends each polynomial section to a rational section

Now $B$ has four distinct finite roots generically. Its affine binary Hessian, up to nonzero scalar, is
$$
                4B B_{YY}-3B_Y^2=C(X)Q(X,Y)^2 .
$$
Indeed the homogeneous second-derivative determinant transforms by the square of the determinant of a linear coordinate change. For $Y^4-Z^4$ it is proportional to $Y^2Z^2$. Unique factorization therefore gives the displayed identity in $k[X,Y]$, with $Q=q_2Y^2+q_1Y+q_0$ primitive over $k[X]$, and $C$ a nonzero polynomial. Weighted degree gives $\operatorname{wt}(Q)\le E-d\le3d+l$. At a good site, substitution of a binary form with a triple root gives a nonzero fourth power of its linear root form as Hessian. Thus $C(x)q_2(x)\ne0$ and $Q(x,Y)=q_2(x)(Y-y)^2$. In particular $Q$ has degree two generically and two distinct roots, by the two distinct roots of the generic Hessian.

Consider the involution
$$
         \phi(Y)=-\frac{q_1Y+2q_0}{2q_2Y+q_1}.
$$
Its matrix has trace zero and nonzero determinant. It specializes at every good site to the rank-one map with image and kernel the received point $y$. Exclude at most one polynomial word for which the denominator vanishes identically. On any remaining $f$ the unreduced numerator and denominator of $z=\phi(f)$ have degrees at most $3d+l$ and $2d+l$. They vanish simultaneously at at least $t-b$ good hits. The reduced numerator and denominator have degrees at most $d+u$ and $u$, where $u=l+100000+b\le24099669$. At every good site where the denominator is a unit, $z$ agrees with the received value. There are at least $G-(2d+l)\ge512871245$ such sites.

For a rational function $z=N_0/D_0$ with those reduced degrees, $D_0^6 g(X,z,z')$ is polynomial of degree at most $D+6u$. This uses $\deg_Y A\le5$, $\deg_Y B\le4$ and $D_0^2z'=N_0'D_0-N_0D_0'$. At every good agreement of $z$ with unit denominator it has a triple zero by the radial order of $g$: the formal substitutions $S=z(x+U)-z(x)$ and $V=z'(x+U)$ have $S/U$ and $(V-S/U)/U$ regular. Three times the agreement count exceeds $D+6u$, so $g(X,z,z')=0$.

Let $\delta=\partial_X-(A/B)\partial_Y$. We have just shown that $A(X,\phi(Y))+B(X,\phi(Y))\delta\phi(Y)$ vanishes at more than a billion distinct $Y=f(X)$ in $K$. Multiplying by $B(X,Y)(2q_2Y+q_1)^6$ gives a polynomial of $Y$-degree at most eleven, by the quotient rule. Therefore the identity holds in $K(Y)$ and $\phi$ commutes with the derivation. At a fixed point $c$ of $\phi$ the derivative in $Y$ equals $-1$. Neither fixed point is a pole of $-A/B$, since the roots of the Hessian of $Y^4-Z^4$ are distinct from its four zeros. Differentiating $\phi(X,c(X))=c(X)$ and comparing with the commutation identity gives $c'=-A(X,c)/B(X,c)$ for both roots of $Q$. Infinity is a horizontal section as well: in the chart $1/Y$ the defining vector field vanishes there since $\deg_Y A-\deg_Y B\le1$. Its image $r=-q_1/(2q_2)$ is therefore a rational solution. At every good site $r(x)=y$. The rational sections $\phi(f)$ have finite poles only at roots of the leading coefficient of $B$ in $Y$, a fixed set of at most $l$ sites. Indeed at a place where this coefficient is a unit, the right side $-A/B$ has pole order at most $m$ along a pole of order $m$ of a section. Its derivative has pole order $m+1$ since $0<m\le u<p$. The written argument leaves the resulting symmetric differential equation as the remaining case at this error count.
