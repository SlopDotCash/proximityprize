# A quadratic interpolation bound at the second prime

Put $n=1073741824$, $d=67108863$, $t=425022954$ and $e=n-t=648718870$. On every injective evaluation domain of length $n$ over a prime field of characteristic $p>2n$, dimension $d+1$, every received pair has at most **1867863086** MCA-bad scalars at $e$ errors. In particular the second prime, whose budget is $2n$, has operational supremum at least **648718871/n**. The established three-head upper endpoint is $715827882/n$. This is a written proof. The numerical checks use [the exact integer probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_second_prime_quadratic_discriminant.py).

## An interpolant of degree two in the folded value

Fix received rows $u,v$. For each bad scalar $z$ choose a witnessing polynomial $P_z$ of degree at most $d$ with at least $t$ agreements. Its witness prevents the joint pair from agreeing on the whole witness set. Work over the algebraic closure of the prime field, retaining the original scalars. Put $h=2097152$. Seek a nonzero polynomial

$$
Q(X,Y,Z)=a(X,Z)Y^2+b(X,Z)Y+c(X,Z)
$$

with monomials $X^iY^j Z^l$ satisfying $0≤j≤2, i+dj<t, j+l≤ h$. There are $3ht-(3h-2)d$ monomials. The identities $Q(x,u(x)+Zv(x),Z)=0$ for all $n$ domain sites impose at most $n(h+1)$ homogeneous linear constraints. The surplus is 2097150. At any chosen scalar $z$, $Q(X,P_z(X),z)$ has degree less than $t$ and vanishes at $t$ sites. Hence it is identically zero. We only use this last identity below. Any common factor of all coefficients depending on $X$ alone may be divided out, preserving the identities for every $P_z$.

## Quadratic fibers and the Sylvester determinant

First suppose $a≠0$. Write $Δ=b^2-4ac$, of $Z$-degree at most $2h$ and $X$-degree at most $2t-2d-2<p$. The exceptional $z$ for which $a(X,z)$ is the zero polynomial number at most $h$. If $Δ≠0$, unique factorization gives $Δ=s(Z)V(X,Z)^2 H(X,Z)$, with $s$ squarefree and $H$ the product of the distinct odd-multiplicity irreducibles that depend on $X$. Its $X$-squarefreeness over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Z)$ follows from Gauss's lemma and the characteristic bound. All three factors are polynomials.

If $r=\deg_X H>0$, the polynomial $S(Z)=\operatorname{Res}_X(H,\partial_X H)$ is nonzero, of degree at most $(2r-1)\deg_Z H$. Discard scalars where $a(X,z)$ or $V(X,z)$ is the zero polynomial, $s(z)=0$, or the $X$-leading coefficient of $H$ vanishes. These cost at most $h+h+2h+2h=6h$, by taking one nonzero coefficient of every nonzero polynomial whose entire specialization vanishes. For any remaining witness,
$$ (2a(X,z)P_z(X)+b(X,z))^2 = Δ(X,z), $$
so $H(X,z)$ is a square up to a nonzero scalar. Its $r$ roots counted with multiplicities occupy at most $r/2$ distinct sites over the algebraic closure. The specialized Sylvester matrix of $H,H_X$ has corank at least $r/2$, since its corank equals the degree of their gcd and $0<r<p$. A polynomial matrix losing rank by $k$ at $z$ has determinant divisible by $(Z-z)^k$: use a constant invertible row operation making $k$ rows zero at $z$, then divide those rows by $Z-z$. Comparison with $\deg S$ gives at most $4\deg_Z H≤8h$ additional witnesses. Thus $16h$ already bounds the entire count in this case.

Otherwise absorb a constant $H$ into the factorization. For $Δ=0$ use $s=1,V=0$. Away from at most $h$ scalars where $a(X,z)$ is identically zero, attach to each chosen witness a $λ$ with
$$ λ^2=s(z),\qquad 2a(X,z)P_z(X)+b(X,z)=λ V(X,z). $$
Such a sign exists by the difference-of-squares identity over a field, including at a zero of $s$. If $s$ is constant there are at most two components, one for each constant sign. If $s$ is nonconstant then $λ^2=s(Z)$ is irreducible over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Z)$ and there is a single component. Each selected scalar occurs just once.

## From a component to a secant

Consider a component carrying $M≥e+2$ chosen witnesses. Remove the domain sites where $a(x,Z)$ is identically zero. There are at most $t-2d-1$ of these since a nonzero coefficient of $a$ as a polynomial in $Z$ has that $X$-degree bound. At each surviving site put
$$ F_x(Z)=2a(x,Z)(u(x)+Zv(x))+b(x,Z). $$
Let $U$ consist of those surviving sites where $F_x=λ V(x,Z)$ is an identity on the component. At any other surviving site this equality can hold for at most $2h+2$ of the chosen scalars. For a constant sign the difference is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $h+1$. In the irreducible case its norm $F_x^2-sV(x,Z)^2$ is nonzero and has degree at most $2h+2$. Actual agreement always implies this equality. Thus
$$ |U|\ge 2d+1-\left\lceil(2h+2)n/M\right\rceil \ge 127275425. $$
At a site of $U$, any chosen witness with $a(x,z)≠0$ actually agrees. As $a(x,Z)$ is nonzero there, the total count of misses across these sites and all $M$ witnesses is at most $hn$. Put $L=8388607$. At most $\lfloor hn/(L+1)\rfloor=268435456$ witnesses miss more than $L$ sites of $U$. Any three of the remaining witnesses agree jointly with their respective folds on more than $d$ common sites, because $127275425-3L>d$. Fix two remaining points $(z,P_z)$ and interpolate a polynomial secant $A+ZB$ through them. The difference between the third polynomial and its secant value has degree at most $d$ and vanishes on these common sites, so the third point belongs to the secant. The chosen two suffice for all remaining points. If there are fewer than three, the same cardinality bound is immediate.

A secant carries at most $e+1$ bad scalars. If its joint core $C={x:(A(x),B(x))=(u(x),v(x))}$ has at least $t-1$ sites, each bad witness needs one fresh agreement outside $C$, where at most one scalar can agree. This gives $n-|C|≤e+1$. If $|C|<t-1$, every scalar on the secant needs at least $t-|C|$ fresh sites, disjoint between scalars, hence their number is at most $1+\lfloor e/(t-|C|)\rfloor$. This proves $M≤e+1+268435456$ on each component, a bound that also covers $M<e+2$. Two components and the generous exceptional allowance $16h$ give 1867863086 bad scalars.

If the interpolant has degree one in $Y$, write it $bY+c$ and divide out any common factors in $X$ alone. If $b(x,Z)$ vanishes identically at a site, $c(x,Z)$ must be nonzero there as a polynomial in $Z$. Then $c(x,z)=0$ for every witness by the polynomial identity, giving at most $h$ witnesses. Otherwise use all sites and let $U$ be those where $b(x,Z)(u(x)+Zv(x))+c(x,Z)$ vanishes identically. At a site outside $U$ there are at most $h+1$ agreeing scalars. At a site of $U$ there are at most $h$ misses. Thus $|U|\ge t-\lceil(h+1)n/M\rceil$ and the same secant argument applies, with one component. An interpolant independent of $Y$ bounds the entire count by $h$ since one of its coefficients in $X$ is nonzero.

The argument uses characteristic $p>2n$ only to ensure that the squarefree derivative and the quadratic discriminant behave as stated. Multiplicity-one interpolation itself works over all fields. For $Y$-degree at least three, a specialized polynomial can have a polynomial root while its discriminant still has odd-multiplicity factors. The quadratic fiber count above does not supply the analogous classification at the last open predecessor.

## Generic quartic discriminants at the last predecessor

At $e=715827881$, use $t=357913943$, $T=t-1$, the preceding $n,d$, and the quartic interpolant with $j\le4,\ i+dj\le T,\ j+l\le34$. The corresponding numbers of coefficients and constraints differ by 595. Suppose the polynomial has $Y$-degree four. Homogenize the coefficient of $Y^j$ in the coordinate variable to the binary degree $T-jd$. Its quartic discriminant is then a binary form $D_Z(X_0,X_1)$ of degree $D=6T-12d=1342177296$ and parameter degree at most $c=6\cdot34-12=192$. Indeed each quartic discriminant monomial has coefficient-degree six and index-weight twelve. If this discriminant is identically zero the quartic already has a generic multiple root.

Otherwise compute the gcd degree $r$ of the two binary first partials of $D_Z$ over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Z)$. Here $D<p$. An overbudget family of $M=2n+1$ actual bad scalar witnesses forces
$$r\ge 290804865.$$
In particular a generically squarefree discriminant permits at most **1772** such specializations.

For the proof, homogenize each selected polynomial $P_z$ in the coordinate variable to degree $d$. The identity $Q_z(X,P_z(X))=0$ gives a global factorization $Q_z=(Y-P_z)G_z$; the coefficient of $Y^j$ in $G_z$ has binary degree $T-(j+1)d$. Polynomial division by the monic linear factor proves this assertion even when the leading coefficient specializes to zero. The product identity for the discriminant gives
$$D_z=G_z(P_z)^2\operatorname{Disc}(G_z).$$
This is the universal identity for polynomials of the indicated nominal degrees, which also holds by specialization when leading coefficients vanish. The binary form $G_z(P_z)$ has degree $J=T-d=290805079$. If this form vanishes identically, $D_z=0$; otherwise it divides both first partials of $D_z$. Thus the corank of the binary Sylvester matrix of the partials is at least $J$ at every witness parameter, with roots at infinity fully counted. Over the generic parameter this matrix has size $2D-2$ and corank $r$. Choose one nonzero minor of size $2D-2-r$. For $r<J$ it loses rank by at least $J-r$ at every witness, while its parameter degree is at most $c(2D-2-r)$. Consequently
$$ M(J-r)\le c(2D-2-r),\qquad J-r\le\left\lfloor \frac{c(2D-2-J)}{M-c}\right\rfloor=214.$$
This only constrains the multiplicity of the generic discriminant. Large persistent singularities or ramification in the coordinate projection can satisfy it. [Exact quartic interpolant](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecondPrimeQuarticInterpolation.lean)

## A cyclic cubic component

Let $k$ be the algebraic closure of the constants and let $Q=aY^3+bY^2+cY+d_0$ be a factor in $k[X,Z,Y]$ of parameter degree at most $h$ and coordinate degree at most $T$, with $a\ne0$. Suppose its discriminant in $Y$ is a nonzero square in $k(X,Z)$. The following classification suffices for $6T<p$ and $p>3$. It applies, in particular, to an irreducible cubic with cyclic generic Galois group. At more than $50h$ specializing parameters possessing a polynomial root, the cubic splits after extending only the parameter field $k(Z)$, with roots rational in $X$. More specifically, after the same extension its rescaled roots $3aY+b$ are polynomials in $X$.

Put $A=b^2-3ac$ and $B=-2b^3+9abc-27a^2d_0$. The substitution $W=3aY+b$ gives $W^3-3AW-B=0$. Now $B^2-4A^3$ is $-27a^2$ times the discriminant of $Q$. By unique factorization it has a polynomial square root $D$, since $k$ is algebraically closed. Let $J=(B+D)/2$; choose the other sign for $D$ if necessary to make $J\ne0$. We have $\deg_Z J\le3h$ and $\deg_X J\le3T$. Outside at most $h+3h$ parameters, $a(X,z)$ and $D(X,z)$ are both nonzero polynomials. In a remaining fiber with a root, the specialized cubic splits over $k(X)$ since it has square, nonzero discriminant and one root. For its three rescaled roots $W_1,W_2,W_3$ and a fixed primitive cube root $\omega$, put $u=(W_1+\omega W_2+\omega^2 W_3)/3$ and $v=(W_1+\omega^2W_2+\omega W_3)/3$. The identities $uv=A$ and $u^3+v^3=B$ imply $\{u^3,v^3\}=\{(B+D)/2,(B-D)/2\}$. In particular $J(X,z)$ is a cube in $k(X)$.

Write $J=s(Z)V^3H_1H_2^2$ in $k[X,Z]$, where $H_1,H_2$ are squarefree, coprime and all their irreducible factors depend on $X$. If $H=H_1H_2$ has positive coordinate degree $r$, it is squarefree over $k(Z)$ since $r<p$. Discard the roots of $s$, parameters with $V(X,z)=0$ identically and zeros of the leading coefficient of $H$. This costs at most another $9h$. Every simple root of $H(X,z)$ would appear in $J(X,z)$ with multiplicity one or two modulo three; therefore this fiber of $H$ has at most $r/2$ distinct roots. The same Sylvester argument as for the quadratic gives at most $4\deg_Z H\le12h$ further specializations. Thus $25h$ already suffices in this case. If instead $H=1$, adjoin $s^{1/3}$ to the parameter field and put $u=s^{1/3}V$ and $v=A/u$. Since $v^3=(B-D)/2$ is a polynomial in $X$, $v$ is a polynomial in $X$ over the extended field as well. Its three roots are now $u+v,\omega u+\omega^2 v,\omega^2u+\omega v$. The original cubic roots divide these translated expressions by $3a$ and are rational in $X$. This last denominator still matters when seeking polynomial joint explanations for the original code.

There is also a direct bound on the number carried by a genuinely irreducible cyclic cubic at the last predecessor. Assume the weighted degree in $(X,Y)$ with weights $(1,d)$ is at most $T=t-1$, and $h\le34$. In the split-after-extension case, if $A\ne0$ write $J'= (B-D)/2 =s'(Z)V'^3$ by $JJ'=A^3$. Then $K=A/(VV')$ is a polynomial in $Z$ only, because $K^3=ss'$. Choose $\lambda^3=s$ and the roots have $W=\lambda V+(K/\lambda)V'$ and its conjugates after choosing a component of the parameter cover. If $A=0$ use $V'=K=0$ instead. All $V,V'$ have parameter degree at most $h$, and $K$ has parameter degree at most $2h$. If $s$ is a cube in $k(Z)$, use the three individual components. Otherwise the Kummer cubic is irreducible. Remove at most $4h$ scalars at which $a(X,z)$ vanishes identically or $s(z)=0$.

Suppose one component carries at least $10^6$ distinct remaining witnesses. Ignore the at most $T-3d$ domain sites where $a(x,Z)$ vanishes identically. On any surviving site, define
$$F_x=\lambda^2 V(x,Z)+K V'(x,Z)-\lambda[b(x,Z)+3a(x,Z)(u(x)+Zv(x))].$$
The degree in $Z$ of this polynomial in $(Z,\lambda)$ is at most $3h+1$; its degree in $\lambda$ is at most two. Its resultant with $\lambda^3-s$ has $Z$-degree at most $3(3h+1)+2(3h)=15h+3$. Thus, except at sites $U$ where $F_x$ vanishes identically on the component, at most $15h+3$ chosen scalars can agree. For a degree-one component the same bound follows directly by substituting its polynomial cube root, of degree at most $h$. Consequently $|U|\ge3d+1-(15h+3)n/10^6>200000000$. At these sites the total number of misses among the chosen witnesses is at most $hn$, from zeros of $a(x,Z)$. All but at most $\lfloor hn/10^7\rfloor\le3650$ points miss fewer than $10^7$ sites. Every three remaining points share more than $d$ agreement sites, so they lie on one polynomial secant $P_z=A_0+zB_0$ by the interpolation argument above. Since their number exceeds $h+3$, the identity $Q(X,z,A_0+zB_0)=0$ holds identically in $z$ (its degree in $z$ is at most $h+3$), contradicting irreducibility over $k(X,Z)$. Hence a genuinely irreducible cyclic cubic of these weighted degrees carries fewer than $3\cdot10^6+4h$ distinct selected scalars. This bound requires no polynomial-section claim for the original generic roots before incidence is used.

## Dimension of a putative last-predecessor family

At the last predecessor, an overbudget family of selected points $(z,P_z)$ must have affine dimension at least **10526872** over the prime field. This uses the [degree-twelve and quartic interpolation identities](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecondPrimeQuarticInterpolation.lean), the [moving-factor derivative and height argument](second-prime-moving-cubic.md) and the [bound of one secant with 186 selected points](second-prime-conditioned-cores.md). The result here is a written proof.

Work first over $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ and put $r=\dim_k\operatorname{span}\{P_z\}$. If $r=0$ all points lie on one secant, so $r\ge1$. Also $r\le d+1$. Choose a basis $w_1,\ldots,w_r$ for this span. Its square Wronskian $V=(\partial_X^iw_j)_{0\le i<r}$ has nonzero determinant. After constant row reduction the basis has distinct degrees below $p$, and the leading coefficient of its Wronskian is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant in those degrees. Thus all the $P_z$ satisfy a single monic scalar linear differential equation of order $r$, with coefficients in $k(X)$. Extension of constants does not alter the original matrix rank.

For an irreducible moving component $F\in k(X)[Z,Y]$ of total degree $m$ and positive $Y$-degree $b$, substitute its generic root $y$ into this differential equation. Clear the denominator with $J^{2r-1}$, where $J=F_Y$. The resulting numerator has total degree at most $m+2(r-1)(m-1)$, and $Y$-degree at most $b+2(r-1)(b-1)$. These are exactly the bounds for the highest derivative from the moving-factor proof: each preceding numerator $N_i$ is multiplied by $J^{2(r-i)}$, and the undifferentiated term is $yJ^{2r-1}$. Coefficients in $k(X)$ add no degree in $Z,Y$. For $F$ not dividing the numerator, the same Sylvester estimate bounds the count by
$$ H_r(m,b)=2mb-b^2+2(r-1)(2mb-m-b^2). $$

If $F$ divides it, the generic $y$ instead solves the scalar equation. In the component field define $(c_j)_j=V^{-1}(\partial_X^iy)_{i<r}$. Differentiating the first $r-1$ equations gives zero after subtracting the next equation. The order-$r$ scalar relation gives the last row of $V(\partial_X c_j)_j=0$, hence every $\partial_X c_j$ is zero and $y=\sum_j c_j w_j$. These constants are algebraic over $k(Z)$ by the same resultant height argument as in the moving-cubic proof. Explicitly, using the common denominator $\det(V)J^{2r}$, a numerator and denominator for every $c_j$ have $Y$-degree at most $12(r+1)$ and $X$-degree at most $10(r+1)(n+1)$. The Wronskian cofactors have degree at most $rd$; the numerator of $\partial_X^i y$ has $X$-degree at most $(2i-1)n$, and padding by powers of $J$ preserves the stated bound. Taking the resultant with $F$ gives an annihilating polynomial for $c_j$ of $X$-degree at most $52(r+1)(n+1)<p$. The primitive minimal polynomial inherits this degree bound, and its monic coefficients have derivative zero. Reduced rational functions of degree less than $p$ in $X$ with derivative zero belong to $k(Z)$. The generic root is therefore a polynomial in $X$ of degree at most $d$ after an algebraic parameter extension. The incidence argument in the linked proof now gives either an actual secant or at most $\lceil12(n-d)/(t-d)\rceil=42$ labels.

The two interpolants leave at most 408 isolated labels by Bézout. Their common positive-degree components satisfy $\sum_i m_i\le12$ and $\sum_i b_i\le4$; allow another 34 labels from factors depending only on $Z$. The functions $2mb-b^2$ and $2mb-m-b^2$ are superadditive on $m\ge b\ge1$ and increasing on this range. Thus all factors with a nonzero differential numerator together carry at most $80+136(r-1)$ labels. At most four more factors can have constant differential solutions. For a generous bound allow $4\cdot42$ labels on nonheads and $e+1+3\cdot185$ on actual heads, since at most one secant has 186 selected points. Consequently
$$ 2n+1\le408+34+80+136(r-1)+4\cdot42+(e+1)+3\cdot185. $$
This forces $r\ge10526873$. The affine dimension of $(z,P_z)$ is at least $r-1$ by projection and linear span, as claimed.

## Published parameter estimates

At the last predecessor the exact agreement is $357913943/1073741824$. In the characteristic-free Johnson bound of Dao, Kominers and Thaler as recorded in the repository, the interpolation multiplicity is 3, the candidate degree is 14 and the parameter height is 65. Writing $\theta=(n-d)/(t-d)$, the integer upper bound from their displayed recipe is
$$ \left\lfloor (2\cdot14-1)65+
\theta(65+14+4d\cdot14\cdot65)+(n-d-1)14\right\rfloor=859664530354. $$
The printed bound of Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Häböck, Kopparty and Saraf gives an integer upper bound of 24071860576204 at the same step. Both apply to the correct scalar experiment, but their numerators exceed the available $2n$.
