# Arbitrary targets for four-monomial cross ratios on the first-prime 64-site quotient

On $H=\mu_{64}$ in the first named prime, let $R$ be a nonconstant cross ratio of four different scaled Laurent monomials over the algebraic closure, of degree $m<16$. For **any** three target values, let $r$ be the size of their union on $H$. Then $2r-3m\le36$. Thus this 64-site family cannot improve the first-prime quarter-rate cubic score, even after an arbitrary target projectivity. This is a finite-field proof using exact rank and univariate factorization. [Three-head score](quarter-three-head-root-score.md) · [Other normalized sizes](quarter-monomial-target-compression.md)

It suffices to treat exponents with gcd of differences one. If that gcd has a dyadic factor, divide it out and use the known three-head bound on the smaller quotient exactly as in the fixed-target proof. If it is odd, division preserves the number of sites and can only increase the score. The degree and span argument gives translated distinct exponents $0=a_0<a_1<a_2<a_3\le42$ with $a_3+a_2-a_1-\sum g_i\le15$, where $g_i$ is the gcd of the other triple's differences. This span bound uses only $m\le15$. An improved score would require $13\le m\le15$ and $r\ge38$. [Exponent degree and multiplicity](quarter-monomial-cross-ratio.md)

## A twelve-point noncollision fiber

The pair-collision target values are $0,1,\infty$. The case where all three selected targets are these values has already been excluded. Some selected noncollision fiber must have at least twelve sites. Otherwise an improvement is numerically possible only for $(15,15,11)$ at $m=15$. The two full fibers would give a two-full-head improvement on this quotient, already excluded. This last cited exclusion applies over the algebraic closure as well: normalizing the two full fibers to zero and infinity gives the ratio of monic grid vanishers up to scale, and the value at one site of the third fiber determines that scale in the ground field. [Two-full-fiber exclusion on the named order-64 subgroup](quarter-mu64-mu128-cyclic-exclusion.md) · [Pair-collision targets](quarter-monomial-cross-ratio.md)

Write $T_i=c_iX^{a_i}$. In the equation $(T_0-T_1)(T_2-T_3)-\lambda(T_0-T_2)(T_1-T_3)=0$, the coefficients of $T_iT_j$ on the three complementary pairs of exponents are respectively $-\lambda,1,\lambda-1$. A noncollision value has all three nonzero. With $v_{ij}$ denoting the actual monomial coefficient and $A=v_{01}v_{23},B=v_{02}v_{13},C=v_{03}v_{12}$, this implies
$$
                      (A+B-C)^2=4AB .
$$
For distinct original exponents there are five or six different exponents modulo 64 after adding in pairs, as verified across all degree-eligible tuples. At six distinct exponents the coefficient vector must have all entries nonzero and satisfy the displayed symmetric identity. At five exponents, one complementary pair has coinciding exponents. The four unmerged coefficients must be nonzero.

The verifier enumerates all admissible exponent tuples and identifies the pair-sum spectra up to a common exponent shift, an odd exponent multiplier modulo 64, and permutations of and within the complementary pairs. These operations preserve the number of grid roots of every candidate and the coefficient tests. Rotating the grid scales each complementary product by the same nonzero scalar, so also preserves the tests. Any twelve roots have four in a cyclic arc of exponent length at most 16: the average distance from a root to the third following root is at most $3\cdot64/12$. Rotate the first to exponent zero and take every possible three additional exponents in $1,\ldots,16$.

For each such anchor compute the kernel of its evaluation rows on the actual spectrum. Its common roots are the rows annihilating the entire kernel. If at least twelve are common, a one-dimensional kernel supplies one coefficient vector. For a higher-dimensional kernel the finite check shows there are already at least sixteen common roots whenever the requisite coefficients can be nonzero. If fewer than twelve are common, any successful vector must solve another independent grid equation. The verifier enumerates the resulting subspaces; for a two-dimensional kernel each extra site selects just one projective coefficient pair. Row spans with the same set of common roots are equal, since they were generated by rows from this set. This enumeration includes coefficient vectors over every extension of the named field. Every permissible vector with twelve roots in this census actually has **at least sixteen raw roots**.

A degree-at-most-15 rational map cannot put sixteen different sites in one reduced fiber. At one of these raw roots both pairing products must vanish. At least three of the original monomials coincide there. The 575 original distinct-exponent tuples give 204 different spectra. Only 19 original tuples survive the necessary coefficient tests with a possible grid triple.

## The one remaining coefficient

Rotate one such grid triple to $X=1$ and rescale all four monomials to give its three coefficients value one. Denote the fourth coefficient by $u\ne0$. First test each $u\in H$ directly, using the leading coefficients of the pairing products at common roots to evaluate the reduced map. For distinct exponents in this range, every monomial difference at a coincidence has a simple zero; its leading Euler derivative is $(a_i-a_j)T_i(x)$.

For $u\notin H$, the fourth monomial differs from each of the first three at every grid site. At a given site the reduced target therefore has a fixed expression as a projective ratio of two affine forms in $u$, possibly constant. This includes coincidences of the three fixed monomials via the preceding derivative. Group the 64 sites having the same rational target function in $u$. Write $r_0$ for the total size of the three largest generic groups. For each two different groups, take their cross determinant in $u$, raising it to the product of their sizes. The product of all nonconstant factors is a univariate event polynomial. If specialization adds at least $38-r_0$ sites to the top-three total, that parameter is a root of multiplicity at least $38-r_0$. Indeed merging two groups of sizes $a,b$ gains at most $\min(a,b)\le ab$ in the top-three total, and each newly equal pair contributes one vanishing determinant. The generic target counts and all irreducible factors of the required multiplicity are checked. Over an irreducible factor, equality of target values is computed in its residue field. This checks non-rational coefficients too.

The degree can also fall without a new grid equality. Let $g_0$ be the difference gcd of the fixed exponent triple, and let $j,k$ denote two fixed indices and $t$ the varying index. Their triple, including $t$, occurs geometrically exactly when $u^{|a_j-a_k|/g}=1$, for $g=\gcd(a_j-a_k,a_t-a_j)$, contributing $g$ common roots. The four-way overlap occurs at a single point exactly when $u^{g_0}=1$, by primitivity. Thus subtract $g_0$ and the three eligible $g$'s from $a_3+a_2-a_1$, and add two in the four-way case. This counts exactly the common factors removed from the pairing products. Include every nonzero root of these degree-drop equations in the specialization check. None of the eligible maps improves the score.

## Repeated original exponents

With two or fewer different exponents a primitive map has degree at most two. With three different exponents the two repeated powers can be divided out, leaving different nonzero constants $A,B$ and two monomials $CX^j,DX^k$ with $\gcd(j,k)=1$. Their original span is at most 17. For any noncollision target the defining equation is a projectivity between the values of the two varying monomials fixing $A$ and $B$. Each of the three selected fibers of an improving map must have at least eleven sites. The pairs $(x^j,x^k)$ are distinct and the projective graph therefore gives at least eleven distinct matches. Over the named field, seven such pairs between grid cosets force a scaling or scaled reciprocal. The map on the unscaled grid determined by three matches is defined over that field, even if the two constant coset scalars are in its algebraic closure. A scaling fixing $A,B$ is the identity, which gives a collision target. A reciprocal fixing $A,B$ requires $B=-A$ and has equation $CDX^{j+k}=A^2$. This is the only possible large noncollision fiber. [Seven-match theorem on dyadic cosets](six-dyadic-projective-matches.md#prime-field-conclusion)

Scale $A$ to one. The only four potentially large targets are the three collision values and this harmonic value. Their sites are contained respectively in the sets $CX^j=1$ or $DX^k=-1$, $CX^j=-1$ or $DX^k=1$, $CX^j=DX^k$, and $CDX^{j+k}=1$. At least three of these four sets must be nonempty. This forces both $C,D\in H$: if neither belongs, the first two are empty, and if just one belongs, the last two are empty. The verifier tests all remaining $j,k,C,D$ up to rotation of the source grid. At 3,284 phase representatives the degree belongs to $\{13,14,15\}$; none improves the score.
