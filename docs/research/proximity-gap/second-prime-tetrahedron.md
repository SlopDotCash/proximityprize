$$
U_{ab}(t)=(a+b)t-ab,\qquad
W_{ab}(t)=(a^2+ab+b^2)t-ab(a+b).
$$
Use $V_{ab}=W_{ab}+7U_{ab}$ to avoid vertical directions in the selected comparisons. At each vertex fiber the three incident edges agree. From that common point, the other three edges give three distinct directions. Away from the vertices, a received value equal to one edge gives three distinct foreign directions after duplicates in the faces of the tetrahedron are removed. A received value of $(17,29)$ on the selected off-head sites gives six distinct directions. Lean checks every selected agreement, nonzero denominator and direction determinant over the named field. ### The remaining sextic has no dihedral equivariance

Suppose $T(\sigma x)=\beta(T(x))$ on the 32-node domain for a nonidentity dihedral symmetry $\sigma$ and a target projectivity $\beta$. The unique six-element fiber must be preserved. A nontrivial rotation preserving six nodes has order two, since its orbits have equal power-of-two size. Thus it suffices to consider the half-turn and the two conjugacy classes of reflections. In each case $\beta^2$ fixes at least three target values and is the identity. If $\beta$ itself is the identity, only fibers containing source fixed points can have odd size. The involution has at most two fixed points, whereas three fibers of size five are needed.

Otherwise, the six-fiber value is a rational fixed point of the target involution. Since the characteristic is odd, a projective change of target makes that value zero and the involution negation. The equality on 32 nodes gives a rational-function identity: after clearing denominators its degree is at most 12. For a reflection $x\mapsto u^2/x$, use the source coordinate $y=(x-u)/(x+u)$ to turn it into $y\mapsto-y$. The half-turn needs no source change. Reflections through vertices give 15 pairs plus $0,\infty$, and the other two involutions give 16 pairs.

With a reduced degree-six rational function equivariant under negation, the numerator and denominator have opposite parity. When the six-fiber contains three ordinary pairs it has numerator $\prod_{a\in A}(y^2-a^2)$ and denominator $yQ(y^2)$ with $\deg Q\le2$. When it contains both fixed points it has numerator $y\prod_{a\in A}(y^2-a^2)$ for two ordinary pairs and denominator $Q(y^2)$ with $\deg Q=3$. Scaling the target makes a five-element nonzero finite fiber have value one. Such a fiber exists because the preimage of the other fixed target can have odd size only when it contains one of the source fixed points, and in these cases both fixed points occur together. Each pair contributes at most one signed input to the chosen five-fiber. Interpolating at all choices of three or four signed inputs therefore recovers every possible $Q$. Negating all signs gives the same fiber partition. The half-turn case also identifies zero sets by rotation and inversion.

The exact verifier considers 23,491 candidate denominators in the half-turn case, 640,640 in the fixed-point-free reflection case, and 399,767 or 600,149 in the reflection cases distinguished by the zero fiber. Only the last case produces a six-fiber with two five-fibers. Each such profile begins $(6,5,5,1,1)$, so none has a third five-fiber. ### Counting the vertices used by active heads

Take a simple graph whose edges are heads and whose vertices have distinct values under a rational quotient of physical degree $d$. The graph condition is that at a site outside the $r$ common locator roots, if several heads jointly agree with the received pair then they are all incident to one vertex and the site lies in that vertex's quotient fiber. Each such fiber has at most $d$ sites. Secants in barycentric coordinates satisfy this condition since a nonvertex point on an edge has exactly its two endpoints in its support. A projected configuration with additional intersections needs its own incidence analysis. Allow any received values, any root placement and any finite directions at fresh sites. The degree allocation for the common locator and site-separating shear is $r+d+2\le n/2$.

For any $m$ active edges, let $v$ be the number of their distinct endpoints and $c$ their smallest core. Count one membership per site, plus at most $m-1$ extras per common root. At an ordinary vertex fiber each extra membership is charged to the vertex's selected degree minus one. Summing those vertex charges gives at most $(2m-v)d$. Hence
$$
mc\le n+(m-1)r+(2m-v)d.
$$
Any four or more edges using at least five vertices admit a four-edge subset covering at least five vertices. Start with an edge and add an edge at a missing vertex until five are covered, then fill the selection to four edges. It follows that $4c\le n+3r+3d$ and $8c+12\le5n$. At the named length this gives $c\le671088638$. At any earlier step than 402,653,185 errors, a graph using five vertices cannot succeed with four or more active heads. At most five heads on any graph give fewer than $2^{31}$ labels at these steps. Thus the only active simple graph that could exceed this budget earlier is the complete tetrahedron. This holds for all rational quotients, including those on larger power domains. [Finite-set charging and selection proofs](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/SecantGraphIncidence.lean)

At 402,653,185 errors each active head needs $c\ge671088638$. Suppose there are at least eleven active heads. They use at least six vertices, since five vertices support at most ten simple edges. If two edges are vertex-disjoint, start with those four endpoints and add edges and endpoints until four edges cover at least six vertices. The four-edge bound now gives $4c\le n+3r+2d$, which forces $d\le2$. But an arbitrary six of the heads would then satisfy $6c\le n+5r+12d$, contradicting the same core requirement. If every pair of edges intersects, the graph is a star or a triangle. Indeed, two edges $ab,ac$ force any edge missing $a$ to equal $bc$, and no fourth edge can intersect all three sides of a triangle. Six edges of a star have seven endpoints and hence satisfy the impossible $6c\le n+5(r+d)$. At most ten heads can be active. Each has at most $n-c\le402653186$ fresh sites, proving the matching upper bound $4026531860$.

More generally, for every integer budget from **2,415,919,110 through 4,026,531,859**, the same error step is the exact first failure in this construction class. On the preceding step a five-vertex active graph cannot have four heads, and graphs with at most four vertices have at most six heads with at most 402,653,185 labels each. At the next step the ten-head witness exceeds every budget in the stated range. The second prime's 128-bit budget is below this range.
