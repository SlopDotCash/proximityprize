# Testing norm-one elliptic supports at the second named prime

No order-seven rational elliptic kernel over \(\mathbb F_{127}\) produces the required four-word configuration on the 128th roots of the second named prime by lifting its full abscissa fibers, even after an arbitrary projective relabeling. The certificate tests all 46 relevant elliptic maps up to source-curve isomorphism and 8,001 projective relabelings of each. Of the 1,599 full fiber pairs surviving the characteristic-zero parity condition, each has an injective ratio off its supports in the named field. This test concerns the degree-seven power-quotient construction for a possible four-word list at the second prime's sixteenth-rate MCA cutoff. [Pivot and required pair counts](cubic-list-padding.md#what-a-four-word-power-map-construction-would-require-at-the-pivot) · [Exact checker](../../../scripts/probes/probe_septic_elliptic_lift.py)

Use the Cayley bijection between \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{127})\) and the 128th roots in \(\mathbb F_{127^2}\), and fix corresponding exponents for a generator of the 128th roots in the named field. A source template consists of the distinct seven-element abscissa fibers of the degree-seven quotient associated with a cyclic subgroup of seven rational points. The question is whether, after one common source projective transformation, the full supports in a seven-degree quartet could be chosen from this template. Its unsaturated pair differences are unrestricted.

The probe enumerates the smooth short Weierstrass curves \(y^2=x^3+ax+b\) modulo \((a,b)\mapsto(t^4a,t^6b)\) for \(t\ne0\). This covers every elliptic curve over the 127-element field. On each such curve it enumerates rational abscissas and finds each order-seven subgroup. For a curve of order \(7^e m\), with \(7\nmid m\), multiply a representative of each \(\{\pm P\}\) by \(m\), then by seven until the final nonidentity point has order seven. This misses no subgroup since a point already of order seven generates the same subgroup after multiplication by \(m\). With \(K\) the three finite abscissas of its nonzero members, the template map is
\[
 x\longmapsto x+\sum_{c\in K}\left[
  \frac{2(3c^2+a)}{x-c}+\frac{4(c^3+ac+b)}{(x-c)^2}\right].
\]
This is the abscissa formula obtained by adding the two terms for each pair of opposite kernel points in the cyclic quotient. The pole fiber has at most four distinct points. The numbers of families with 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 full fibers are respectively 5, 5, 16, 10 and 10. Fewer than three full fibers cannot yield four saturated edges with distinct seven-point supports. A repeated full support would cost at least one wasted pair coincidence on each of its seven sites, regardless of whether the corresponding two edges meet, whereas the quartet can afford only two.

A postcomposition of the source labeling by a rotation or inversion of the cyclic group preserves the size of a constant-ratio fiber in the named field. Inversion changes \(X\) to \(1/X\), contributing the same denominator degree to both locators. This dihedral group acts transitively on the 128 permuted sites of \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{127})\), and inversion acts in the Cayley coordinate by \(x\mapsto -x\). Hence representatives modulo that action for every source projectivity are \(x\mapsto ux+v\) with \(1\le u\le63,\ 0\le v<127\). The checker examines all \(63\cdot127=8001\).

Two full supports \(A,B\) from distinct fibers are disjoint. If their locator ratio is constant on at least five sites outside \(A\cup B\), it is constant at three sites of one exponent parity. The order-128 unit theorem lifts the constant-ratio comparisons to the complex roots. The complex parity lemma then forces both \(A\) and \(B\) to be invariant under the antipodal shift on the other parity class. Since each has seven elements, it can have that symmetry on at most one class. The probe tests this binary condition on every pair. For each survivor it forms the two monic locators in the named prime field, evaluates their ratio at every one of the 114 remaining sites, and counts equal values. Their maximum multiplicity over all survivors is **one**. For a quartet scoring at least 40 useful pair coincidences, at least four of its six edges have all seven roots in the grid. Two of those edges share a vertex. Translate the words by their common vertex and scale their two full differences, giving the ratio of the two monic locators above. The third edge of that triangle has at least five roots, all outside the disjoint full supports. The parity test and finite computation exclude this.
