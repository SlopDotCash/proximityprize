# Marking the fibers of a fourth head

The first dimension above the exact sixteenth-rate window already allows **2,147,483,654** distinct bad labels at **715,827,881 errors** on a freely chosen injective domain over a finite extension of the second named field. Here $n=2^{30}$, $k=71,582,790$, and the comparison is to the fixed integer $2n$. In a finite extension the probability denominator changes. At the named prime itself a smaller construction on 34 chosen sites, in dimension four, has **72** literal bad labels at 21 errors. The production construction requires its marked roots to be available and gives no assertion that the designated subgroup contains them.

## Degree and site counts

Use the four hyperbola heads $W_i$ from the six-edge product construction, with vertex zero distinguished. Their degree is $d=k-1$. Opposite edges have equal direction-map degree, and the degrees at vertex zero are $m_1,m_2,m_3>0$, with $\sum m_i=d$. Take the balanced choice $m_i=\lfloor(d+i-1)/3\rfloor$, $1\le i\le3$. At a double site exactly the two incident heads coincide. The $4d$ distinct double sites give each head exactly $2d$ core positions. Write $r_i$ for the direction map on the edge $0i$, so its opposite edge has map $1/r_i$. These three rational functions have independent generic numerator and denominator coefficients. [Pair factorizations and generic double sites](four-head-extension-sharpness.md)

Set $t=n-e$, $c=t-1$. In addition to the double sites, each old head $i=1,2,3$ receives $h=c-2d$ singletons, and head zero receives $h_0=n-3c+2d$ singletons. All $4d+3h+h_0=n$ sites are in the evaluation domain. The resulting fourth core has $2d+h_0$ positions. Each of $M$ designated outside scalars needs $v=t-(2d+h_0)>0$ further agreements.

Fix $M$ distinct finite nonzero scalars away from $1,-1$ and from each other's reciprocals. For a mark $z$, an old singleton of type $i$ with $r_i(x)=z$ agrees with head zero after folding by $z$. There are $m_i$ simple eligible roots. The old head has room for at most $h$ such selected sites in total, over all marks. A schedule exists whenever
$$
 Mv\le\sum_{i=1}^3\min(h,Mm_i).
$$
Choose three column lengths at most $\min(h,Mm_i)$ summing to $Mv$, and fill them consecutively with the cyclic sequence of all $M$ marks. Each mark is used exactly $v$ times and at most $m_i$ times in column $i$. The Lean schedules give the actual number of roots to select for every pair of mark and old head in each production row. ## Separation after the marks are prescribed

Work over the algebraic closure of the coefficient function field. In the positive characteristic application the constants containing the finitely many marks may already be chosen in a finite extension of the prime field. The six numerator and denominator polynomials are independent. Each map has positive degree, with no common numerator and denominator zero including at infinity. At a prescribed finite input, fixing a finite map value imposes one linear condition on its coefficients. At two distinct inputs these are independent even for the same map, since a degree-one numerator allows independent evaluations. A value and a first derivative give two independent conditions at one input. Zero or infinite values use the numerator or denominator. Thus prescribing $a$ independent conditions with only $b<a$ free input or value parameters produces a proper exceptional locus. One may equivalently compare transcendence degrees over the fixed constant field.

A marked fiber has full degree, has no root at infinity and has simple roots by the leading-coefficient test and the first-jet condition. Two marked fibers of distinct maps meeting would impose two fixed values with only the input free. Fibers of one map at different marks are disjoint. A coincidence between a marked input and a double node either fixes two map values at one input, or fixes one map value and equates the other two up to reciprocal. The latter has three conditions with at most the input and one auxiliary value free. Hence every marked site has four distinct evaluated heads.

At an old singleton of type $i$, the two old heads other than $i$ supply directions from the two maps with indices different from $i$, in the appropriate direct or reciprocal orientations. If both directions coincide, there are three conditions on the three maps at that point, with the input and common value free. Either old direction equal to a prescribed mark, zero, or infinity imposes two fixed values at one input. For two distinct marked sites to share a fresh old label, each imposes its pinned map value and one further prescribed value or reciprocal of the common label. There are four independent conditions with only the two inputs and common label free. At a double site a proposed fresh label fixes two map values up to reciprocal, so the same four-condition argument covers a marked site and a double. The double-double case is the original generic separation argument, and two old slots at a single double are distinct by that argument. An old label at a double can equal a fixed mark only when two maps take fixed values there. These exclusions hold simultaneously for the entire finite collection of fibers.

Use the selected roots and fill the remaining singleton quotas by successively avoiding earlier sites, used labels, marks, poles and coincidences. The relevant edge direction functions are nonconstant. At each old singleton its two old fresh directions differ unless the three old values are collinear. The equation for this is among the direct or reciprocal equalities between two distinct maps. At a fourth singleton its three old fresh directions are the three distinct maps, again pairwise different off finitely many points. This gives a domain and two received words over the algebraically closed field. The finitely many polynomial identities and nonvanishing differences, with every required denominator inverted, specialize in a finite extension of the prime field by the finite type field lemma. ## Literal bad events

Each off-core position of each old head supplies a distinct finite scalar with a fold agreement on that site and its entire $c$-site core. The core contains at least $k$ positions, so joint agreement on the support would force the two polynomial rows to equal that head, contradicting the fresh-site mismatch. This gives $3(n-c)=3(e+1)$ labels. Each mark agrees after folding on head zero's entire core and its $v$ selected sites, totaling $t$. The fourth core also has at least $k$ sites, and its polynomial pair differs from the received pair at every selected old singleton. All $M$ marks are additional literal bad events. Lean checks this step against the operational MCA definition. [Native events](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/ThreeHeadMarkedFibers.lean)

For the 34-site example, the three degree-one maps are supplied by the existing second-prime cubic quartet. Each of the six marks $2,3,5,6,7,8$ gets one marked root at each old head. Starting from the twelve double sites gives eighteen old singletons and four fourth-head singletons. Each old core has twelve positions and the fourth core has ten. The verifier interpolates every folded witness and both proposed joint rows on four core positions. It checks at least thirteen agreements and a joint mismatch for every one of the 72 distinct scalars. [Prime-field reconstruction](../../../scripts/probes/probe_three_head_marked_fibers.py)
