# Four polynomial heads attain every fresh label over finite extensions

Let $d\ge3$, $k=d+1$, and $h\ge1$. In every characteristic there is a finite field, an injective domain of length $n=4d+4h$ and one received pair for which four degree-at-most-$d$ heads supply exactly $4(e+1)$ different bad scalars on their off-core sites at
\[
t=2d+h+1,\qquad e=n-t=2d+3h-1=3n/4-k .
\]
Each head contributes its entire personal capacity. Other witness polynomials may contribute additional bad scalars.

* For $n=2^{30}$ and $k=n/8$, take $h=k+1$. The construction supplies **2,684,354,564** bad scalars at **671,088,640 errors**. This exceeds $2n$ by **536,870,916**. The previous error count has a four-head cap of $2n$ over every finite field and injective domain. Thus the first possible four-head overrun of this integer budget, taking the worst case over finite extensions and injective domains, is exactly **671,088,640**. [Predecessor bound](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md)
* For $n=4k$ take $h=1$. The four heads supply $2n+4$ labels at $n/2$ errors. Below this error count their combined personal capacities are at most $2n$.
* The characteristic can equal the second named prime. The existence proof allows a field extension and chooses its domain from incidence roots. On that extension the probability lower bound is $4(e+1)/|F|$, and its sponsor numerator depends on $|F|$. The first failure on the prescribed production subgroup over the prime field remains open.

## Six polynomials

Work first over an algebraic closure of the rational function field in the coefficients of six polynomials $P_{ij}$, one for each edge of the tetrahedron on vertices $0,1,2,3$. The coefficients are algebraically independent over $\mathbb F_p$. Give the opposite edge pairs $01,23$; $02,13$; and $03,12$ equal positive degrees $m_1,m_2,m_3$, respectively, with $m_1+m_2+m_3=d$. Define
\[
 A_i=\prod_{j\ne i}P_{ij},\qquad B_i=\prod_{j<k,\,j,k\ne i}P_{jk},\qquad W_i=(A_i,B_i).
\]
Both coordinates have degree $d$ and all four products $A_iB_i$ agree. With $i,j,k,l$ distinct, subtraction gives
\[
 W_i-W_j=(P_{ij},-P_{kl})(P_{ik}P_{il}-P_{jk}P_{jl}).
\]
Away from coincidence, the scalar identifying the two folded values is $P_{ij}/P_{kl}$ wherever the denominator is nonzero. The six factorizations hold over every commutative ring. [Lean edge identities](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FourHeadExtensionSharpness.lean)

Put $r_1=P_{01}/P_{23}$, $r_2=P_{02}/P_{13}$ and $r_3=P_{03}/P_{12}$. The six double strata are the solutions of $r_j=r_k$ or $r_jr_k=1$ for the three unordered map pairs. For example, $W_0=W_1$ on $r_2r_3=1$, while $W_2=W_3$ on $r_2=r_3$. At a node from either stratum the two heads other than its owners have fresh directions $v$ and $v^{-1}$, where $v=r_j(x)$ or $r_j(x)^{-1}$. The product construction relates opposite directions by one fixed projective involution at every node.

## The double sites have distinct scalar directions

There are $D=2d+6$ independent coefficients. The following dimension count can be made entirely with transcendence degrees. Over a field generated by $s$ auxiliary values, $r$ independent linear conditions on the coefficient tuple give total transcendence degree at most $s+D-r$. Thus a forbidden event with $r>s$ cannot occur for our tuple. At distinct prescribed $x,y$, evaluations of one numerator at the two points are independent linear functionals because its allowed degree is at least one. At one point, value and first derivative are also independent, in any characteristic. These facts prove the independence of the conditions below even when the two events involve the same map.

1. No two edge polynomials share a root, by two independent evaluations against one variable $x$. All six cleared coincidence equations have the full degree $m_j+m_k$, as their leading coefficients differ by a nonzero polynomial in independent variables. A root of a coincidence equation cannot be a zero or a pole of either participating map, since this would give two edge polynomials with a common root. A zero or pole of the third map at the same point adds a third independent condition with only $x$ and the common value free.
2. For two different map pairs to give an incidence at the same $x$, all three map values must be prescribed by $v$ or $v^{-1}$. These are three conditions with only $x,v$ free. For both incidences of the same map pair to hold, $v^2=1$, giving two conditions with just $x$ transcendental. This also shows that the two fresh labels at each double are distinct.
3. A repeated root of $r_j=r_k$ or $r_jr_k=1$ prescribes both values and both first derivatives after choosing only $x$, $v$ and the first map's derivative $w$. The other derivative must be $w$ for equality and $-w/v^2$ for a reciprocal. Explicitly, prescribing value $a$ and derivative $b$ of a ratio $N/Q$ gives the linear equations $N(x)-aQ(x)=0$ and $N'(x)-aQ'(x)-bQ(x)=0$. The four constraints are independent because each numerator has its own independent value and derivative. All six coincidence divisors therefore have simple roots.
4. Suppose distinct double sites $x,y$ share a fresh label $v$. At each site its two participating maps must each take a prescribed value from $v,v^{-1}$. This gives four independent evaluations with three free values $x,y,v$. If one or both maps are repeated, their evaluation functionals at $x$ and $y$ remain independent. There are finitely many choices of the two pairs and the reciprocals. Consequently the two fresh labels on each double site are different from all fresh labels on every other double site.

There are now exactly $2\sum_{j<k}(m_j+m_k)=4d$ double sites. Opposite edges have the same counts, and each vertex owns $2d$ double sites.

## The received pair and the nonjoint witnesses

Choose $h$ additional singleton sites for each vertex. For a proposed singleton of owner $i$, the three fresh directions are the three maps, each taken in the direct or reciprocal orientation for that edge at $i$. Each map is nonconstant. Excluding its fibers over all previously used labels costs only finitely many sites. Also exclude poles, zeros, pair coincidences in either orientation and all previously chosen sites. The algebraically closed field is infinite, so the choices can be made successively. All three fresh directions are distinct at every singleton.

At each site define the received pair by the evaluated value of an owner head. A head's joint core consists of its $2d$ double sites and $h$ singletons. Every other site supplies one finite label. The preceding construction makes all $4(n-2d-h)=4(e+1)$ labels distinct. For a given head and off-core site with label $\gamma$, the degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial $A_i+\gamma B_i$ agrees on the entire core and that site, hence on $t$ sites. A joint explanation by two degree-at-most-$d$ polynomials would equal $A_i,B_i$ since the core has at least $d+1$ sites. Their mismatch at the added site contradicts that explanation. These are literal MCA events for the same received pair. [Lean reduction from injective off-core labels to actual events](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FourHeadExtensionSharpness.lean)

Finally take the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by the finitely many coefficients, sites, received entries and labels, and the inverses of all their required nonzero differences and denominators. For every off-core mismatch include the inverse of one nonzero coordinate difference. This is a nonzero finitely generated algebra. Its quotient by a maximal ideal is a finite extension of $\mathbb F_p$ and preserves every recorded equality, difference and root. This produces the required finite field and injective domain. [Hilbert Nullstellensatz, The Stacks Project, Theorem 10.34.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00FV)

## Prime-field checks

For $d=3$ the existing three Möbius maps over the second named prime have twelve rational, simple, disjoint signed-coincidence sites. Replacing each map $z$ by $(1+z)/(1-z)$ turns negation into reciprocal and gives the six edge polynomials for this product construction. With one singleton per head the resulting word has 36 distinct bad labels at length 16 and eight errors. With five singletons per head it has **84 distinct bad labels at length 32 and 20 errors**. The checker interpolates each folded witness and the two rows separately on four core sites. The arbitrary-degree existence and finite-extension specialization above are written proofs.
