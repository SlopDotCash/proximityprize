# A 256-root locator comparison lifts when both named primes agree

Take the canonical primitive 256th roots $z_i=g_i^{(p_i-1)/256}$ in the two named fields, with $g_1=3$ and $g_2=37$. Use the same exponent labels in both fields and let $\zeta$ be a primitive complex 256th root. For any two disjoint subsets of exponents $P,Q$ and any two sites outside both supports, the monic locator ratio $L_P/L_Q$ agrees at those sites over $\mathbb C$ **if and only if it agrees in both named fields**. The sets may have different sizes. A simultaneous odd relabeling of both primitive roots gives the same result.

At each named prime separately there are degree-62 counterexamples on four sites. The joint theorem excludes a single exponent layout causing the same nonlifting comparison at both specified roots. It also rules out a common saturated quarter-rate quartet layout on the two 256th-root domains. Indeed every protected equality in such a layout would transfer. The characteristic-zero three-centre argument would allow at most ten changed ordinary pair labels under negation, although the polynomial recovery and rotation proof in either finite field requires at least $256/4-1=63$. ## Keeping the phase in both congruences

Rotate the first site to $1$ and write the second as $z^h$. Interchanging them covers all displacements with $1\le h\le128$. The signed indicator $w_j=1_P(j)-1_Q(j)$ has squared norm at most 254 on the other sites. One normalized site factor is
$$
 c_j=(1-z^j)/(z^h-z^j).
$$
Let $u_r=1-z^r$ for $r=1,3,\ldots,127$. For $s=2^a b$ with $b$ odd, factor $1-z^s$ into the $2^a$ terms $1-z^{b+i\,256/2^a}$. Each term with index $l>128$ becomes $z^{128+l}u_{256-l}$. Thus the exponents and phase of every $c_j$ are integers determined by its site. Write the resulting column as $(A_j,t_j)$ so $c_j=z^{t_j}\prod_r u_r^{A_{rj}}$. The verifier also checks each of these identities using full-field logarithms at both primes. At $h=128$ each column sums to zero in the primitive exponents, so delete the last exponent coordinate and use the log differences with that last unit.

In the integer lattice generated by $(A_j,t_j)$ and the phase period $(0,256)$, impose the two congruences
$$
 \sum_r \log_{g_i}(u_r)e_r+((p_i-1)/256)t\equiv0\pmod{p_i-1}, \qquad i=1,2.
$$
They share the same phase variable $t$. Hermite normal form, together with two single-row modular kernel calculations, gives a lattice on projecting away $t$. The code checks containment of every source generator and certifies each modular kernel by its index. All 128 supplied unit logarithms are verified by powers in the actual fields. A recursive primality certificate and the factorizations of $p_i-1$ check the primitive generators. ## Excluding nonzero projected exponents

A realizable exponent $e=Aw$ satisfies
$$
 e^{\mathsf T}(AA^{\mathsf T})^{-1}e\le \lVert w\rVert^2\le254.
$$
This follows by orthogonally projecting $w$ onto the row space of $A$. In the antipodal case the expression uses the reduced independent rows. For an integer basis of the projected lattice, the verifier forms this positive definite quadratic form in basis coordinates. After basis reduction it checks the integral change matrix has determinant $\pm1$ and reproduces the new form exactly. Basis reduction may use floating point. All enclosures and the branch test use exact arithmetic. [Lean projection inequality](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicUnitEllipsoid.lean)

In Gram–Schmidt coordinates a row contributes $d_i(v_i+\sum_{j>i}\mu_{ji}v_j)^2$. Round $8192\mu_{ji}$ toward zero to obtain integers. The error in the scaled linear form is at most $\sum_{j>i}|v_j|$. Round $1024d_i$ downward. Squaring the nonnegative lower bound on the absolute linear form and multiplying by the rounded weight gives a lower bound on the true scaled row cost. The exact inverse form bounds each $|v_i|$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. The search includes both closed ends of each interval and uses sign symmetry on the last nonzero coordinate. The integer search checks its fixed-width input limits before enumeration. [Lean rounding inequalities](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/JointLocatorRounding.lean) · [Branch verifier](../../../scripts/probes/probe_mu256_joint_transfer.cpp)

At every one of the 128 displacements, the rounded outer bound contains **no nonzero lattice point**. The run with block size 36 visited 4,227,091,567 nodes in total. Hence $e=0$ for every common finite comparison. Its factorization in characteristic zero is then a pure 256th root of unity. Equality in either finite field of exact root order 256 forces that phase to be trivial. Conversely an equality in characteristic zero specializes at both primes because the denominators consist of differences of distinct roots. This proves the joint equivalence. The calculation leaves open the two separate unrestricted production questions.
