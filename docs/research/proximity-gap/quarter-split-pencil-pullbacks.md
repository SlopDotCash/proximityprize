# Which pullbacks could produce the quarter-rate split pencil?

Let $H=\mu_n$, $n=2^{30}$, in the first designated prime field. Put $D=n/4-2=268435454$. The quarter-rate successor construction asks for a rational function $R=P/Q$ of degree $D$ whose fibers above $0,1,\infty$ consist of $D$ distinct points each in $H$. All three fibers are unramified, disjoint, and avoid the source point at infinity. We allow decompositions $R=V\circ U$ over the algebraic closure, so the obstructions also apply to constructions defined over the named field. [Split-pencil criterion](quarter-successor-split-pencil.md)

The only possible proper inner degrees $\deg U>1$ are **$2$, $19173961$, $38347922$, and $134217727$**. The first permits exactly the source involution types described below. The other three have respective outer degrees $14$, $7$, and $2$. The existence of a pencil of one of these types, or of an indecomposable pencil, remains open. Lean proves the source-symmetry theorem, the integer cutoff, and the divisor classification. The bound on the intersection of a general curve with $H^2$ uses the published theorem below. [Lean declarations](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitFiberSymmetry.lean)

## A source symmetry is unique if it exists

A nonsingular Möbius map preserving any one complete fiber sends $268435454$ points of $H$ back into $H$. Every such map except $x\mapsto ux$ or $x\mapsto u/x$ sends at most $3960117$ points back to $H$, by the proved subgroup-intersection bound. Evaluating the map at one fiber point shows that $u\in H$. If it is a scaling, taking the product of all elements of the fiber before and after scaling gives $u^D=1$. As $\gcd(n,D)=2$, its only possible nonidentity value is $u=-1$. [Production intersection bound](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/MobiusSubgroupBound.lean)

Negation and a scaled reciprocal cannot both preserve the three fibers. If they did, squaring would map the three fibers to disjoint sets, each of odd size $D/2=134217727$. The induced involution $w\mapsto u^2/w$ would have a fixed point in each of those sets. Its only possible nonzero fixed points are $u$ and $-u$. Two different reciprocals cannot both preserve the fibers either, since their composite would be a nonidentity scaling. Thus at most one nonidentity source automorphism preserves all three fibers, even just as sets. In particular, no Galois inner cover of degree greater than two can yield the pencil. Every deck transformation of such a cover would preserve all three fibers. [Lean source-symmetry proof](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitFiberSymmetry.lean)

Every quadratic inner map is separable here. Its fixed subfield must be either $\overline{\mathbb F}_p(x^2)$ or $\overline{\mathbb F}_p(x+u/x)$ with $u\in H$. Each displayed generator has degree two and is fixed by the corresponding involution, so the original inner map differs from it by a Möbius change on its target. Consequently the power lift $x\mapsto x^2$ requires three complete fibers of **odd degree $134217727$ on $\mu_{2^{29}}$**. A second squaring cannot produce that degree. In the reciprocal case the same odd degree is required on $\{x+u/x:x\in H\}$. Values corresponding to fixed points must be omitted because their preimages ramify. Conversely, any three complete fibers of this odd degree on the respective image set, using only values with two preimages in $H$, pull back to the required degree-$D$ pencil. Finding such a pencil on either quotient is still necessary for these two constructions.

## An obstruction for inner maps without symmetries

Write $t=\deg U>1$. Above every point in each of the three specified fibers of $R$, all $t$ sites in its $U$-fiber are distinct and lie in the same specified fiber. There are therefore at least $3D(t-1)$ ordered pairs $(x,y)\in H^2$ with $x\ne y$ and $U(x)=U(y)$.

The equation $U(x)=U(y)$ defines a curve of bidegree $(t,t)$ in $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$. Removing the diagonal leaves bidegree $(t-1,t-1)$. It has no vertical or horizontal components since $U$ is nonconstant. On a component where $x,y$ are multiplicatively dependent modulo constants, the primitive character relation gives a parametrization $x=c z^a$, $y=c' z^b$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$ in absolute value. Comparing the degrees of $U(cz^a)$ and $U(c'z^b)$ gives $|a|=|b|=1$. Thus such a component is the graph of a scaling or a reciprocal preserving $U$ and hence all three specified fibers of $R$. At most one off-diagonal component of this type can occur, contributing at most $n$ points. The diagonal has multiplicity one since $t\le D<p$ implies that $U$ is separable.

Consider a remaining irreducible component of bidegree $(a,b)$ over the algebraic closure. On its smooth projective normalization, the coordinate functions have degrees $b,a$. Their zeros and poles occupy at most $2a+2b$ places, and the genus is at most $(a-1)(b-1)$. Thus deleting the zeros and poles gives $\chi=2g-2+|S|\le 2ab$. Apply Corvaja and Zannier, Theorem 2, to the multiplicatively independent functions $x^n,y^n$. The coordinate projections have degrees smaller than $p$, and $p\nmid n$, so both functions have nonzero differentials. Each point of this component in $H^2$ lifts to at least one place counted by their gcd bound. Its contribution is at most
\[
\max\!\left(\sqrt[3]{108n^2 a^2b^2},\,12n^2ab/p\right).
\]
The second term is below one since $12n^2D^2<p$ for the first named prime. [Corvaja and Zannier, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/409)

The sums of the component degrees in each coordinate are at most $t-1$. Cauchy–Schwarz and $\sum_i a_i^{4/3}\le(\sum_i a_i)^{4/3}$ give $\sum_i(a_i b_i)^{2/3}\le(t-1)^{4/3}$. Combining the upper and lower counts yields
\[
3D(t-1)\ \le\ n + \sqrt[3]{108n^2(t-1)^4}.
\]
Lean verifies that this inequality fails throughout the integer interval $3\le t\le4194300$. If $t$ exceeds this interval and divides $D$, its only possibilities are $19173961,38347922,134217727,D$. The last corresponds to a degree-one outer map and imposes no decomposition. [Checked cutoff and divisor calculation](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitFiberSymmetry.lean)

For the unrestricted map $U=R$, this estimate is too large even if its off-diagonal curve is irreducible and contributes no symmetry graph. Put $m=D-1$. The fibers require $3Dm=216172778087251986$ ordered pairs. Projecting to $x$ allows $nm=288230372930486272$. The floor of the torsion estimate $(108n^2m^4)^{1/3}$ is $864691115570233368$. Thus controlling the genus or another property of this particular curve is necessary to exclude a pencil by this estimate. These are written consequences of the displayed bounds.

The four outermost coefficient rows of the three monic candidate vanishers also admit false positives at these parameters. Delete the two fixed points of $x\mapsto 1/x$ in $H$, then allocate disjoint inverse pairs to sets $A,B,C$ of size $D$. Their monic vanishers are palindromic with constant term one. The degree-$D-1$ coefficient is the negative sum of the pair traces $x+x^{-1}$, and equals the linear coefficient. Different inverse pairs have distinct traces, since $x$ is determined up to inversion by its trace. Choose $A,B$ with different trace sums, which is possible by varying the last pair of $B$. Fix all but the last pair in $C$. There are **134217731** choices for this pair. At least **134217729** give a third trace sum different from the first two. For each, the four rows at degrees $0,1,D-1,D$ admit a linear relation with every weight nonzero. At most one of the resulting monic vanishers of $C$ can be in the actual pencil of $A,B$. Two would be independent pencil members with shared roots in the fixed part of $C$, impossible because the vanishers of $A,B$ are coprime. This gives at least **134217728** false positives for the four-row test.

## A primitive factor must have alternating or symmetric monodromy

Every complete decomposition of a candidate over the algebraic closure has an indecomposable factor of degree
\[
m\in\{19173961,38347922,134217727,268435454\}
\]
whose geometric monodromy group on its $m$ sheets is $A_m$ or $S_m$. There is at most one preceding factor, of degree two, on the source side. In particular the monodromy of the whole pencil has $A_m$ as a composition factor. This excludes every solvable monodromy construction, including radical towers. Whether such a large alternating or symmetric cover can split the three prescribed fibers on the named subgroup remains open.

**The degree and the characteristic.** Read the decomposition from the source outward. The first prefix degree greater than two is one of the four large degrees by the collision-curve bound above, applied also to the whole map if this prefix finishes the decomposition. Before this stage the prefix degree is one or two. Dividing an even large degree by two gives either 19,173,961 or 134,217,727. Each possible stage degree divides the squarefree $D=2\cdot7\cdot73\cdot262657$ and is divisible by $\ell=262657$ and by 73. Its monodromy $G$ is primitive by the Galois correspondence and Lüroth's theorem. By the classification of primitive groups of squarefree degree, $G$ is almost simple with transitive nonabelian simple socle $T$. In particular $m\mid |T|$. Since $p>D$, the group order divides $m!$ and is prime to $p$. The prime-to-$p$ specialization theorem gives branch permutations of product one generating $G$, with their tame inertia cycle lengths. Riemann–Hurwitz gives a genus-zero system in this same permutation action. The following uses the genus-zero group theorems on that system. [Li and Seress](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024609303002145) · [SGA 1, Exposé XIII, Corollaire 2.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206203) · [Lean degree calculation](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitMonodromy.lean)

**The possible Lie-type fields.** The sporadic and exceptional Lie-type primitive genus-zero cases have degree at most 280 and 65 respectively. For $T=\mathrm{PSL}_2(q)$ the rank-one genus-zero classification gives $q\le64$ (including the alternating low-parameter isomorphisms). For all other simple classical $T$ over $\mathbb F_q$ the Liebeck–Saxl fixed-point-ratio bound gives $q\le113$. Here is the last deduction. Outside the small-field exceptions, every nonidentity group element fixes a proportion at most $4/(3q)$. Let $d_i$ be the inertia orders in the genus-zero system. The signature must be hyperbolic, since finite quotients of a Euclidean sphere orbifold group are solvable, and the spherical cases are cyclic, dihedral or have order at most 60. Hence $\sum_i(1-1/d_i)\ge2+1/42$, with the minimum attained at $(2,3,7)$. Averaging the fixed-point bound over the powers of each inertia generator bounds its index below by $m(1-4/(3q))(1-1/d_i)$. For $q\ge114$ the resulting total exceeds $2m$, contradicting $2m-2$. [Liebeck and Saxl, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-63.2.266) · [Rank-one classification](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/genus-0-actions-of-groups-of-lie-rank-1) · [Genus-zero degree bounds and references, pp. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1303)

**The classical order test.** Let $d$ be the natural dimension, using types $D_r$ only for $r\ge4$ to avoid the low-rank isomorphisms. The classical order formulas and $\ell\mid |T|$ imply $q^i\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell$ for some $1\le i\le d$, since $q\le113<\ell$. For untwisted linear groups the sign must be positive. For unitary groups it is $(-1)^i$. In types $B,C,D$ these exponents are even except that the even-dimensional orthogonal group can use the additional factor $q^{d/2}\pm1$.

If the Lie rank is at most two, $d\le6$. Otherwise Li–Seress gives a parabolic point stabilizer on $T$, so $m\ge q^{d-2}$. To see the latter bound, the opposite unipotent cell on the flag variety has $q^h$ points for $h$ its dimension. In type $A_{d-1}$ every proper parabolic quotient has dimension at least $d-1$. In types $B_r,C_r$ the minimum dimension is $2r-1$ for $r\ge3$ and in $D_r$ it is $2r-2$ for $r\ge4$. These dimensions follow by subtracting the number of positive roots of the Levi factor from that of the group. They apply to the absolute root system of a twisted form as well, since the flag and its opposite cell are defined over $\mathbb F_q$. Thus $i\le6$ or $q^{i-2}\le D$, and $i<30$.

Testing every integer $2\le q\le113$ and $1\le i<30$ under this size bound leaves only $(q,i)=(2,27),(8,9)$, with residue $+1$ in both cases. Each exponent is odd. The degree bound yields $27\le d\le29$ or $9\le d\le11$ respectively. Therefore neither exponent is $d/2$, and only $T=\mathrm{PSL}_d(q)$ remains. In each case a parabolic preserving an intermediate $k$-space, $2\le k\le d-2$, has index at least $q^{k(d-k)}\ge q^{2(d-2)}>D$. A flag of a line in a hyperplane also has dimension $2d-3$ and is too large. The only remaining actions are on lines or on hyperplanes. With $m\mid D$, their point counts $(q^d-1)/(q-1)$ leave just $\mathrm{PSL}_{27}(2)$ at $m=134217727$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_9(8)$ at $m=19173961$. Any automorphism extending these actions preserves the corresponding parabolic conjugacy class, so the full primitive action is a classical point action, up to duality. Frohardt–Guralnick–Magaard's Theorem 4 excludes genus zero for every such action of degree at least 10,000. [Squarefree-degree classification](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024609303002145) · [Point-action genus theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1303) · [Lean modular sieve](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitMonodromy.lean)

**Alternating action and whole-map monodromy.** This leaves $T=A_a$. As $\ell\mid |T|$, we have $a\ge262657$ and $m\le D<\binom a2$. The small-index subgroup theorem for $A_a$ shows that a proper subgroup of this index must be a point stabilizer in the natural action. Thus $a=m$ and $G=A_m$ or $S_m$ on the natural sheets. [Dixon and Mortimer, Theorem 5.2A](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0731-3)

Write the full map as $V\circ W\circ U$, where $W$ is this primitive stage and $\deg U\le2$. The monodromy of $V\circ W$ is a quotient of that of the full map. Its action has $s=\deg V\le14$ blocks of size $m$. The setwise stabilizer of a block induces the full geometric monodromy of $W$ there, by applying Galois correspondence over the corresponding intermediate function field. The kernel of the action on all blocks thus induces a normal subgroup of $G$ of index at most $s!$. Since $|A_m|>14!$, this normal image contains $A_m$. Hence $A_m$ is a composition factor of the kernel, then of the full geometric monodromy group $G_R$. The Galois closure of the whole pencil has genus at least $1+|G_R|/84\ge1+m!/168$: its tame Galois cover has hyperbolic signature by the same spherical and Euclidean exclusions, so Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g-2\ge |G_R|/42$.

There is also a consequence for applying torsion bounds to this factor: the locus of ordered, pairwise distinct $j$-tuples in unramified fibers of $W$ is geometrically irreducible for every $2\le j\le m-2$. Its geometric connected components are the orbits of $G$ on such tuples, and $A_m$ is $(m-2)$-transitive. In particular the off-diagonal collision curve of $W$ has a single component.

The classification and function-field arguments are written above. The stage degrees, modular orders, flag and point counts, alternating-group index bound and fixed-point-ratio comparison have Lean proofs.

The resulting pair curve excludes a $(2,3,7)$ signature in every case and gives stronger exclusions at the smaller prefix degrees. [Branch orders and genus restriction](quarter-split-branch-orders.md)
