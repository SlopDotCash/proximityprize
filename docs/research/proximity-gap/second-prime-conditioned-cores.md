# Conditioning on a secant at the second rate-sixteenth prime

For the repository's second prime, length $n=2^{30}$ and degree bound $d=2^{26}-1$, two-row MCA stays within the $2n$ bad-scalar budget at **647627101 errors**. Consequently the operational supremum is at least $647627102/n$. The known upper bound remains $715827882/n$. Two further results below constrain any violation at the last open predecessor, 715827881 errors. Every such selection of bad witnesses must have affine dimension at least seventeen, and at most one of its secants can contain 186 selected points. ## Incidence facts

Fix two received rows and choose one witnessing polynomial $P_\gamma$ per selected bad scalar. Let $A_\gamma$ be its full agreement set and $t=n-e$. A secant in the space of selected points $(\gamma,P_\gamma)$ is an affine family $A+\gamma B$. Its joint core $C$ is exactly the intersection of the agreement sets of any two selected points on it. Distinct cores intersect in at most $d$ coordinates. Every outside point agrees on at most $d$ positions of $C$, by the root bound for $P_\gamma-A-\gamma B$. Writing $z=|C|$ and $L$ for the line occupancy gives $L\le e+1$ and $(L-1)(t-z)\le e$ when $z<t$. Each bad scalar on a line needs at least one fresh agreement outside its core. [Repository incidence lemmas](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_TwoLargeCoreCount.lean)

Write $f(v)=v(v-1)(v-2)$. In a subfamily of exactly $m$ selected points let $m_x$ count the agreeing points at $x$. The mean number of common agreements of an unordered triple is $W=\sum_x f(m_x)/f(m)$. A noncollinear triple has at most $d$ common agreements; a collinear triple has exactly its core. If every secant has occupancy at most $B$ and every core has size at most $z$, counting completions of a pair gives

$$W\le d+(B-2)(z-d)/(m-2),\qquad z\ge d.$$

This estimate uses only the subfamily in question. For $v\ge2$, $y\ge0$, the tangent lower bound follows from $f(y)-f(v)-f'(v)(y-v)=(y-v)^2(y+2v-3)\ge0$. [Third-moment upper bound](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/_ThirdMomentUpperLineCap.lean)

## The improved unconditional floor

Suppose $e=647627101$, $t=426114723$ and select $2n+1$ bad scalars. Some coordinate occurs in at least $m_1=\lceil(2n+1)t/n\rceil=852229447$ agreement sets. Keep exactly this many of them and delete their common coordinate. Each remaining agreement set has size at least $t-1$, every noncollinear triple has at most $d-1$ common coordinates, and the remaining weight of a collinear triple is its core size minus one.

Set $s=172000000$. If every secant in this subfamily had global core size at most $s-1$, each would have at most $1+\lfloor e/(t-s+1)\rfloor=3$ selected points. On the $n-1$ remaining coordinates, the tangent at $a=m_1(t-1)/(n-1)$ would give

$$ (n-1)f(a)/f(m_1)\le W\le d-1+(s-1-d)/(m_1-2).$$

The lower expression exceeds the upper by more than $11/100$. Fix a secant with at least $s$ core positions and keep any $s$ of them as $S$.

Remove this secant and at most two more secants already found. At least $m=2n+1-3(e+1)=204602343$ points remain. Keep exactly $m$. Each has at most $d$ agreements on $S$ and at least $t$ overall. Put $a=md/s$ and $b=m(t-d)/(n-s)$. Then $2\le a<b$. Apply the tangents at $a$ on $S$ and $b$ off $S$. The total incidence is at least $mt$ and the incidence on $S$ is at most $md$. Since $0\le f'(a)\le f'(b)$, the sum of all linear corrections is nonnegative. Thus

$$W\ge [s f(a)+(n-s)f(b)]/f(m).$$

If the remaining points determined no secant with core of size $z=350093427$ or more, their lines would have occupancy at most $9$. The upper bound on $W$ would be $d+7(z-1-d)/(m-2)$. The lower exceeds this by more than 10827. Successively extracting and removing a line therefore produces three distinct secants with cores of size at least $z$. A point outside all three agrees on at most $3d$ sites in their union, whose size is at least $3z-3d$. Since $n+6d<t+3z$, such a point cannot reach $t$ agreements. The three secants contain at most $3(e+1)<2n+1$ bad scalars, a contradiction. Monotonicity and constancy between consecutive Hamming steps give the claimed floor. Lean verifies this argument from the definition of a bad-scalar witness through both endpoints of the named operational supremum. The two endpoint classifications below have written proofs with checked arithmetic. ## At most one 186-point secant at the final predecessor

Now take $e=715827881$, $t=357913943$ and $Z=350457399$. Call a core large if its size is at least $Z$. Any line with a smaller core has at most 96 selected bad scalars. Suppose two secants each contain at least 186 selected points. Each core contains a subset of size $z=t-\lfloor e/185\rfloor=354044604$. Denote the two subsets by $C_1,C_2$; their intersection size $j$ is at most $d$. Every outside point has at most $d$ agreements on each of them.

Keep $m=626349393$ points outside both lines. The same argument applies after deleting more lines, whenever that many points remain. Let $V=2(z-d)$, $O=n-2z+d$, $a=2md/V$, $b=m(t-2d)/O$, and $I(v)=f(v)-vf'(v)$. The exact verifier checks $2\le a<b$, $2f'(a)\le f'(b)$, and $I(b)\le2I(a)$. On positions in neither $C_i$ use the tangent at $b$, and on positions in exactly one use the tangent at $a$. In their intersection use $f(m_x)\ge(2f'(a)-f'(b))m_x$, which holds since $f(m_x)\ge0$ for integer $m_x\ge0$. The total linear charge is at least $f'(b)mt-(f'(b)-f'(a))2md$. The intercept part is $(n-2z+j)I(b)+(2z-2j)I(a)$, minimized at $j=d$. At that value the sum simplifies to $Of(b)+Vf(a)$. Hence if no new large core existed, comparison would give

$$[Of(b)+Vf(a)]/f(m)\le W\le d+94(Z-1-d)/(m-2).$$

The left side exceeds the right by more than 2233. This forces a further large secant whenever at least $m$ uncovered points remain.

For completeness, the three-, four- and five-core closure uses the following counts. Write a head as a point $(A_i,B_i)$ over $F(X)$. A noncollinear triple of heads has a nonzero homogeneous determinant with rows $(1,A_i,B_i)$ of degree at most $2d$. At a coordinate in $v\ge2$ of its cores, row subtraction shows that the determinant has a root of multiplicity at least $v-1$. Its three cores therefore have union size at least their summed sizes minus $2d$. For three large noncollinear heads an outside point agrees at most $n-3Z+5d<t$ times. Thus these three would cover the family and have capacity at most $3(e+1)<2n+1$. A triple of large heads must instead be collinear over $F(X)$.

For four heads, if they are not all collinear, at most one triple determinant is identically zero. The nonzero triples can each pay $2d$ in the preceding multiplicity count. For a zero triple charge its three pair intersections, costing at most $3d$. At a site matching two, three or four heads, the charge across the four triples is at least $2,5,8$ respectively. Thus $2(4Z-n)\le9d$, contradicted by the exact parameters. Four large heads must all be collinear over $F(X)$. Five large heads are impossible because $5Z>n+10d$, by counting pair intersections.

For $j=3,4$ collinear heads the distinct fresh labels supplied by the lines number at most $jn-(j-1)|U|$ where $U$ is their core union. Each site of $U$ supplies at most one fresh scalar: the vanishing triple determinants specialize to collinearity of the evaluated pairs. Every other site supplies at most $j$. Also $|U|\ge jZ-\binom j2d$. Starting with two large heads leaves at least $2n+1-2(e+1)=715827885$ uncovered points. With three collinear large heads at least 626349393 remain; with four at least 850045607 remain. Each time the three-zone moment argument using the original two cores forces another large head, ultimately forcing the impossible fifth. This proves that a budget violation has at most one 186-point secant. In particular removing at most one line leaves at least $2n+1-(e+1)=1431655767$ selected points with every secant containing at most 185.

## Affine dimension at least seventeen

Here dimension is the affine dimension over $F$ of the selected points $(\gamma,P_\gamma)$. At a coordinate $x$, the equation $P(x)=u_0(x)+\gamma u_1(x)$ defines an affine hyperplane. Any selected family of affine dimension at least two has at most $d$ universally satisfied coordinates since it contains a noncollinear triple. Suppose a family of $M$ points has at least $u$ agreements per point on a region of at most $N$ coordinates. After discarding its $c\le d$ universally satisfied coordinates, some remaining hyperplane in that region contains at least $\lceil M(u-c)/(N-c)\rceil\ge\lceil M(u-d)/(N-d)\rceil$ selected points. The new span has strictly smaller affine dimension. All numerical applications below have $N>d$ and $u>d$. If the original span has dimension at most 16, at most fifteen iterations of this count produce a line. A line reached earlier retains the larger count.

Start at the preceding $e,t,Z$ with $2n+1$ points. Fifteen cuts with $(N,u)=(n,t)$ give a line with at least 18 points, hence a joint core of size at least $t-\lfloor e/17\rfloor=315806421$. If already large, retain it. Otherwise this line contains at most 96 points. Outside it there are $2n+1-96$ or more points, each having at least $t-d$ agreements away from the core. Cutting that smaller region down from dimension 16 gives a line with at least 98 points, hence a large core.

Once a first large line is available, its complement has at most $n-Z$ coordinates. Applying the same fifteen cuts to the remaining points with at least $t-d$ agreements there forces a second large line with at least 141 points. With $j\ge2$ large lines already available, use the complement of two of their cores, of size at most $n-2Z+d$, where every outside point has at least $t-2d$ agreements. At $j=2,3,4$ the lower bounds just proved for the uncovered population give lines with at least $1599,1399,1898$ points respectively. Each forces another large core. The five-core contradiction excludes every overbudget family of affine dimension at most sixteen. Both classifications describe necessary conditions for a counterexample; neither limits the dimension of a general family of bad witnesses.
