## Reduction to three saturated heads

Use $e=715827877$, $t=n-e=357913947$ and $d=76695847$. A double interpolant with value degree at most seven, total scalar/value degree 25 and coordinate weight at most $2t-1$ contains every selected witness. Its surplus is $\sum_{j=0}^7(26-j)(2t-jd)-77n=1073742436$. Factors independent of the value variable represent at most 25 scalar labels. For positive value degrees two through seven the nonlinear caps give respectively **9,444, 25,759, 49,104, 77,031, 107,265 and 137,704** labels. Their discriminant coordinate degrees are below the designated characteristic, and $t^2>nd$.

Put $L=500000$. A linear factor with more than $L$ labels is an affine head by the identity-site test, since $t-\lfloor25n/L\rfloor-3\lfloor25n/(L-25)\rfloor>d$. Each such head has a joint core of size at least $C=t-\lfloor e/(L-1)\rfloor=357912516$ and accounts for at most $e+1$ labels. At most seven heads can occur. If four contain a noncollinear triple, their core sizes sum to at most $n+4d<4C$, an impossibility. For $j\ge3$ collinear heads the size of their union is at least
$$V_j=\max\left(jC-\binom j2d,\left\lceil\frac{jC^2}{C+(j-1)d}\right\rceil\right).$$
Counting distinct fresh labels there gives at most $jn-(j-1)V_j+(7-j)L+25\le1535925483$ for $3\le j\le7$. With at most two large heads the bound is at most $2(e+1)+5L+25<2n$. Thus exceeding $2n$ requires exactly three large noncollinear heads. Each needs at least $2n+1-2(e+1)-4L-25=713827868$ labels. Since twice this number exceeds $n$, disjoint fresh agreements force each core to have at least $t-1$ sites. ## Restricting outside witnesses to a fourth head

Write $U$ for the union of those three cores and $s=n-|U|$. Their nonzero determinant has degree at most $2d$, so $s\le n-3(t-1)+2d$. A polynomial outside all three heads can meet each core at at most $d$ agreement sites. Its agreements on the complementary $s$ sites therefore number at least $\tau=t-3d$. Three outside witnesses have at least $3\tau-2s$ common agreement sites there. At such a site the folded values all lie on the received affine line. If $3\tau-2s>d$, their degree-$d$ scalar interpolation identity holds as a polynomial. This puts every outside point on the affine head determined by any two outside points. The numerical inequality follows from $14d<9t-2n-6$.

If there are two outside points, let $c$ be the core size of their head. It differs from the original three, so the four-head determinant gives $3(t-1)+c\le n+4d$, hence $c\le306783374$. For $M$ outside labels, disjoint fresh agreements give $M(t-c)\le n-c$. At $M=15$ this fails even at the largest possible $c$. Consequently there are at most fourteen outside labels, and the whole family contains at most
$$3(e+1)+14=2147483648.$$
The same bound holds immediately for zero or one outside point. The three-head locator already fails by 715827882 errors. Dimension monotonicity transfers predecessor safety to smaller dimensions because the prescribed agreements exceed the largest dimension used, and the established dimension-two attack persists. Interleaving invariance gives every positive width. ## A necessary split pencil at the first dimension beyond the exact window

Return to $e=715827881$, $t=357913943$, and $d=71582789$. At this degree $n+5d=4t-3$. The same value-degree-seven interpolant has positive surplus and the small-factor and collinear estimates above still have slack (the weight decreases by eight and the individual nonlinear prices stay below $L$ by the logarithmic formula). Hence a failure needs the same three saturated noncollinear heads and at least three outside labels, since $3(e+1)=2n-2$. Every inequality bounding one outside witness by $n-3(t-1)+5d=t$ agreements must be tight. All three cores have $t-1$ sites. The witness agrees everywhere off their union and on exactly $d$ sites of each core, with all those sites outside the other two cores.

The outside labels lie on a fourth head $H_4$ by the complementary agreement argument. The complement has size $s=n-3(t-1)+2d$ and lies in its joint core because at least two distinct outside labels agree there. For each old head $H_i$ take the monic common polynomial factor $G_i$ of the two coordinates of $H_i-H_4$, of degree $g_i$. For every outside label $z$, the scalar difference of these heads at $z$ has degree exactly $d$ and $d$ distinct roots in the exclusive part of the $i$th core. Thus all roots of $G_i$ are simple and lie in this exclusive part. They also belong to the fourth core. The sets for different $i$ are disjoint, so $s+\sum g_i\le c_4\le n+4d-3(t-1)$, and $\sum g_i\le2d$.

For at least one $i$, dividing the coordinate pair $H_i-H_4$ by $G_i$ therefore gives a coprime polynomial pencil of degree $d-g_i\ge\lceil d/3\rceil=23860930$. Its fibers at the three distinct outside scalars all have full degree and split into simple roots on the supplied evaluation domain. The three root sets are pairwise disjoint because the two coordinates are coprime. On the designated subgroup, a failure at this step therefore requires such a split pencil supported inside one exclusive old core. The condition is necessary. Its realizability together with the remaining core and label constraints is open.
