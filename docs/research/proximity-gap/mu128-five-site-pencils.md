# Five-point fibers for split pencils on the 128-site subgroup

At either named prime, take two degree-\(d\) polynomials with disjoint full root sets on \(\mu_{128}\). The largest possible nonzero finite fiber of their ratio has size **four** at \(d=5\) and \(d=7\). At \(d=6\), any nonzero finite fiber containing five grid points contains **six**, and all three sets of six are unions of antipodal pairs. These claims hold over the complex numbers by the same locator-ratio transfer. The quintic and septic upper bounds are sharp, as witnessed by direct evaluations of candidate support pairs. ## The enumeration

Among five chosen test sites, rotate so at least three exponents are even. The three-center parity lemma makes both disjoint supports antipodal on the odd exponents. Their monic locators and their ratio at each even site belong to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{64})\). Every odd test site therefore brings its antipode into the same fiber. We may select five even exponents or three even exponents plus one odd antipodal pair. If four even sites and one odd site were initially chosen, retain three of those even sites and use the odd site with its antipode. Affine normalization under rotation and odd Galois multipliers gives **4,085** all-even and **651** mixed representatives. The mixed representatives have odd pair \(\{1,65\}\); their three even indices vary under its stabilizer. [Parity and affine action](mu128-septic-six-site-fibers.md#six-test-sites-and-the-cyclotomic-equations)

Use the same mod-three primitive-unit equations as in the six-test census, with four differences of test evaluations. There are 64 singleton and 32 paired coordinates, with physical weights one and two. A solution needs ordinary positive and negative weights \(d\) each. All summands in the root-support vector lie in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), so modulo three preserves them without ambiguity.

The main traversal packs information sets of the ternary kernel. An information set is a set of coordinate columns giving a nonsingular square submatrix of a kernel generator matrix. Row operations turn those coordinates into an identity matrix. Pack \(m\) such sets subject to using each singleton at most once and each paired coordinate at most twice across the whole packing. Gaussian elimination finds the sets; only the verified rank, coordinate capacities and row operations enter the argument. For a codeword of physical support weight \(2d\), the total number of its occupied coordinates across the packed sets is at most \(2d\). Some information set therefore has at most \(\lfloor2d/m\rfloor\) nonzeros. For each set, enumerate every choice of that many or fewer systematic coordinates and their two nonzero signs. The resulting vector is uniquely determined. Test its two physical weights, discarding duplicate outputs already covered by an earlier set. The independent direct traversal in the same program searches all ternary coordinates of a single kernel basis, pruning only when an irrevocably fixed weight exceeds \(d\). ## What 33 useful coincidences require of four sextics

Let \(C=\sum_x(\max_y\#\{i:f_i(x)=y\}-1)\). Four distinct sextics on either named 128-site grid have \(C\le33\). Equality can occur only when three words, after subtracting one of them, are cubics in \(X^2\) and have three fully split six-point differences. The fourth word meets each of those three on exactly five sites, with all 33 pair sites distinct. Existence in this remaining case is open.

Let \(r_{ij}\) count the equality sites of each word pair, \(D=\sum_{i<j}(6-r_{ij})\), and \(W\) the sum of the differences between the number of equal pairs at a site and its useful coincidence count. Then \(36-C=D+W\le3\). A quadruple point costs three units, so it would force every edge fully split and no other coincident pair sites. Removing the common linear factor from the differences would give a disjoint full quintic triangle, excluded above. Hence every all-equal triple of words costs one unit of waste and belongs to a unique triple of indices.

For a triangle of pair counts \(6,6,5\) with no common site, the census forces the third edge to have six roots, a contradiction. A triangle of counts \(6,6,6\) with exactly one common site becomes a forbidden disjoint quintic triangle after removing that site. For every disjoint \(6,6,6\) triangle, the fully split sextic classification shows that all three edge differences are cubics in \(X^2\). Two such triangles on four vertices share an edge, so subtracting one word puts all four in the cubics in \(X^2\). Their coincidence count is then at most \(2\cdot16=32\). [Cubic maximum and sextic classification](mu128-split-sextics.md#four-cubics-on-the-64-site-subgroup)

The finite case check enumerates every six-tuple of nonnegative edge deficits with sum \(D\le3\) and every four-tuple of common-triple counts with total at most \(3-D\). Apply the preceding three exclusions and relabel vertices. The sole surviving case has three zero deficits around one triangle, one lost root on each edge to the fourth vertex, and zero common-triple counts. Since all three allowed units were spent on deficits, there are no sites containing two disjoint equal pairs either. This gives the claimed structure and cap.

In particular any four septics with at least **36** useful coincidences have at most one common grid root. Two common roots yield at most \(6+30=36\) by dividing the four translated words by their common quadratic. Equality in that bound would require a disjoint fully split quintic triangle, which the census excludes. One common root gives \(C\le3+33=36\), and equality requires exactly the sextic configuration just described. The unrestricted four-word threshold on the named 128-site grid continues to lie between 87 and 88 errors. [Known matching bounds](mu128-septic-six-site-fibers.md#four-word-consequence)
