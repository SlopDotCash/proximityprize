# Three arbitrary target fibers of a four-monomial cross ratio on the first named subgroup

Let $H=\mu_n$ with $n=2^{30}$ in the first named prime field. Let $R$ be a nonconstant cross ratio of four different scaled Laurent monomials over the algebraic closure, of degree $m\le n/4-2$. For any three different projective values, let $r$ count the sites of $H$ whose **reduced** image under $R$ is one of them. Then
\[
                 2r-3m\le 9n/16=603979776 .
\]
Thus none of these pencils improves the three-head attack at 480946859 errors, even after an arbitrary target projectivity. The named unrestricted three-head maximum allows every coprime polynomial pair and remains open. [Three-head root score](quarter-three-head-root-score.md)

## Reduction and ranges

Let $g$ be the gcd of the four exponent differences from any one exponent. Dividing all four monomials by a common power and dividing the resulting exponents by $g$ gives a primitive cross ratio $R_0$ with $R(X)=R_0(X^g)$. Put $k=\gcd(n,g)$ and $N=n/k$. Then $m=g m_0$, where $m_0=\deg R_0$, and $r=k r_0$, where $r_0$ counts the three images on $\mu_N$. Since $m_0<N/4$ and $g\ge k$, bounding $2r_0-3m_0$ by $9N/16$ suffices. If $N\le16$ this follows from $r_0\le3m_0$ and the integral degree bound. At $N=32$ the unrestricted degree-at-most-seven root-score bound applies. At $N=64$ the separate finite census handles arbitrary targets. Every dyadic $512\le N\le2^{30}$ is covered by the moderate- and large-quotient argument. The remainder of this note treats $N=128,256$. [32-site proof](quarter-three-heads-32-fibers.md) · [64-site proof](quarter-monomial-mu64.md) · [All sizes from 512 upward](quarter-monomial-moderate-quotients.md)

Write $m,r$ for the primitive quantities until the end. In an improving triple every fiber would have size larger than $9N/32-m/2>5N/32$, since each of the other two contributes at most $m$. The three targets from the pairings of coincident source monomials will be called collision targets. Their joint score is already bounded. Thus one selected target is a noncollision target. At $N=256$ it has at least 41 sites. At $N=128$ some noncollision target has at least 22 sites. Otherwise two collision targets and one fiber of size at most 21 are needed, and $2(2m+21)-3m>72$ with $m\le31$ forces sizes $(31,31,21)$. The named two-full-fiber exclusion rules this out. The exclusion applies over the algebraic closure because the two full fibers give monic vanishers in the named field and any site of the third fiber specifies its value there up to the same target scaling. ## Repeated exponents

With at most two distinct exponents the primitive degree is at most two. With three distinct exponents, divide by the repeated power. The entries are then distinct nonzero constants $A,B$ and $CX^u,DX^v$ with $\gcd(u,v)=1$. At least one varying exponent is odd, giving a bijective parameter on $\mu_N$. A noncollision target requires the two varying values to be related by a projectivity fixing $A,B$. A projectivity other than a scaling or a reciprocal scaling has at most $b$ matches with a power on a cyclic grid of size $N$, where $b(b-1)\le2(N-1)$. This gives $b\le16,23$ at the two sizes. The scaling case gives a collision target. The reciprocal case requires $B=-A$ and gives a single possible harmonic target. The four possible large fibers in that case have upper bounds
\[
\gcd(N,u)+\gcd(N,v),\quad\gcd(N,u)+\gcd(N,v),\quad
\gcd(N,u-v),\quad\gcd(N,u+v).
\]
The first two come from hitting the two constants in either order, the third from equality of the moving entries, and the fourth from their product $A^2$. These are upper bounds by the unreduced equations, including base points. The degree estimate gives span at most $m+2$, hence $|u\pm v|<N$. An identically true reciprocal equation yields a constant cross ratio and an identically false one contributes zero. For mixed parity the last two bounds are one and for two odd exponents the first two are two. Thus at most two fibers are large. In the absence of a reciprocal target all three would have to be collision targets. ## A gap divisible by 32

This reduction treats the 256-site case when two distinct exponents are congruent modulo 32. The same proof applies on 128 sites. For distinct exponents their span is at most $N/2+10$, by the degree estimate used below. Consequently every difference of two monomials has a simple zero at a nonzero coincidence, with leading Euler derivative $(a_i-a_j)T_i(x)$. At an exact triple the reduced cross ratio is one fixed value determined by the three exponent differences and the positions of the coinciding entries. Indeed both pairing products have one vanishing factor and the other factor is, up to sign, the same difference with the fourth entry. At an all-four coincidence the value may differ. Primitivity gives at most one such site.

Choose the congruent pair and divide by the first monomial power. On every coset of $\mu_{32}$ this pair has constant values $A,B$. When $A=B$, a site mapping to a noncollision target must have at least three coincident entries, because a unique vanishing pairing gives a collision target. When $A\ne B$, a noncollision target gives a projectivity fixing $A,B$ between the two remaining entries. At least one remaining exponent is odd, since the original differences have gcd one. After reparametrizing by this odd power and rescaling the argument and output, count matches of a power with a projectivity on $\mu_{32}$. The exact bound is four unless the projectivity is a scaling or reciprocal scaling. The verifier rotates one match to 1 and considers all pairs of further matches with distinct inputs and distinct outputs. The three pairs determine the projectivity, including over field extensions. It counts its entire graph on this grid by cross multiplication and discards the two special types at their anchors. Fewer than three matches already meet the cap. [Verifier](../../../scripts/probes/probe_quarter_monomial_mu128_mu256.py)

A scaling fixing the two constants is the identity and gives a collision target. A reciprocal fixing them requires $B=-A$ and relates the two moving values by $UV=A^2$. The coordinate $(Y-A)/(Y-B)$ changes sign under this reciprocal, so its noncollision target is the same harmonic value on every coset for the chosen pair.

* With two exponents of each parity, the two same-parity pairs form one perfect matching. Each of the other collision targets uses only two mixed-parity pairs, each with at most one grid coincidence. Every geometric triple includes a mixed pair, and there are at most four triple sites in all. Any noncollision target except the harmonic value therefore has at most $4N/32+4\le5N/32$ sites. Only one collision target and the possible harmonic value can be large.
* With three exponents of one parity, the two entries outside the chosen congruent pair have opposite parity. Even a reciprocal contributes at most one point per $A\ne B$ coset. If the same-parity triple never occurs on the grid, every triple site uses one of at most three mixed-pair coincidences. Hence all noncollision targets have at most $4N/32+3$ sites. If the same-parity triple does occur, the three pair-equality sets in that triple are cosets of dyadic subgroups through a common point. They are nested and the two smaller ones coincide, by the two equal smallest 2-adic valuations in a triple of exponent differences. Outside the triple set only the largest of these three pair sets can contribute, leaving at most one collision target with more than the three mixed-pair sites. At an exact same-parity triple the reduced value is fixed as above and is a noncollision target, since the three exponent differences are nonzero. All other noncollision targets take at most $4N/32+4$ sites, allowing the mixed triples and the all-four exception. At most two values can be large.

## Sparse evaluation on the remaining grids

For four different exponents translate them to $0=a_0<a_1<a_2<a_3$. The degree argument gives $a_3\le N/2+10$ and the necessary inequality
\[
 a_3+a_2-a_1-\sum_{i<j<k}\gcd(a_j-a_i,a_k-a_i) < N/4.
\]
For the span estimate, if $a_3<N/2$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the large-span inequality there, with $m\le N/4-1$, gives $a_3\le N/2+10$. Enumerate every such primitive tuple, removing gaps divisible by 32 only at $N=256$. Form its three complementary pairs of pair-sum frequencies modulo $N$. Identify spectra under a common shift, an odd multiplier, and permutations of and within the complementary pairs. There is an odd difference between some two frequencies since the source exponents have both parities. It suffices to take the minimum representative over choices placing such an ordered pair at $0,1$. [Exponent and cancellation bounds](quarter-monomial-cross-ratio.md)

A noncollision target gives a vanishing six-term polynomial at all its sites. As in the 64-site proof, at six different frequencies the six coefficients must be nonzero and the three complementary products $A,B,C$ satisfy $(A+B-C)^2=4AB$. With one merged complementary pair, only the other four entries are required to be nonzero. The census checks that each retained spectrum has five or six distinct frequencies. To accelerate the 256-site case, restrict a spectrum to cosets of a subgroup of size $T\mid N$. If some mandatory coefficient is the unique frequency in its class modulo $T$, its polynomial is nonzero on every such coset. After an odd multiplier and shift taking its frequencies into a cyclic arc of width $w$, the root bound per coset is $w$. Discard that spectrum if $Nw/T<41$.

With threshold $t=22,41$, any $t$ cyclic roots have four within an arc of length $\lfloor3N/t\rfloor$ by averaging third-neighbor gaps. Rotate the first to exponent zero and enumerate all choices of the other three. The rotation scales all complementary products by the same factor. For each anchor, intersect its evaluation kernel with further root equations until finding the projective coefficient vectors with at least $t$ raw roots or a subspace with that many common roots. A one-dimensional kernel vector is tested for the obligatory coefficients and the complementary product identity. Every projective line cut by an additional independent grid equation gives just one coefficient vector over the named prime field. All the intersections and common roots thus cover fibers originally defined over its algebraic closure. Whenever a subspace of positive projective dimension already has $t$ common roots, the check verifies that in fact it has at least $N/4$. The same lower bound holds for every permissible singleton reaching $t$. Each flagged original tuple consequently needs a grid base point, since a degree $m<N/4$ reduced fiber cannot contain all those raw roots. Such a base point requires a geometric triple of coincident monomials. [64-site evaluation-kernel proof](quarter-monomial-mu64.md)

At 128 sites, rotate one grid triple to 1 and scale the four entries so three corresponding coefficients are one. The verifier treats all four choices of the remaining coefficient for each of the 27 tuples. For the remaining parameter in $\mu_{128}$ it evaluates all reduced images directly. Otherwise every site's reduced image is a pair of affine forms in that parameter. The finite exceptions occur when two nonidentical target functions coincide or the rational map drops degree. The same univariate elimination as for 64 sites checks these events over the algebraic closure, accounting for their possible increase of the top-three population. No specialization improves the score. Combining the two new orders with all the previous ranges proves the stated bound on $H$.
