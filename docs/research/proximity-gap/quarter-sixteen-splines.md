# Four cubic heads on the sixteen-site quarter-rate domain

On the order-sixteen subgroup of either named prime field, four degree-at-most-three polynomial heads cannot represent more than 32 bad scalar labels at eight agreements. The exact maximum within the balanced incidence pattern forced by an overrun is **22**. Over the field of 97 elements the same pattern attains **32**. The calculation does not bound the number of labels represented by five or more heads.

## Why the enumeration covers a four-head overrun

A head consists of two cubics. Its core is the set of positions where both agree with the two received rows. On its core complement a site supplies at most one new projective scalar. A bad witness must use a site outside the core. The general four-head count applies here with $t=8$ and $d=3$. Its small-parameter inequalities are $t-2=6>1+\lfloor t/2\rfloor=5$ and $3d-1=8>3$. Hence an overrun forces four seven-site cores, no triple site, one singleton per head, and six disjoint double strata. Each triangle has six double sites and every pair difference has nonconstant projective direction. Opposite edges have equal size and each edge has at most $d-1=2$ sites. Therefore every edge has two sites. [Four-head counting argument](quarter-saturated-symmetry.md#equality-in-the-four-head-count)

Translate head 0 to zero. For each of the other three scalar cubics keep its four coefficients. An edge $ij$ at $x$ contributes the homogeneous linear equation $P_i(x)-P_j(x)=0$. The resulting $12\times12$ matrix has one row per double site. The signed relation among the four triangle determinants is necessary for any overrun. For every surviving layout in all four fields the matrix has kernel dimension exactly two. Every genuine pair of scalar rows is thus a basis of the kernel. Changing this basis only permutes projective directions. A dependent pair has constant direction and cannot overrun.

At a double site take the common value of its two owners for the received pair. Assign the four singleton sites bijectively to the four heads and use their assigned values. These choices exhaust all 24 received families for the prescribed incidence. The probe checks the exact owner set at every double and that all four head values differ at each singleton. For a mismatch $(a,b)$ the unique projective label satisfying $a+\gamma b=0$ is $-a/b$, or infinity when $b=0$. Count distinct labels across the whole domain. A common shear $(A,B)\mapsto(A,B+sA)$ makes all labels finite. Each of the 36 mismatches forbids at most one $s$. Every resulting label has a witness of eight sites, consisting of the corresponding seven-site core and its mismatching site. Any joint pair of cubics agreeing on these eight sites would equal the chosen head on its seven core sites and consequently everywhere, contradicting the mismatch. The probe constructs these witnesses whenever the running maximum increases.

## Exact counts

The initial sieve uses the dihedral orbits of the four singleton sites and orders the three opposite-edge pairs by their least remaining site. Counts weight the singleton orbit size and retain the sieve's triangle orientations. They are counts in that enumeration, not counts of distinct received words. Rotation acts on cubics by substitution. Reflection substitutes $u/x$ and clears the common denominator by multiplying both rows by $x^3$, which preserves equality and direction at every nonzero domain point.

The spline and direction ranks themselves are stable for splitting primes $p>2^{144}$. Work over $\mathbb Z[\zeta_{16}]$. Direction equality is a rank test on the twelve incidence rows augmented by one or two mismatch rows. These rows all have at most eight entries of absolute value one under every complex embedding. Any nonzero square minor of order at most twelve has all conjugates of absolute value at most $8^6$ by Hadamard's inequality. Its absolute norm is at most $(8^6)^8=2^{144}$, so its reduction at any prime above a larger rational prime is nonzero. Zero minors stay zero. The signed-minor inventory transfers for $p>432000^8$. ## Obstruction to lifting the twelve double sites intact

Pull the twelve labelled double sites back along $x\mapsto x^2$ on the 32-site group. If two degree-seven rows agree along all twelve lifted edges on both sheets, their even and odd parts separately satisfy the cubic incidence equations on the original sixteen-site group. After subtracting head 0, those scalar cubics all belong to the same two-dimensional kernel. Thus at site $x$ the four head vectors are obtained from the four base vectors at $x^2$ by one common two-by-two matrix $M(x)$ whose entries have degree at most one. Each of the eight remaining sites lies above an old singleton, where every pair of base vectors differs. Creating a new double at four of these sites forces four distinct roots of $\det M(x)$. Its degree is at most two, so it vanishes identically and every triple of lifted heads is collinear over the function field. This rules out an exact, noncollinear 32-site saturation preserving all twelve doubled pair labels. It allows perturbations that release some of the old coincidences.

## Independent 32-site symmetry searches

Consider a noncollinear saturated quartet of row degree at most seven on the 32-site domain. Its four singleton sites and its 28 double sites have been specified, with equal sizes on opposite edges. Two independent scalar splines modulo common polynomials are necessary. For $\mathbb F_{65537}$ and both named primes, the probe exhausts faithful label actions of order-four subgroups of the domain's dihedral group. None has the required scalar splines.

* **Cyclic order four.** Relabel the vertices so rotation by $r=\zeta_{32}^8$ acts as $(0123)$. It has eight four-site orbits. A further rotation puts the unique singleton orbit at the one containing $1$. An ordinary orbit meets the four adjacent edges once each or the two diagonals twice each. If $D$ orbits use diagonals, then $0\le D\le3$ since no distinct pair of degree-seven heads can agree at eight sites. After fixing the first adjacent phase by cyclic relabelling, the probe tests every remaining edge phase. In rotation character $r^q$, the scalar solution has form $P_h(x)=r^{qh}P(r^{-h}x)$. Terms $x^d$ with $d\equiv q\pmod 4$ are common to all four heads and disappear in the quotient, leaving six columns in each character. One representative equation per ordinary orbit suffices. The sum of the four nullities is below two for every layout.
* **Klein four.** The half-turn $T\colon x\mapsto-x$ commutes on sites with the reflection $R\colon x\mapsto\zeta_{32}^b/x$. Up to conjugation by rotation, $b=0$ or $1$. Up to vertex relabelling there is one transitive action. An intransitive faithful action has two transposition orbits, and one chooses which of $T,R,TR$ is the double transposition. The probe treats all four cases, including the two-site orbits at even $b$. On degree-seven scalar tuples the reflection lifts to $P_h(x)\mapsto x^7P_{Rh}(\zeta_{32}^b/x)$. It exchanges the two eigenspaces of the lifted half-turn, since the degree is odd. Both preserve the constraints and common-polynomial subspace, so a nonzero quotient kernel has a nonzero half-turn-even member. That eigenspace has twelve quotient columns. The probe tests all four-site invariant hole sets and all edge placements compatible with stabilizers, rejecting edges of size above seven and unequal opposite-edge counts. Every remaining matrix has rank twelve.

The exclusion holds over the complex numbers as well. The full $28\times24$ incidence matrix after subtracting head zero has rank at least 23 in characteristic 65537. For each layout, a nonzero 23-minor in that field therefore lifts to a nonzero cyclotomic integer. Its conjugates have absolute value at most $4^{23}$ since each row has at most sixteen unit entries. The norm bound $4^{23\cdot16}=2^{736}$ transfers the exclusion to every splitting prime above that bound. The direct runs cover both named primes, which are smaller.

Neither search assumes the bad scalar directions are pairwise distinct. Both screen the weaker necessary condition of having two scalar splines with the prescribed pair agreements. An asymmetric quartet, or one with just a single involution, remains possible. [Order-four symmetry probe](../../../scripts/probes/probe_quarter_32_symmetries.py)

## Affine exponent symmetries over the complex numbers

In characteristic zero the degree-seven rank obstruction extends to every subgroup of order four of the full affine permutation group of the cyclotomic exponents, provided the subgroup acts faithfully on the four head labels. These permutations are $i\mapsto ai+b$ on $\mathbb Z/32$, with $a$ odd. In particular the symmetry can involve a Galois action on the primitive root as well as an ordinary rotation.

The probe constructs all 512 affine permutations, enumerates the order-four subgroups, and groups them under conjugation. There are five cyclic classes and six Klein four classes. A faithful cyclic label action is a four-cycle up to vertex relabelling. A Klein four subgroup has four possible embeddings up to vertex relabelling, as above. For each of the resulting 29 cases the program enumerates invariant four-site hole sets. At each ordinary orbit it assigns a vertex pair and propagates it by the group, discarding inconsistent stabilizers. Every edge may have at most seven sites and opposite edges must have the same size. Gaussian elimination on the full 24-column incidence matrix over $\mathbb F_{65537}$ prunes a branch as soon as the rank reaches 23. No admissible layout has rank below 23.

A complex solution would give one for the canonical representative of its subgroup. Indeed multiplication of the exponents by a unit acts on the cyclotomic coefficients by Galois conjugation, and index translation scales each polynomial coefficient by a nonzero power of the primitive root. Both preserve the ranks over the complex numbers. Reduction at 65537 cannot increase the rank of the resulting cyclotomic matrix, contradicting the calculation. The norm bound $2^{736}$ in the preceding section also applies to all these conjugates. Reduction at 65537 alone does not settle the extra Galois symmetry cases at the smaller named primes. ### The full affine stabilizer has order at most two

The same exclusion holds for the nonfaithful actions. Thus for a saturated noncollinear quartet of degree at most seven over the complex numbers, at most two affine exponent permutations can preserve the six double strata up to relabelling their vertices.

If the kernel on labels contains a geometric involution, its action fixes the labelled strata individually and contradicts the rotation and reflection certificates for a saturated quartet. Every nonfaithful cyclic action of order four has the square of its generator in the kernel. Where that square is nongeometric, the action on four vertices can be trivial, a transposition, or a double transposition. Each Klein subgroup in the enumeration contains a geometric involution, so it cannot act trivially. Its possible remaining actions have image of order two and a nongeometric order-two kernel. These possibilities yield 22 cases. The probe chooses hole sets up to affine exponent permutations commuting with the given domain action. At each successive orbit it also chooses one edge assignment under the still-available vertex relabellings commuting with the label action. These normalizations preserve the characteristic-zero classification. [Geometric kernel obstruction](quarter-saturated-symmetry.md)

The 65537 rank screen returns 142 records representing 125 distinct layouts, which reduce to 16 orbits under affine exponent permutation and vertex relabelling. To exclude each of these last orbits, append two rows to the incidence matrix $M$ for evaluating $P_b(0)-P_a(0)$ and $P_c(0)-P_a(0)$, for a fixed triple of heads. On a genuine saturated layout $M$ has nullity exactly two. To see this, fix the two rows of the putative quartet and take an arbitrary scalar spline. On heads $0,1,2$ the two Cramer numerators for expressing that spline in the known pair vanish at all fourteen sites of the corresponding triangle, as does the nonzero Cramer denominator. All three have degree at most fourteen. Each numerator is therefore a constant multiple of the denominator. These scalar constants also give the right value at the fourth head, by agreement at its fourteen double sites. It follows that $M$ has rank 22. A noncollinear triple at $X=0$ would then raise the augmented rank to 24.

The checker uses the prime $q=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$. It verifies its primality by checking an element of order $q-1$. For each of the 16 orbits it finds one fixed head triple whose two evaluation rows at zero raise the rank by at most one at **all sixteen** primitive thirty-second roots modulo $q$. Every full-size minor of this augmented matrix is consequently zero over the complex numbers. Indeed write that minor as $D=\sum_{j=0}^{15}c_j\zeta_{32}^j$ with integer coefficients. Every conjugate has absolute value at most $4^{24}$ by the row norm bound. Fourier inversion,
\[
c_j=\frac1{16}\sum_{u\text{ odd}\bmod32}\zeta_{32}^{-uj}\sigma_u(D),
\]
gives $|c_j|\le4^{24}$. Reduction of $D$ at all sixteen primitive roots gives sixteen roots of its degree-at-most-fifteen coefficient polynomial modulo $q$. Hence every $c_j$ is divisible by $q>4^{24}$, forcing every coefficient to vanish. The triple is collinear at $X=0$. Its face determinant already has fourteen distinct, nonzero roots, which forces it to vanish identically and contradicts saturation.

Each quartet has at least two nonempty incident edges at every vertex, since its per-vertex total is fourteen and each edge size is at most seven. A vertex relabelling that fixes all nonempty edges individually is therefore the identity. It follows that compatible affine permutations act unambiguously on the heads. Their subgroup sits inside the group of order 512. Any subgroup of order greater than two would contain a cyclic or Klein subgroup of order four, all of which have now been excluded. The same conclusion holds over splitting prime fields above $2^{736}$, by the 23-minor norm bound and the integral zero identities for the last 16 orbits. At the two named primes the separate geometric exclusions still apply.
