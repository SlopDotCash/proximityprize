# Monodromy of the two pencils at the first half-rate successor

Put $n=2^{30}$, $h=n/4$ and $D=h-1$. A received pair exceeding the first prime's half-rate budget at $h+1$ errors gives two degree-$D$ rational functions $F,G$. Each has three complete simple fibers on $H=\mu_n$. Each has an indecomposable right factor of degree **53,687,091, 89,478,485 or 268,435,455** with natural alternating or symmetric geometric monodromy. Each admits at most one proper intermediate field over its target. The two functions together generate the full rational function field. The existence of the required pair at the named prime remains open. [Punctured-list equivalence](first-prime-punctured-four-list.md) · [Two required pencils](four-fiber-quadratic-characters.md)

There is also a restriction on the other adjacent odd degree at either prime. Every degree-**268,435,457** rational function with three complete simple fibers on $H$ is indecomposable and has natural alternating or symmetric geometric monodromy.

The two lower-degree pencils also satisfy a joint restriction. A section-height argument rules out simultaneous decomposition, forcing at least one of them to have natural alternating or symmetric monodromy on all **268,435,455** sheets. [Joint pencil height](half-successor-joint-pencil-height.md)

## Off-diagonal components and intermediate fields

Work over the algebraic closure $K$ of either named field. Let $B$ be one of the odd-degree pencils just described, of degree $d=h\pm1$. Each irreducible off-diagonal component of $B(x)=B(y)$ has the same degree $a$ in both coordinates. Its ordered-pair orbit in the permutation action on the sheets has $da$ elements and specializes injectively in each of the three full fibers. Its normalization $W$ therefore contains at least $3da$ distinct points of $H^2$. An off-diagonal component defined by a multiplicative relation would be the graph of a scaling by an element of $H$ or a scaled reciprocal. Either would give a deck transformation of even order dividing the odd number $d$. The previous estimate for independent components gives $a\ge9{,}085{,}508$. [Sheet-pair and Kummer proofs](production-fiber-correspondence.md)

Let $W'$ be the normalization of the image under $(x,y)\mapsto(x^n,y^n)$. The covering degree $c$ is at most $n$, since its torus stabilizer projects injectively to the first coordinate in the absence of a nontrivial scaling deck transformation. The coordinate functions of $W'$ have common degree $na/c\ge a>1$ and their degrees sum to at most $2nd<p$. The three-dimensional Wronskian inequality applies. Write $\chi(W)=2g(W)-2+|S|$ with $S$ the support of the coordinate zeros and poles. Tame logarithmic Riemann–Hurwitz cancels $c$ in the pullback, and adjunction gives the second inequality below. [Large-characteristic Wronskian and pullback](quarter-split-wronskian-suborbits.md#self-contained-wronskian-gcd-bound-in-large-characteristic)

$$
3da\le \tfrac23na+\chi(W)\le \tfrac23na+2a^2,
\qquad a\ge\left\lceil\frac{9d-2n}{6}\right\rceil.
$$

The floors are **44,739,242** for $d=h-1$ and **44,739,245** for $d=h+1$. For any rational right factor $T$ of degree $m>1$, the off-diagonal components of $T(x)=T(y)$ are components of $B(x)=B(y)$ of total coordinate degree $m-1$. Thus $m$ exceeds the corresponding floor and the outer degree $s=d/m$ is at most five. At $h-1=3\cdot5\cdot29\cdot43\cdot113\cdot127$, this allows only $s=1,3,5$. At $h+1=17\cdot15{,}790{,}321$, it forces $s=1$. [Lean degree calculation](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/HalfSuccessorPencilMonodromy.lean)

If $L_1,L_2$ are proper intermediate fields over $K(B)$ in the lower degree, Lüroth's theorem and the degree test give indices $s_1,s_2\in\{3,5\}$ over $K(B)$. Their compositum has degree at most $s_1s_2\le25<D$. Its degree is divisible by both $s_i$ and must itself be 3 or 5. Both $L_i$ consequently equal the compositum. Thus a nontrivial decomposition, when present, is unique up to a Möbius change of intermediate parameter.

For a pencil defined over the named prime field, this uniqueness also gives a decomposition over that field. The unique geometric intermediate field is fixed by the action of Frobenius on the factorization. To see descent explicitly, choose a rational generator over a finite extension of constants. Its Frobenius conjugates differ by target Möbius transformations, defining a descended smooth genus-zero curve and maps from the original projective line to that curve and onward to the target. The image of any rational source point gives a rational point on the descended curve, so it is a projective line over the prime field itself. Each of the three outer fibers then gives three or five distinct prime-field values with complete simple inner fibers on $H$.

In all three four-head markings the quotient $F/G$ has degree one or two. The index $[K(X):K(F,G)]$ divides both $D$ and $\deg(F/G)$, so it is one. In particular no nonconstant right factor of degree greater than one can occur for both pencils. [Quotient formulas for all three markings](four-fiber-quadratic-characters.md#the-other-two-overlap-patterns)

## Excluding other primitive monodromy groups

Let $T$ now denote an indecomposable right factor, allowing the whole pencil. Its degree $m$ is one of the three lower degrees in the opening paragraph or the upper degree $h+1$. Each is squarefree and composite. The classification of primitive groups of squarefree degree makes its geometric monodromy $G$ almost simple with transitive simple socle $L$, so $m\mid |L|$. Since $m<p$, the Galois closure has degree prime to $p$. The prime-to-$p$ fundamental group of the punctured projective line gives generating inertia cycles with product one. Tame Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus zero in the sheet action. [Li and Seress](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-london-mathematical-society/article/abs/primitive-permutation-groups-of-squarefree-degree/C55B0D6BB03F83258632995076BBB95C) · [SGA 1, Exposé XIII, Corollaire 2.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206203)

The sporadic and exceptional Lie-type genus-zero bounds are below 281, and the rank-one bound for $\mathrm{PSL}_2(q)$ gives $q\le64$. In rank one $q(q^2-1)$ then has neither 127 nor 15,790,321 as a prime factor. Both exclusions apply since every lower degree has prime factor 127, and the upper degree has prime factor 15,790,321. For the remaining classical groups, the genus-zero fixed-point-ratio argument gives $q\le113$. In rank at least three Li–Seress gives a parabolic stabilizer on the socle. Its degree is at least $q^{b-2}$ in natural dimension $b$, by the opposite cell dimension. The rank-at-most-two exceptions have $b\le6$. [Genus-zero bounds and fixed-point-ratio argument](quarter-split-pencil-pullbacks.md#a-primitive-factor-must-have-alternating-or-symmetric-monodromy) · [Liebeck and Saxl](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-63.2.266)

The classical order formulas give the following necessary test for every prime $\ell\mid m$. Either $\ell\mid q$ or $q^i\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell$ for some $1\le i\le b$. In the lower degree use $\ell=29,43,113,127$. Testing all prime powers $q\le113$ and the dimension bound above leaves the ranges in the table. The last column lists the parabolic actions of a possible stage degree.

| Field size | Natural dimensions | Surviving actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 14 through 29 | Linear or symplectic points in dimension 28, odd orthogonal points in dimension 29. The degree is $D$. |
| 4 | 7 through 15 | Linear or symplectic points in dimension 14, odd orthogonal points in dimension 15. The degree is $D/3$. |
| 16 | 7 or 8 | None. |

For reproducibility, the exact sieve uses the Gaussian coefficient $\binom bi_q$ for a linear $i$-space. Its unitary index is $\prod_{j=b-2i+1}^b(q^j-(-1)^j)/\prod_{j=1}^i(q^{2j}-1)$. In symplectic dimension $2r$ and odd orthogonal dimension $2r+1$ the index is $\binom ri_q\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(q^{r-j}+1)$. In split orthogonal dimension $2r$ replace the product by $\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(q^{r-j-1}+1)$ and divide by two when $i=r$ to select a family. In nonsplit orthogonal dimension $2r$ use $\binom{r-1}i_q\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(q^{r-j}+1)$ for $i\le r-1$. Every maximal parabolic containing a flag stabilizer has index dividing $m$, hence dividing $D$. Only points and linear dual points pass this divisibility test. The linear incident line–hyperplane flag has index $\binom b1_q\binom{b-1}1_q$, which never divides $D$ among the survivors. This proves the last column. Dual linear actions are allowed there. Any automorphism extending one of the actions preserves its point-stabilizer conjugacy class. All these point actions exceed the genus-zero degree bound of 10,000. [Exact integer sieve](../../../scripts/probes/probe_half_successor_pencil_monodromy.py) · [Frohardt, Guralnick and Magaard, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1303)

For the upper odd degree the primes 17 and 15,790,321 leave $q=2$ in dimensions 28 through 30, $q=4$ in dimensions 14 through 16, and $q=16$ in dimensions 7 through 9. None of the maximal parabolic indices in these nine cases divides $h+1$. [Upper-degree sieve](../../../scripts/probes/probe_half_successor_pencil_monodromy.py)

It remains to identify the action of an alternating socle $A_b$. Here $b\ge127$, so $G$ is $A_b$ or $S_b$ abstractly. A sheet stabilizer $J$ has index at most $2m$ in $S_b$. If $J$ were primitive on the natural $b$ letters, Bochert's index bound would exceed $2m$, since $J$ cannot contain $A_b$. If $J$ preserved a transitive block system of $v\ge2$ blocks of size $r\ge2$, pair $\lfloor v/2\rfloor$ disjoint pairs of blocks. In each pair match the letters across its two blocks. Swapping any subset of the $r$ matched pairs gives $2^{r-1}$ distinct unordered partitions of that pair of blocks. Thus the partition orbit has size at least $2^{(r-1)\lfloor v/2\rfloor}\ge 2^{(b-2)/4}>2m$. The exponent inequality follows from $(v-2)(r-2)\ge0$. Hence $J$ preserves a proper subset of $t\le b/2$ letters. Maximality of $J$ in $G$ makes it the full subset stabilizer. [Bochert's bound and subgroup indices, Dixon and Mortimer, Chapter 5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0731-3)

In the upper case $b\ge15{,}790{,}321$ implies $\binom b2>m$. In the lower cases $t\ge2$ would force $b<24{,}000$ and $t\le5$, by $\binom{24000}2>D$ and $\binom{127}6>D$. An orbit of $J$ on the subsets differing by one letter would then have size at most $t(b-t)<120{,}001$, below the proved subdegree floor. Equivalently the exact sieve finds no such binomial index. Thus $t=1$ and the action is the natural $A_m$ or $S_m$ action. [Subdegree and neighbor count](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/HalfSuccessorPencilMonodromy.lean) · [Independent binomial calculation](../../../scripts/probes/probe_half_successor_pencil_monodromy.py)

For a composed lower pencil the whole geometric group acts on three or five blocks of size $m$. A block stabilizer induces the primitive stage group there. Its subgroup fixing all blocks induces a normal subgroup of index at most $5!$, which contains $A_m$. Thus the full pencil has $A_m$ as a composition factor in all cases. These arguments give necessary group and factorization conditions. They provide neither a marked pair nor a nonexistence proof for the named first successor.
