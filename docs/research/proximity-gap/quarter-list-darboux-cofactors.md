# Quarter-rate list safety from logarithmic differential forms

On the first named prime, length $n=2^{30}$ and message degree at most $n/4-1$, every ball through **539,340,518 errors** has at most $n$ Reed–Solomon words. This gives a first-failure lower bound of **539,340,519**. The argument works at every positive interleaving width, on any injective grid of this length, in characteristic zero or prime characteristic $p>2^{157}$. The second named prime already has a stronger budget-specific safety bound. The exact first-failure counts remain open. ## Reduction to a rational differential equation

Work over an algebraically closed scalarization field $k$. Write $R=1/4$, $s=4977/10000$, $d=n/4-1$ and use the normalized coefficient weights $1/n,R,R$. Take the primary of order eight and derivative degree two, of generic degree at most fifteen. Put $L=250000$ and $M=n-250100$. The quotient thresholds $Z_v(D;r,h)$, interpolation rank $C(r,h)$, source dimension $S(w,h)$ and degree comparisons are those of the previous quarter-rate proof. All its exterior linear-factor comparisons still hold with these parameters. We explain the stronger derivative-quadratic comparison. [Normalized interpolation and quotient thresholds](quarter-list-four-term-uniform.md#length-independent-interpolation) · [Exterior factor tests](quarter-list-rational-midpoint.md)

For an irreducible quadratic or the squarefree product of two linear factors let $a$ be the ordinate degree of its coefficient of $V^2$, $c=a+2$, $q=4D$. Crop the hits to mean $s$ as in the previous proof. Write $M_0=L-15^2$, $\sigma^2=R-s^2+(s-R)/M_0$. At order zero a nonzero quadratic in the slope vanishes on the hitting slopes. At order one $g(x,y,V)$ vanishes identically, and the first coefficient of $g(x+U,y+Uz,z+Uw)$ is the nonzero polynomial $g_X(x,y,z)+zg_Y(x,y,z)$ of degree at most three. Four times the loss in weighted Cauchy–Schwarz therefore bounds the fraction of sites of order at most one by $\alpha=4[1-s^2/(R+(2s-R)/M_0)]\le1/16$. The same-slope pairs used here contribute one extra root multiplicity. [Slope counting](quarter-first-rational-observable.md#slopes-and-orders-below-the-exceptional-weight-interval)

The leading coefficient from the previous proof gives $\mathbb E(o-c)_+\le(q-c)/4$, with $0\le o\le q+2$. On this interval one has
$$
 (o-2)^2\le(c-2)(o-2)+q(o-c)_++2c\mathbf1_{o\le1}.
$$
Consequently $4\operatorname{Var}(o)\le(c-2)^2+q(q-c)+8c\alpha\le(q-1)^2$ by convexity in $2\le c\le q$ and the two endpoint tests. The usual regular-jet estimate becomes $\mathbb E o\le1+(D-R)/s+(q-1)\sigma/(2s)$.

A sharper estimate covers the remaining 22 weight intervals. If the leading ordinate coefficient of $V^2$ has order $j$ at $x$, its first translated monomial is a nonzero multiple of $U^j(Y-y)^a V^2$. In the radial substitution, the block of monomials with fixed $i+b=j+a$ and $b+k=a+2$ has possible derivative indices $\max(0,2-j)\le k\le2$. Its first $1+\min(j,2)$ rows are the matrix $\binom{k}{l}$ for $0\le l\le\min(j,2)$ and have full column rank. Different blocks have distinct triples of exponents after fixing the power of $w$. Hence $o\le a+j+\min(j,2)$. For $\psi_a(o)=\min\{j:o\le a+j+\min(j,2)\}$, we obtain $\mathbb E\psi_a(o)\le D-(a+2)R$. The checker majorizes either $o$ or $(o-u)^2$ pointwise by $c_0+c_1\psi_a(o)+c_2\mathbf1_{o\le1}$ with $c_1,c_2\ge0$. In the second case one uses the variance estimate and the affine upper tangent $(h+v^2)/(2v)$ for $\sqrt h$. The quotient threshold is concave on each interval, so strict comparisons with these affine bounds at both endpoints suffice. Each interval to the left of a multiple of $R$ uses its left-endpoint degree caps. The next interval handles the actual integer degree at the shared point.

The quadratic comparison excludes a quadratic group of size $L$ and excludes two distinct linear groups each of size $L$. If a list exceeds $n$, one linear factor therefore has at least $M$ sections after allowing for the remaining small group and at most fifteen derivative-independent words. Retain exactly $M$ distinct polynomials $f_i$. Outside $5R\le D\le5R+40(2R-s)$ the exterior linear comparisons exclude the factor. Within this interval write it as $A(X,Y)+B(X,Y)V$, with $A,B$ coprime, $\deg_Y A\le5$, $\deg_Y B\le4$, and $B(X,f_i)\ne0$. Its affine plane intersection length is at most $nK$, $K=4D+5(D-R)-20R$. For each tested cap $h$, order $50000$ gives the lower bound $Z=Z_1(D;50000,h)$ on mean radial order.

## Exceptional collision classes

At a physical site use $U=X-x$, $V_0=Y-y_x$. For order $o\ge2$ the multiplicities of $A,B$ there are at least $o-1$, and their leading forms of degree $o-1$ are $-V_0 L,U L$ for a common homogeneous $L$ of degree $o-2$. This follows by expanding the radial substitution. Suppose $L\ne0$. Blow up by $V_0=Uz$, divide $A,B$ by $U^{o-1}$ and denote the resulting polynomials by $A_1,B_1$. Then $A_1+zB_1=U D_1$ and a hitting graph satisfies $D_1+B_1z'=0$. At a slope $v$ with $L(1,v)\ne0$ this is a regular equation. Two distinct graphs through that slope would have positive contact after blowup of order less than $p$, which is incompatible with differentiating their difference in a regular equation.

Each realized slope $v$ with $L(1,v)=0$ is an exceptional class of words. It forces $D_1=B_1=0$ there. The intersection formula for a blowup first counts $(o-1)^2$ at the original point. At a finite tangent slope, intersection of the strict transforms counts the order of $L(1,z)$ at $v$ and the extra intersection of $D_1,B_1$, because $A_1+zB_1=U D_1$. The same lower reserve holds at the tangent direction at infinity by interchanging the two local coordinates and using $UA_{o-1}+V_0B_{o-1}=0$. Thus the fiber consumes at least $b(o)=(o^2-o-1)_+$ at the physical ordinate, plus one for each exceptional slope. If $L=0$, both multiplicities are at least $o$. All possible slopes satisfy the nonzero leading radial coefficient, a polynomial of degree at most $o+1$. Its nonvanishing follows from the definition of $o$ since $B$ has ordinary multiplicity at least $o$ and its derivative-variable term starts one order later. All such slopes may be exceptional, paid by $o^2-b(o)=o+1$.

At order one there are at most two possible slopes by the same first-coefficient argument for a linear equation. At order zero two different words cannot hit the same ordinate, since a collision either solves a regular equation or forces both $A,B$ to vanish. At any other finite point where two polynomial graphs meet, regular uniqueness forces a common zero of $A,B$. Count one more exceptional class consisting of the sections through that point. These additional points include nonreceived ordinates over physical abscissas. Keep only classes containing at least two words. For each of $h_0(o)=4o-7,6o-13$ the number of classes plus the reserved $h_0(o)$ at all grid sites is bounded by the total affine intersection length. Indeed $b(o)\ge h_0(o)$, and the slack at order one covers either of its two slopes. The checker chooses a tangent and an interpolation cap on each weight interval to give
$$
       \#\{C\}/n\le \min(K+7-4Z,K+13-6Z)\le J=1/10
$$
with the corresponding $Z$ for that test.

Let $p_x$ be the fraction of all $M$ selected graphs agreeing at $x$, $q_C$ the fraction in class $C$, and $\mu=n^{-1}\sum p_x\ge s$. Each ordered pair of distinct graphs pays one root of its difference per common true hit, one further multiplicity per shared exceptional slope, and a root per shared nonreceived ordinate. Their total is at most $d$. Restoring diagonals gives
$$
 \mu^2\le R+1/M,\qquad n^{-1}\sum_C q_C^2\le R-s^2+(1+J)/M.
$$

## Rank of the polynomial cofactors

For each section form the polynomial $H_i=(A+B f_i')/(Y-f_i)$. Its leading coefficient at $Y^4$ is the coefficient of $Y^5$ in $A$, independent of $i$. Fix four distinct anchors $f_j$ in the group. The differences of the $H_i$ have ordinate degree at most three, so evaluation at these four anchors determines the differences. Discard the four anchor columns when bounding the image dimension and restore them at cost at most four. On every other column the value is
$$
      H_i(X,f_j)/B(X,f_j)=-(f_i-f_j)'/(f_i-f_j).
$$
Thus each evaluated column is determined by the residues at the finite roots of the differences. At a physical site where an anchor hits, all its nonexceptional collisions have residue $-1$ and supply the same true-hit indicator row, independently of the anchor. An additional row can occur only when the anchor belongs to an exceptional slope class there. Outside those coordinates one further row for each incident exceptional point class suffices. This bounds the cofactor rank by five plus the size of the union of the four anchors' physical hits plus their number of incidences with exceptional classes.

For four uniformly chosen distinct anchors the expected union fraction is at most $1-(1-\mu)^4+6/M$. Indeed sampling with replacement gives the Jensen bound, and the collision probability in four draws is at most $6/M$. Expected class incidences are $4\sum_C q_C\le4n\sqrt{J(R-s^2+(1+J)/M)}$. Some four distinct anchors attain this bound on the sum. The certificate bounds the cofactor rank by **1,071,694,441**, giving nullity $k_0\ge1797283$.

## From cofactor relations to completely split fibers

Each linear relation gives a closed rational form $\omega=\sum c_i\,d(Y-f_i)/(Y-f_i)$ annihilating $B\partial_X-A\partial_Y$. The residue along each graph recovers $c_i$, so these forms span a $k_0$-dimensional space. Every two are proportional over $k(X,Y)$. Choose two independent ones and take their ratio $R_0$. It has bidegrees at most $(dM,M)$ from the coefficients of $dY$ with the common denominator $\prod_i(Y-f_i)$. Closedness gives $dR_0\wedge\omega=0$. In particular $R_0$ is a first integral, and it depends on $Y$ since a rational function of $X$ alone of degree below $p$ with zero derivative is constant.

The relative algebraic closure of $k(R_0)$ in $k(X,Y)$ is rational. Its smooth projective curve is dominated by a generic constant-$X$ line, so this follows from Lüroth's theorem. Write it as $k(T)$, $R_0=H(T)$; the bidegrees of $T$ are at most $(dM,M)$ by composing with $H$ in each variable. The nonconstant map $H$ is separable, and $T_Y\ne0$ since its positive ordinate degree is below $p$. Every relation form is $G\,dT$ where $G$ is its $dY$ coefficient divided by $T_Y$, of bidegrees at most $(3dM,3M)$. Closedness gives $dG\wedge dT=0$. If $G,T$ were algebraically independent, their generically finite map would have degree at most $6dM^2<p$ by intersection on $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$, hence would be separable. Thus $G\in k(T)$ by the relative algebraic closure. This justifies the descent of the differentials also in positive characteristic.

View $T$ as a map in $Y$ of degree $m$ over $k(X)$. At every pole of any descended differential on the target, each horizontal point in its fiber is a pole of the pullback. The map is tame since $m<p$. All these poles on the source are simple and supported on $Y=f_i$ and $Y=\infty$, so the descended poles are simple too. The dimension of differentials on the projective line with at most simple poles at $l$ points is $l-1$. We therefore have at least $l\ge k_0+1$ distinct constant targets whose entire fibers split among the marked graphs and infinity. Riemann–Hurwitz for the degree-$m$ map in $Y$ gives $lm-(2m-2)\le M+1$, hence $m\le(M-1)/(k_0-1)$.

## Moving branch values

A rational map of this bounded degree with a moving branch value has few completely split constant fibers. Write $b\le2m-2$ for its number of distinct branch values and $u$ for the number of nonconstant ones. Every branch value has degree in $X$ on its minimal field at most $(2m-2)dM$, from the binary discriminant of a fiber of $T$. This is below $p$, so a nonconstant branch value has nonzero derivative. At a critical point $\alpha$ of local degree $e$, choose local ordinate coordinates and write $T=\beta+c(Y-\alpha)^e+\cdots$. Moving $\beta$ produces a pole of order $e-1$ in the ordinate differential equation $Y'=-T_X/T_Y$. For a stationary $\beta$ the quotient is regular there. The expansions apply in reciprocal coordinates at infinity as well. Thus a moving value needs a distinct pole of the given vector field, and $u\le\deg_Y B+1\le5$.

If $u=0$, the ordinate vector field is regular on the projective line and the equation is Riccati. Cross-ratios of its distinct polynomial solutions are constant: their derivatives vanish, and their degrees are at most $2d<p$. Normalize by three solutions. At any abscissa the resulting map from constant labels to section values is either a projective automorphism on reduction, or has constant reduction away from at most one label. This follows by specializing a primitive matrix over the local valuation ring. Therefore each received ordinate has at most one hit or at least $M-1$ hits. Counting ordered pairs bounds total hits by $n+M(M-1)d/(M-2)$, strictly below $Msn$.

Suppose $u\ge1$. Let $L_b$ split the branch values over $k(X)$, with degree $e_b$, and let $H_b$ be the largest degree of a branch-value function on the smooth projective curve of $L_b$. Choose three completely split targets disjoint from the branch labels, and mark one rational preimage of each. They exist since $k_0+1>b+3$. The squarefree binary form of the branch labels and three targets has coefficient height in $X$ at most $uH_b/e_b$. Indeed over $L_b$ the product of their homogeneous linear factors has projective height at most the sum of their heights, and height scales by $e_b$. Its discriminant is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $2(b+2)$ in those coefficients. Including the base place at infinity, at most $2(b+2)uH_b/e_b$ base places have colliding labels. The splitting field is unramified at every other place by separability of the reduced binary form, and is tame everywhere since its group embeds in the permutation group of at most $b<p$ labels. For the set $S_b$ of all places of $L_b$ above collisions, Riemann–Hurwitz and the number of places in a fiber give
$$
       2g(L_b)-2\le2(b+2)uH_b,\qquad |S_b|\le2(b+2)uH_b .
$$

At any remaining place the tame cover has potentially good reduction with the same ramification indices above disjoint branch labels. This is the prime-to-characteristic extension theorem for tame covers of a smooth pointed curve, applied to the Galois closure with group a subgroup of $S_m$ and then quotienting by a sheet stabilizer. Equivalently the tame Hurwitz stack is proper and étale over the configuration of distinct branch labels. On a good model after extension, the three marked preimages have distinct reductions because their target reductions differ. Sending them to $0,1,\infty$ identifies the source with the standard projective line over the extended valuation ring. This coordinate is defined already over the original function field. The resultant of the normalized map is consequently a unit already over the original valuation ring, and its reduced geometric fiber has the same ramification indices. To lift a ramified point above a branch label, use a finite local ordinate chart and the original strict henselian valuation ring. Hensel-factor the preimage polynomial around the chosen reduced point, isolating a monic factor of degree $e$ from the other reduced points. After the potentially good extension this factor is $(Y-\alpha)^e$, since the marked branch fiber retains its distinct support and its indices. Its coefficient of degree $e-1$ is $-e\alpha$. Since $e<p$, this puts $\alpha$ in the original strict henselian ring. This ring has ramification index one over the original place. [Romagny–Wewers, Theorem 4.11 and Remark 4.15(ii)](https://imag.umontpellier.fr/~romagny/articles/hurwitz_spaces.pdf)

Fix a moving label $\beta$ of degree $H_b$. At a good place above $\beta=c$, for a completely split constant target $c$ different from the marked and stationary branch labels, the normalized map has a ramified section $\alpha$ above $\beta$ of local degree at least two. In local integral coordinates the map minus $\beta$ is a unit times $(Y-\alpha)^e$ near the ramification point. Some one of the rational preimages of $c$ reduces to this point by splitting of the whole fiber and good reduction. It follows that $\beta-c$ has valuation at least two. This uses the good model over the original place, so an additional ramified extension cannot change the order in this deduction.

For each such $c$, every unramified preimage under $\beta$ therefore belongs to $S_b$. Applying Riemann–Hurwitz to the separable function $\beta$, each fiber degree is at most twice its ramification contribution plus its number of unramified places. Summing over all these $c$ gives
$$
   (l-b-3)H_b\le2(2g(L_b)-2+2H_b)+|S_b|,\qquad
   l\le b+7+6(b+2)u .
$$
The first integral has degree at most **597**, giving at most **37,019** completely split fibers with a moving branch value. This is below $k_0+1$. This excludes the rational equation throughout the exceptional weight interval and finishes the claimed all-center bound.
