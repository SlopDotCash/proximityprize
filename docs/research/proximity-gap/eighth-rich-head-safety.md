# Eighth-rate safety through the predecessor of five eighths

At length $n=2^{30}$, dimension $k=n/8$ and tolerance $2^{-128}$, every injective domain over the second named prime is safe through **671,088,639 errors**, at every positive width. On its named subgroup the first failing error count lies in **[671,088,640, 679,477,248]**. The critical four-head configuration at 671,088,639 has one projective involution pairing all opposite directions, which limits its supply of distinct fresh scalars. The upper endpoint has a complete Lean witness with 2,148,807,811 bad scalars. The lower endpoint uses the written tangent-head population estimate on a normalized surface. ## Selecting the rich heads

Take $e=671088639$, $t=n-e=402653185$, and $d=k-1=134217727$. Given a received pair, select a bad witness polynomial for each bad scalar. The two mixed Hasse interpolants of shapes $(6,16,335)$ and $(9,26,128)$ have surpluses 77,656 and 805,373,634. Assign each scalar to one primary factor containing its section, allowing up to 335 scalars from scalar-only factors. A factor with more than $L=81,920$ assigned scalars divides the companion since $L>335\cdot26+16\cdot128$. For a linear factor the identity-site test $t-\lfloor128n/L\rfloor-3\lfloor128n/(L-128)\rfloor>d$ gives an affine polynomial head. Let $j$ be the number of these rich affine heads. Each scalar is assigned to a single factor. [Construction and identity-site test](hodge-section-variance.md#production-specialization)

For a head with core size $w$, put $D=\max(1,t-w)$ and $P(D)=1+\lfloor e/D\rfloor$. Away from its core, each coordinate can give it a fresh agreement for at most one scalar. A bad witness needs at least $D$ such sites. Hence its assigned population is at most $P(D)$. Richness implies $w\ge C=t-8191=402644994$ since $P(8192)=81920$. For $j$ heads on one line with union size $U$, the aggregate is at most $jn-(j-1)U$. A covered site gives only one fresh direction and each other site gives at most $j$. The pairwise core overlaps are at most $d$. Counting incidences and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives $U\ge\lceil jC^2/(C+(j-1)d)\rceil$. [Core and union bounds](weighted-hodge-windows.md#joint-allocation-among-linear-and-nonlinear-factors)

Every noncollinear quartet has total core size at most $n+4d$. Five heads with no four on a line have three times their total core size at most $3n+20d<15C$. Thus any five rich heads contain four on a line. Once four lie on a line, there can be at most one head outside it. Otherwise three on the line and two outside would form five with no four collinear. Five on a line and one outside would give $13C\le3n+13d$, which the stated $C$ excludes. Every set of six or more rich heads is therefore collinear. [Four-head multiplicities](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md#core-multiplicity) · [Five-head incidence theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FiveHeadTrianglePacking.lean) · [Transverse intersection and cluster bounds](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthCollinearOutlier.lean)

## Four rich heads off a single line

Order their deficits as $D_1\le\cdots\le D_4$. The core bound implies $\sum D_i\ge4t-n-4d=8$. If $D_3\ge2$, the total is at most $2P(1)+2P(2)$. Otherwise the first three deficits equal one. For $D_4\ge6$ the bound is $3P(1)+P(6)=2,125,114,027$. The only remaining profile is $(1,1,1,5)$. Its four core sizes must all equal $t-D_i$ since their sum is already $n+4d$.

If this quartet contains a collinear triple, at least two of that triple have deficit one. In the third head's core at least $C-2d$ sites lie outside both of those cores. At such a site the received pair is the third head's value. The two other heads have at most one common fresh direction there, including when their evaluated values coincide or the direction yields no finite scalar. Select one fresh site per assigned label of each of the two heads. Each selected site is used at most once by each head and, on the designated sites, at most once in total. Hence their combined population is at most $2P(1)-(C-2d)$. Substituting this for two copies of $P(1)$ in the exceptional profile gives a smaller bound than 2,125,114,027. A surviving quartet has no collinear triple. [Finite-set form of the lost-site count](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthQuotientCoreCap.lean)

For this quartet every site belongs to one or two cores. Indeed the four triple determinants have total degree at most $8d$, while core multiplicities force at least $2(I-U)$ zeros with multiplicity, where $I$ is the sum of core sizes. At $I=n+4d$ equality requires $U=n$. A site in three or four cores would contribute strictly more than twice its excess. There are consequently $4d=536870908$ double sites and $n-4d=536870916$ single sites. [Determinant multiplicities](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md#core-multiplicity)

At saturation the four triangle determinants each have exactly $2d$ roots, counted with multiplicity. Each counts the double sites indexed by the three pairs in that triangle. Comparing the four triangle sums shows that opposite pairs have equal counts and that every head owns $2d$ double sites. Write $k=d+1$. Each of the first three heads therefore has $3k-2d=k+2$ singleton sites. Their total number of off-core positions, counted separately by head, is $3(n-3k)=15k$.

The three opposite pairs of side directions are orthogonal for one constant nonsingular binary form. This holds at the double sites too, since the common nodal factor is canceled as a polynomial before specializing. Thus a fixed fourth-head direction determines a unique projective opposite direction. Let $A$ be the number of distinct labels assigned to the first three heads and $M$ the number assigned to the fourth. Choose one fresh position for each of the $A$ labels, and five distinct fresh positions for each fourth-head label. A given fourth-head fresh position serves just one label. Of the $5M$ positions at most $3k+6$ are first-three singletons. Each remaining position is a double with two first-three owners. At that position associate the unique off-core slot of the remaining first-three head. Its displacement and the fourth-head displacement are opposite sides, and both are nonzero since exactly two heads match. For each fixed fourth-head label the associated slots can supply at most one selected first-three label, also when the opposite projective direction is infinity. Hence
$$
 A\le15k,\qquad 15k-A\ge 5M-(3k+6)-M.
$$
Adding three copies of the first inequality to $A+4M\le18k+6$ gives $A+M\le 15k+\lfloor(3k+6)/4\rfloor=2{,}113{,}929{,}217$. This fits the ordinary four-head allowance of 2,125,114,027. [Constant form and projective direction theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FourHeadCoreDeterminant.lean) · [Deficit and core arithmetic](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthRichDeficits.lean)

## Four on a line and a fifth off it

Write $z$ for the transverse head's deficit, and order the four deficits on the line as $D_1\le\cdots\le D_4$. Their union has size at least $U_4=4C-6d$ by the pairwise intersection bound. When $z\ge3$ the aggregate is at most $4n-3U_4+P(3)$. For a constant primitive direction the four collinear heads have at most one finite fresh scalar across their entire covered union. They and the transverse head therefore have at most $1+4(n-U_4)+P(1)$ labels.

Suppose the primitive direction has positive degree $r$. Any two line heads have joint overlap at most $d-r$. The outside head meets the union of any three line cores in at most $d+r$ sites by the transverse determinant. Thus these four core sizes sum to at most $n+3(d-r)+d+r=n+4d-2r$. In particular $z+D_1+D_2+D_3\ge10$.

For $z\le2$ and $D_2\ge2$ the last inequality gives $D_3,D_4\ge3$. The five populations sum to at most $2P(1)+P(2)+2P(3)=2,125,114,028$. For $D_1=D_2=1$ the two heads lose at least $C-2d$ candidate sites by the preceding shared-direction count on the third line head's core. If $z=1$ then $D_3,D_4\ge7$, and if $z=2$ then $D_3,D_4\ge6$. In either case the resulting count, $2P(1)-(C-2d)+P(z)+2P(8-z)$, is below 2,125,114,028. The bound applies to all five-head configurations off one line.

The exceptional saturated quartet has the sharper bound **2,113,929,217** on its own assigned labels. Lean checks the triangle budgets for physical core masks, the saturated site counts, and the loss from repeated opposite directions. ## All remaining factors

For each total assigned value degree $l\le16$ and each $j\le l$, price the remaining positive degrees by the tangent-head theorem with weight $W=6t-1-(l-b)d$ and scalar/value degree $m=128-j$ for a part $b$. A part costing at most the cutoff or absent from the companion is allowed $L$ labels. Include the 335 scalar-only labels. With the four-head bound above, all 3,370 integer allocations fit the budget. The largest total is **2,145,721,437**, at four rich heads and one factor of degree twelve. Five rich heads give at most **2,141,058,972** after all remaining factors are included. ## At exactly five eighths

At **671,088,640 errors**, an overrun on the second named field requires four rich heads with no three collinear over the rational function field. Their sorted deficits must be $(1,1,1,q)$ with $1\le q\le5$. Set $s=n+4d-\sum_i |C_i|$ for their full joint cores. Then $0\le s\le q-1$. All three opposite side directions satisfy one symmetric relation with polynomial coefficients of degree at most $s$. Its discriminant can vanish at at most $2s\le8$ evaluation sites. Whether any such quartet overruns the budget remains open. [Four-head determinant theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FourHeadCoreDeterminant.lean) · [Arithmetic of the exceptional profiles](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthRichDeficits.lean)

Here $t=3k=402653184$, $d=k-1$, $n=8k$, and $C=t-8191=402644993$. The same interpolant shapes have surpluses 44,200 and 805,345,446. The strict richness cutoff is now 81,921. The five-head inequalities still force every noncollinear set of at least five rich heads to consist of four collinear heads and one transverse head. This configuration contributes at most 2,058,021,547 labels. For four rich heads the maximum allowance for all remaining factors, including the scalar-only factors, is 20,607,410. If the third deficit is at least two, or the fourth at least six, the head prices sum to at most 2,125,114,030. Across all 3,370 allocations with the exceptional noncollinear quartet excluded, the largest total is 2,145,721,440. [Five-head bound](eighth-four-plus-one.md) · [Exact allocation certificate](../../../scripts/probes/probe_eighth_rich_head_safety.py)

In the exceptional profile each $|C_i|\ge t-D_i$, giving $s\le q-1$. Suppose three heads are collinear, with primitive direction of degree $r$. The overlap estimate gives $2r\le s$. If $r=0$, their union has at least $3C-3d=805281798$ sites, and at most one label on the line can use a fresh site in that union. The four populations together are at most $1+3(n-805281798)+P(1)=1476468720$. If $r>0$, then $q\ge3$. Two of the collinear heads have deficit one. The third has at least $C-2d=134209539$ private sites relative to that pair. As in the predecessor proof, at each such site the pair loses at least one slot. The four heads then contribute at most $3P(1)+P(3)-(C-2d)=2102752598$. Both cases fit the full budget after adding 20,607,410. [Primitive-direction overlap bound](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthCollinearOutlier.lean)

For a quartet with no collinear triple, put $m_x=|\{i:x\in C_i\}|$. Each of its three quadratic evaluation minors has degree at most $4d$. At $x$, every minor is divisible by $(X-x)^{h_x}$ for $h_x=\max(m_x-1,0)+\mathbf1_{m_x=4}$. The distinct linear factors are coprime. Their product with these exponents has degree at least $\sum_i|C_i|-n=4d-s$ and divides all three minors. Cancel it in the polynomial ring to obtain $a,b,c$ of degree at most $s$. The discriminant identity expresses the product of the four triangle determinants as the square of the common factor times $b^2-ac$. Thus $b^2-ac$ is nonzero and has degree at most $2s$. For opposite finite folded directions $z,w$ at any regular site $x$, the relation is
$$
a(x)+b(x)(z+w)+c(x)zw=0 .
$$
It holds after polynomial cancellation even at a root of the common factor. For fixed $z$, the unique projective partner is given there by $w=-(a(x)+b(x)z)/(b(x)+c(x)z)$, with a zero denominator giving infinity. For $q=1$ the form is constant and every head needs only one fresh site per label. For $s>0$ even the partner of one fixed label may vary with $x$. The predecessor's count of one opposite label per group of five fresh sites therefore does not resolve this count. [Polynomial identity and projective action](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/FourHeadCoreDeterminant.lean)

For $q=5$, a constant nonsingular form actually rules out an overrun for every $s\le4$. To see this, let $x_j$ count sites in exactly $j$ cores and let $I_3$ be the sum of the first three core sizes. Since $|C_4|=3k-5$, we have $I_3+s=9k+1$. The incidence and four triangle bounds give
$$
x_2+2x_3+3x_4+s=4d+x_0,\qquad 2x_2+5x_3+8x_4\le8d .
$$
Every head owns at least $x_2-2d$ double sites, because each double it misses is a zero of the triangle of the other three heads. Thus their total singleton count $S$ satisfies $S+3x_2\le I_3+6d$. Let $A,M$ be the populations on the first three heads and on the fourth. Among $5M$ chosen fourth-head positions, all but at most $S+x_0+x_3$ are doubles owned by two first-three heads. The constant pairing again lets the first three select at most $M$ of those reserved slots. Combining $A\le3n-I_3$ and $A+5M\le3n-I_3+S+x_0+x_3+M$ gives $A+M\le2113929236$, below the available head budget. Hence a surviving $q=5$ configuration must have a genuinely varying opposite pairing and $1\le s\le4$. [Triangle multiplicities](four-arbitrary-head-cap.md#core-multiplicity) · [Checked slot arithmetic](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthRichDeficits.lean)

Reducing the value-degree cap of the primary interpolant to three cannot force a three-head cover by the standard positive-surplus test. At multiplicity $r\ge1$ and scalar/value total-degree cap $l$, the monomial count is at most $\sum_{j=0}^3(l+1-j)_+(rt-jd)$, where $(u)_+=\max(0,u)$. The first two orders of jet conditions already cost $n((l+1)r+l(r-1))$. The former is strictly smaller for every $r,l$. At $r=1$ the four possible coefficient lengths sum to $6k+6<n$. For $l\ge1$ increasing $r$ adds at most $4(l+1)3k$ monomials while those two orders add $(2l+1)8k$ conditions. At $l=0$ the counts are $rt$ and $rn$. Lean checks the integer inequality for every multiplicity and cap. [Cubic surplus obstruction](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthRichDeficits.lean)

### Minimum value degree of a mixed-Hasse interpolant

The positive-surplus test at exactly $5n/8$ errors has the following complete list of feasible shapes with value degree $v\le16$. All use the same weight condition $i+jd<rt$ and total scalar/value degree cap $l$. The listed cap is the smallest possible for that pair $(v,r)$. Increasing $l$ keeps the surplus positive.

| Value cap $v$ | Multiplicity $r$ | Minimum total degree $l$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 14 | 5 | 511,305,640 |
| 15 | 5 | 447,392,437 |
| 15 | 6 | 715,827,893 |
| 16 | 5 | 447,392,437 |
| 16 | 6 | 335 |

In particular the existing $(6,16,335)$ primary surface already has the smallest total degree among all positive-surplus windows of value degree at most sixteen. A degree-fourteen surface exists for every received pair, but at its minimum total degree the monomial surplus is only 90 despite the scalar cap exceeding half a billion. The exact feasibility result concerns this monomial space and the standard count of jet equations. [Lean shape classification and degree-fourteen surface](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/EighthHasseDegree.lean)

For a scalar coefficient column of allowed value degree $h$, let $B_r(h)=\sum_{j=0}^h((rt-jd)_+-n(r-j)_+)$. The total available surplus is at most $\sum_{h=0}^l B_r(\min(v,h))$, since dropping any remaining jet equations only enlarges the surplus. For $r\ge23$ and $j\le16$, the single-order difference is nonpositive. For $1\le r\le22$, the 17 possible column counts have positive values only when $r=5$ and $h\ge14$, or $r=6$ and $h\ge15$. In these cases all jet orders are included. Up through $l=16$ the total score is nonpositive. Each increment after 16 adds the fixed $B_r(v)$. Evaluating the integer crossing in the five remaining cases gives the table.
