# Nine consecutive evaluations determine a locator on each named order-256 domain

Fix one named prime $p$ and the root $z=g^{(p-1)/256}$, with $g=3$ in the first field and $g=37$ in the second. Let $P,Q$ be disjoint subsets of the exponents modulo 256. If the monic quotient $L_P/L_Q$ is constant at **nine consecutive exponent sites** outside the supports, then $P=Q=\varnothing$. The same conclusion holds on **five consecutive sites together with their five antipodes**. The two support sizes may differ. These results also determine an ordinary monic locator from its evaluations up to a common scalar, since common factors of two candidate locators cancel.

At order 128 with root $z^2$ we also obtain a weighted result. For any integers $|w_j|\le2$ supported outside a five-site window, constancy of $\prod_j(T-(z^2)^j)^{w_j}$ at those five sites implies $w=0$. Translation covers every window for the displayed primitive roots. A universal nine-site conclusion for other odd step sizes would require checking those steps. Five ordinary consecutive sites at order 256 remain open. At order 128 the unweighted five-site property already follows from the universal two-center transfer and the characteristic-zero theorem. ## Reflection reduces the dimension of each check

Write $n$ for the current order and $\eta$ for its indicated root. In the quotient of the multiplicative field group by $\mu_n$, use the additive logarithm table
\[
 d(j)=\log_g(1-\eta^j)\pmod{(p-1)/n}\quad(j\ne0),\qquad d(0)=0.
\]
The tabulated value at zero is immaterial because the centers have zero weight. The identity $1-\eta^{-j}=-\eta^{-j}(1-\eta^j)$ gives $d(-j)=d(j)$. Thus constancy of the original quotient implies constancy of $\sum_j w_jd(j-x)$ across all test exponents $x$. Reflection of the weights interchanges $x$ and $-x$. On a window $-h,\ldots,h$, both $w_j+w_{-j}$ and $w_j-w_{-j}$ satisfy all the required differences from zero at $x=1,\ldots,h$. [Lean reflection argument](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicLocatorReflection.lean)

There is one free fixed point $n/2$ with weight $c$. Choose one representative from each other free inverse pair and name its two weights $u_j,v_j$. Compress the symmetric vector to $(u_j+v_j,2c)$ with a metric of weight $1/2$ on the fixed coordinate. The antisymmetric vector is $(u_j-v_j)$ with the usual squared norm. Their energies sum to twice the original energy $E=\sum_j (u_j^2+v_j^2)+c^2$. At least one compressed energy is at most $E$. If the corresponding congruence lattice contains only the origin up to that radius, the original vector is purely symmetric or antisymmetric. In that case the original vector itself satisfies the congruences of the other sector, with compressed energy $E/2$. Applying its certificate proves $w=0$. This last step uses the original comparisons directly, so it does not divide any congruences by two. [Lean energy identities](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicLocatorReflection.lean)

For nine sites at order 256, $E\le247$. The symmetric and antisymmetric lattices have dimensions 124 and 123, with four modular equations each. For weighted five sites at order 128, $E\le123\cdot4=492$. There are two modular equations each in dimensions 62 and 61. In each of these eight sector checks across the two fields, the exact search proves that the only vector in the closed ball is zero.

## Adding all five antipodes

Suppose a ternary order-256 quotient is constant at $z^x$ and $-z^x$ for $x=-2,-1,0,1,2$. Multiplying the two evaluations and using $(T-a)(-T-a)=-(T^2-a^2)$ gives an order-128 quotient with weights $u_j=w_j+w_{j+128}$. Its values at the five squared centers coincide. Since $|u_j|\le2$, the weighted result gives $u=0$.

The order-256 quotient is now a product of $((T-z^j)/(T+z^j))^{w_j}$ over 128 representatives. Negating $T$ inverts each factor, so the common value at opposite centers must be $1$ or $-1$. Both map to zero in the logarithm quotient. The five center evaluations give five congruences on the remaining 123 ternary source weights. At each named prime their lattice has index $((p-1)/256)^5$ and only the origin has squared energy at most 123. This removes the antisymmetric remainder and proves the original supports empty. ## Reproduction

Run `python3 -O scripts/probes/probe_mu256_locator_windows.py` with `python-flint`, `fpylll` and a C++17 compiler. The `--case five-pairs` calculation requires only `python-flint` and the compiler. Each run verifies primality, the designated generator and primitive root, and the expanded unit logarithms by modular powering. An invertible observation minor certifies surjectivity onto every displayed congruence coordinate. Each computed basis satisfies the congruences and its absolute determinant equals their joint index. The reduction transform is verified to be unimodular. Integer enumeration uses an outward rounding of the exact rational Gram–Schmidt form, so absence of a nonzero candidate certifies the original closed ball. The lattice searches are executable finite certificates. The reflection, energy and folding identities are kernel-checked theorems.
