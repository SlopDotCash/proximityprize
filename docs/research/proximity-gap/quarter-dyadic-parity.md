# No saturated quartet on dyadic roots in characteristic zero

Let $n=2^k\ge64$ and $d=n/4-1$. On the $n$th roots of unity in a characteristic-zero field, four planar polynomial heads of coordinate degree at most $d$ cannot have the saturated pattern consisting of four singleton sites and $4d$ distinct double sites, with every triangle determinant nonzero and all its $2d$ zeros prescribed by the pair strata. In particular, the four-head mechanism that would exceed $2n$ labels at $n/2$ errors and rate one quarter is impossible in characteristic zero. This excludes arbitrary heads and arbitrary placements of the four holes. It requires no hypothesis on the number of fresh directions. At the production length in the two specified prime fields, the characteristic-zero parity implication remains a separate problem. [Derivation of the required pattern](quarter-saturated-symmetry.md)

## Independence of the dyadic cyclotomic factors

Here is an algebraic proof of the independence used by the parity argument. Fix a primitive $n$th root $\zeta$. In the multiplicative group of the maximal abelian extension $K=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}$, modulo squares, let $u_r$ be the class of $1-\zeta^r$ for odd $r$ modulo sign. This is well defined since $(1-\zeta^{-r})/(1-\zeta^r)=-\zeta^{-r}$ is a root of unity and every root of unity is a square in $K$. The odd residues modulo sign form a cyclic group of order $m=n/4$, generated by 5. Indeed $v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2$ by successive squaring, and every power of 5 is $1$ modulo 4.

The kernel of the binary linear map that takes the $r$th basis vector to $u_r$ is stable under cyclic shift. The automorphism $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^5$ extends to the algebraic closure and preserves $K$. Identify the source with $\mathbb F_2[Z]/(Z^m-1)$. Every nonzero shift-invariant subspace is an ideal containing $(Z-1)^{m-1}=1+Z+\cdots+Z^{m-1}$ because $Z^m-1=(Z-1)^m$. But the corresponding product $P=\prod_{r\bmod\pm}(1-\zeta^r)$ has $P^2=2\xi$ for a root of unity $\xi$, by pairing the factors of $\Phi_n(1)=2$. Its class is that of $\sqrt2$, which is nonsquare in $K$. A square root would put $\sqrt[4]2$ in a finite abelian extension of $\mathbb Q$, although $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[4]2)$ is not normal: $X^4-2$ is Eisenstein and its nonreal roots are absent from that real field. Equivalently, an ordered normal extension containing a real fourth root would have an automorphism sending its square to the negative of that square, by the irreducible polynomial $X^2-2$. The image of the fourth root would then have negative square. Lean checks this argument and excludes the real fourth root from every abelian subfield of the complex numbers. [Abelian fourth-root obstruction](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/AbelianDyadicRadical.lean)

This proves independence of the square classes. Lean also checks the binary orbit criterion directly: if a relation differs from its half-period translate, adding that translate produces a nonzero relation of half the period. Iteration reaches the all-ones vector. [Binary orbit criterion](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicOrbitIndependence.lean)

If an integer product of these factors is a root of unity, divide all exponents by their largest common power of two. The reduced product is still a root of unity. Any nonzero relation would now give a nonzero binary square-class relation, which was just excluded. This proves multiplicative independence modulo roots of unity using only dyadic Galois theory.

### Independence within a real abelian field

One can also work in a single real cyclotomic field and obtain the independence of chord **lengths** directly. Take $n=2^{k+2}$, $m=n/4$, $t=\exp(\pi i/n)$, and $E=\mathbb Q(t)\cap\mathbb R$. This is an ordered abelian Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$. Set $s_r=i(t^{-r}-t^r)$; here $i=t^{n/2}$, $s_r$ belongs to $E$, and $s_r^2=(1-\zeta^r)(1-\zeta^{-r})$ for $\zeta=t^2$. Replacing $r$ by its negative or adding $n$ changes at most the sign of $s_r$.

Index the $m$ units by $r=5^j$ modulo sign and $n$. Their squared product is two, by grouping all primitive roots in inverse pairs in $\Phi_n(1)$. For any translation of $j$, the automorphism of $\mathbb Q(t)$ that raises $t$ to the corresponding power of five preserves $E$ and carries the squared units to the translated squared units. Thus the signed real orbit satisfies the hypotheses of the following Lean theorem: in an ordered abelian extension, if the translations act up to sign and the product squared is two, a signed integer product equal to $1$ or $-1$ has every exponent zero.

The specialization to the complex primitive factors is now kernel checked for every dyadic order. For any primitive $z$ of order $2^{k+2}$, Lean proves that a product of $(1-z^{5^j})^{e_j}$ of absolute value one has $e_j=0$ for every $j$. With both signs present the two exponents at each inverse pair must sum to zero. The proof enumerates *all* primitive roots by the two signs and the powers of five, evaluates the cyclotomic polynomial at one, and restricts the translating Galois automorphisms to the real subfield containing the signed half-angle chords. [Primitive factor theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicPrimitiveChords.lean)

For the proof take the multiplicative group modulo the subgroup of $x$ satisfying $x^2=y^4$ for some $y$. All squares vanish there. The total product survives because a fourth power cannot equal two in the ordered field. The binary orbit test forces all exponents even in any signed relation. Halving the exponents preserves signed relations, since a real fourth root of one has square one. Repetition reduces the maximum absolute exponent to zero. Because $|s_r|=|1-\zeta^r|$, the same independence holds for the positive chord lengths. Lean checks the orbit theorem, including the passage from binary to integer exponents; the identification of its inputs with these cyclotomic chords is the calculation above. [Signed real orbit theorem](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/DyadicSquareOrbit.lean) · [Fourth-root obstruction](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/AbelianDyadicRadical.lean)

## Three equal ratios in one parity class

For disjoint sets $P,N$ of site exponents write $L_P,L_N$ for their monic locators. If $L_P/L_N$ agrees at three distinct test sites in the same exponent parity, both $P$ and $N$ are invariant under $i\mapsto i+n/2$ on the opposite parity. The common ratio lies in the cyclotomic field by evaluation, including when the original head coefficients lie in a bigger characteristic-zero field.

Rotate coordinates so the three centres $x,y,z$ are even. With $w=1_P-1_N$, form $v(j)=w(j)-w(j+n/2)$ on odd indices, zero elsewhere. The automorphism $\zeta\mapsto-\zeta$ fixes the centres and shifts the odd roots by $n/2$. Divide the ratio at a centre by its conjugate. Up to a root of unity the result is $\prod_{j\,\mathrm{odd}}(1-\zeta^{j-x})^{v(j)}$. Comparing at the three centres and using the independence proved above shows that $v(x+i)+v(x-i)$ has the same value at all three centres for every $i$. This holds also for even $i$, when all summands vanish.

For two centres $x,y$ define $D(j)=v(j)-v(x+y-j)$. The equal sums give $D(j)=D(j+y-x)$. Every nonzero difference generates a subgroup containing the antipode in the dyadic cyclic group. But $D$ changes sign under that antipode, so $D=0$. Thus $v$ is invariant under reflection about $(x+y)/2$ in the sense $v(j)=v(x+y-j)$. Using $x,z$ as well yields invariance under the nonzero translation $y-z$, again forcing $v=0$ by antiperiodicity. Disjointness now shows that the two indicators separately agree at antipodal sites on the opposite parity. ## Applying the six signatures

The six double strata have labels $A,A',B,B',C,C'$, with the primes denoting the opposite sides of the same family. Each individual stratum has size at most $d$ by the pair degree bound. Write the same letters for their monic locators. The four signed face coordinates are scalar multiples of $ABC,AB'C',A'BC',A'B'C$ and sum to zero. At the roots of $A$ the ratio $BC'/(B'C)$ is constant; at the roots of $A'$ the ratio $BC/(B'C')$ is constant. Cyclic permutations give the other four tests. [Face equation and orientations](quarter-opposite-edge-characters.md)

Call a colour heavy on a parity if it occurs at least three times there. On the opposite parity its row signature is invariant under the antipode: the numerator and denominator root sets are separately preserved. For centre $A$ the signatures in colour order $(A,A',B,B',C,C',\mathrm{hole})$ are $(0,0,+,-,-,+,0)$. For $A'$ they are $(0,0,+,-,+,-,0)$. The four remaining rows follow by cyclic permutation. Two rows from distinct families distinguish every one of the seven labels, since the zero locations identify the family and the other sign distinguishes its side. Two rows from one family distinguish all four other edge colours separately.

Let $E,O$ be the two parity classes, each of size $2d+2$. If $E$ has heavy colours in two families, every label on $O$ is preserved. Otherwise the four colours outside one family, say $A,A'$, occupy at most eight sites of $E$. Allowing all four holes there still leaves at least $2d-10$ sites in that family, so each side occupies at least $d-10\ge3$ sites of $E$. Both of its signatures apply on $O$. Only ordinary sites in $A,A'$ can change there, and at most $2d-(2d-10)=10$ such sites remain. If both parity classes concentrate in a single heavy family, their families must differ: otherwise the other four colours together occupy at most sixteen sites globally, although the total outside one family is at least $2d>16$. With different concentrated families, each parity has at most four sites in the other parity's exceptional family. Hence together they have at most eight changeable ordinary sites. In all cases at most **ten of the $4d$ ordinary sites** can change their pair label when negated. ## A nontrivial rotation must change at least $d$ labels

Let $F$ be the tuple of four signed triangle determinants, so $\sum F_i=0$ and $\deg F_i\le2d$. At a site of pair $ij$ this tuple evaluates to a nonzero scalar multiple of $e_i-e_j$. Pair it with each scalar row of the heads evaluated at $-X$. Each resulting polynomial has degree at most $3d$. If fewer than $d$ labels change, each vanishes at more than $3d$ old double sites and hence identically. Evaluating the two zero polynomials at an arbitrary old double site now forces both heads of its original pair to coincide at the antipode. The prescribed simple triangle roots ensure that all these equalities retain their labels: a given pair has no further coincidence outside its own stratum, by intersecting the root sets of its two face determinants.

This would preserve each of the three pair strata in any one triangle under $X\mapsto-X$. The saturated triangle rotation theorem gives $(-1)^d=1$: its determinant has $2d$ distinct nonzero roots and the same marked pairs agree at every negated root. But $d=n/4-1$ is odd. Thus at least $d\ge15$ labels must change, contradicting the upper bound of ten.
