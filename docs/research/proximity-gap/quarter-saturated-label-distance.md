# How many old pair labels must change when lifting a four-head template?

At length $2^{30}$ and rate one quarter, a saturated quartet exceeding $2n$ bad labels at $n/2$ errors must change at least **134,217,729** of the **805,306,368** labelled double sites obtained by lifting any sixteen-site signed face template of degree six. This applies to all 1,344 balanced templates identified at the designated primes. It also applies to the degree-six face determinants of the known asymmetric cubic construction. An old label counts as preserved whenever its two heads still coincide at that site, even if the new received value chooses another head there. Let $F=(F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3)$ be polynomials of degree at most $E$ summing to zero. On the marked sites the specialized vector is a nonzero multiple of $e_i-e_j$, specifying the old pair $ij$. Let $W_i=(a_i,b_i)$ be proposed heads of degree at most $D$. Assume $F_0\ne0$. Suppose one target face, the one omitting head zero, vanishes at more than $E$ distinct sites, and at each of those sites at least one other target face is nonzero. These two properties follow from saturation whenever $E<2D$. [Saturation and simple face roots](quarter-saturated-symmetry.md)

**Agreement bound.** The proposed heads preserve at most $D+E$ old pair labels. Subtract the fourth head from all heads and write $a,b$ for the resulting rows with the zero fourth coordinate suppressed, and $f=(F_0,F_1,F_2)$. The two polynomials $a\cdot f$ and $b\cdot f$ vanish at every preserved site and have degree at most $D+E$. Exceeding this count forces both to vanish identically. At a root of the target face omitting zero, the target matrix has rank two and the zeroth coordinate of its cross product is zero. Every vector annihilated by both rows there must therefore have zeroth coordinate zero. This forces $F_0$ to vanish at more than $E$ sites, a contradiction. The argument works over any field and the two sets of sites may intersect.

For a power lift let the base degree be $d_0$, with $4d_0$ old double sites and simple signed faces of degree $2d_0$. Take $r\ge2$ and new degree $D=r(d_0+1)-1$. Compose the comparison faces with $X^r$; now $E=2rd_0<2D$. When each old site has $r$ preimages this gives $4rd_0$ prescribed labels, of which at least
\[
  4rd_0-(D+E)=r(d_0-1)+1
\]
must change. At $d_0=3$ and $r=2^{26}$ the upper bound on preserved labels is $671,088,639$. This separation concerns saturated quartets. It does not decide whether a sufficiently different configuration exists on the production domain.

**At the minimum distance.** If $S$ is the set of preserved sites, write $L_S=\prod_{x\in S}(X-x)$. Both row pairings factor through $L_S$ with residual polynomials of degree at most $D+E-|S|$ whenever $|S|\le D+E$. At equality the two residuals are constants and at least one is nonzero by the preceding rank argument. A fixed, nonzero linear combination of the two target rows therefore pairs identically to zero with $f$. At every changed old site the difference of the formerly matched heads has one fixed projective direction, given by the two constants. In a two-sheet lift from sixteen sites, equality requires nineteen preserved and five changed old labels. This reduces a search at that boundary to a scalar projection satisfying all twenty-four original constraints.
