# An elliptic quotient test for the unlabelled quarter-rate fibers

Let $p$ be the first designated prime, $n=2^{30}$, and $D=n/4-2=268435454$. A pencil at 447,392,428 errors has three disjoint complete fibers on the designated subgroup. Their union $S$ has $3D$ sites. The monic locator $L_S(X)=\prod_{s\in S}(X-s)$ gives a test before assigning any of its roots to the three fibers. The smooth projective curve $C_S$ with equation $y^3=L_S(x)$ must have **two copies of $T^2+p$ in its Frobenius characteristic polynomial**. At the scaled case $n=16$, $D=2$ in $\mathbb F_{17}$, the test excludes exactly the 2,112 nonsplitting candidates among all 8,008 unions. Its effectiveness on the production sets is open. ## Minimum degree of an equivariant elliptic quotient

The following argument works in characteristic $p>3$ with $p\equiv2\pmod 3$ and $D<p$. Write $E$ for the smooth genus-one curve $v^3=u(u-1)$ with its unique point above infinity as origin. Over the algebraic closure let $\delta$ multiply the cubic coordinate by a fixed primitive cube root $\zeta$, on both $E$ and $C_S$. On $E$ it has three fixed points, above $0,1,\infty$. On $C_S$ it has the $3D$ branch points above $S$ and no others. The points at infinity of $C_S$ are unramified since $\deg L_S=3D$. Riemann–Hurwitz gives $g(C_S)=3D-2$.

Suppose that coprime $P,Q$ of degree $D$ give the three fibers and $P Q(P-Q)=c L_S$. Cubing is bijective on $\mathbb F_p$, so choose $b\in\mathbb F_p$ with $b^3=c$. The equations
\[
                 u=P/Q,\qquad v=b y/Q
\]
give an $\mathbb F_p$-morphism $f:C_S\to E$ commuting with $\delta$ over the algebraic closure. The function-field map extends over the smooth projective curves. Its degree is $D$ by taking degrees in the square with the two cubic covers of the projective line. [Checked affine identity](../../../ArkLib/Research/ProximityGap/Frontier/QuarterSplitTrigonal.lean)

Conversely, such a nonconstant equivariant morphism of degree $h$ sends all $3D$ fixed points into three fibers of size at most $h$, so $h\ge D$. At equality the fibers over all three fixed targets each consist of $D$ distinct fixed source points with multiplicity one. Passing to the quotients by $\delta$ gives a degree-$D$ map $R$ of projective lines. It descends over $\mathbb F_p$ because $u\circ f$ belongs to $\mathbb F_p(C_S)$ and is invariant under $\delta$, hence belongs to $\mathbb F_p(x)$. Its fibers at $0,1,\infty$ are exactly the three sets of $D$ marked coordinates. In particular none contains the source point at infinity. Writing $R=P/Q$ yields the required split pencil. Thus $S$ supports a pencil precisely when the minimum degree is $D$, with the minimum taken over the nonconstant equivariant maps defined over the ground field.

The quotient at equality is optimal even over the algebraic closure. Translate the image of one marked source point to the origin by a fixed point of $\delta$. The induced homomorphism $f_*:J(C_S)\to E$ has smooth kernel since $f_*f^*=[D]$ has invertible differential. It factors through the elliptic curve $E'$ obtained by quotienting by its connected kernel, followed by an isogeny $E'\to E$. All degrees here divide $D<p$, so the isogeny and the original curve map are separable. The connected kernel is stable under $\delta$ and the induced automorphism of $E'$ has order three, since the action downstairs does. Such an origin-preserving automorphism has three geometric fixed points: its endomorphism satisfies $\delta^2+\delta+1=0$, its dual is $\delta^{-1}$, and $(1-\delta)(1-\delta^{-1})=[3]$. The first curve map $C_S\to E'$ is equivariant and therefore already has degree at least $D$. The isogeny must have degree one. In particular $f^*:E\to J(C_S)$ embeds $E$, and its $D$-torsion lies in the connected kernel of $f_*$ by $f_*f^*=[D]$. These statements concern actual morphisms of curves and Jacobians. [Milne, Jacobian Varieties](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1986c.pdf)

## Squared Frobenius factor

Over every odd-degree extension of $\mathbb F_p$, cubing is bijective. The affine equation of $C_S$ has one ordinate above every finite $x$. There is also one rational point at infinity, since the three geometric points above infinity correspond to cube roots of one. Thus $\#C_S(\mathbb F_{p^j})=p^j+1$ for every odd $j$. The same count applies to $E$, with its single geometric point at infinity. Hence the Frobenius polynomial of $E$ is $T^2+p$, and all odd Frobenius power traces on $C_S$ vanish. Write its monic Frobenius characteristic polynomial as $F_C(T)=G(T^2)$, where $\deg G=g$. This follows from the power traces by the Newton identities. Pullback along $f$ injects the rational Tate module of $E$ into that of $J(C_S)$ since $f_* f^*=[D]$. In particular $G(-p)=0$. [Milne, Abelian Varieties](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/av.html)

Here $D$ and $g=3D-2$ are even. The functional equation pairs coefficients of $G$ at equal distances from $m=g/2$. A paired term has the form $b U^{m-j}(U^{2j}+p^{2j})$. Modulo $(U+p)^2$ it equals $2b(-p)^jU^m$, because $(U^j-(-p)^j)^2$ is divisible by $(U+p)^2$. Thus $G$ is congruent to a scalar multiple of $U^m$. Evaluating at $-p\ne0$ shows that scalar vanishes whenever $G(-p)=0$. This proves $(T^2+p)^2\mid F_C(T)$ over the integers, using monic division. The square uses even genus. The geometric degree and Jacobian arguments above, and the application of the Weil functional equation, are written proofs. ## Every six-site union in the order-16 example

Over $\mathbb F_{17}$, each of the $\binom{16}{6}=8008$ unlabelled candidates has genus four. Write $G(U)=U^4+aU^3+bU^2+p^2 aU+p^4$. With $t_i=p^i+1-\#C_S(\mathbb F_{p^i})$, Newton's identities give $a=-t_2/2$, $b=(t_2^2-2t_4)/8$. The obstruction is $G(-p)/p^2=b-2pa+2p^2$. Only the quadratic and quartic extension point counts enter.

There are ten projective equivalence classes after restricting to six-site unions in the multiplicative subgroup. Projective equivalence preserves both tests. A fractional linear change multiplies the binary sextic by a constant and the affine equation by a sixth power of a linear denominator. The constant has a cube root in $\mathbb F_{17}$. To check actual splitting, each of the fifteen partitions into three pairs gives three monic quadratic locators. They admit a pencil exactly when the three pairs of nonleading coefficients are collinear.

Exactly one class fails the Frobenius test, represented by $\{1,2,3,4,5,7\}$ and comprising **2,112** candidates. It has counts $N_2=264$ and $N_4=84612$, giving obstruction $1377$. All nine remaining classes pass. Checking all pair partitions for every one of the **5,896** candidates in these classes finds a split pencil in each. At production length the elliptic-factor test gives a necessary condition, while the minimum-degree criterion retains the full equivalence.
